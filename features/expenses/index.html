The buyer’s guide to expense reimbursement software

Most small employers handle reimbursements with a shoebox, a spreadsheet or a petty cash drawer. That works until someone’s receipt goes missing, a reimbursement is taxed as wages by mistake, or an employee in California asks why they were never paid back for their phone. This guide covers what expense reimbursement software does, the IRS and wage rules behind it, a sample policy and how to choose a tool.

What expense reimbursement software does

Expense reimbursement software collects work costs that employees paid with their own money, keeps the proof, routes each claim to someone who can approve it and pays it back. Larger finance tools, often sold as expense report software, add corporate cards, travel booking and accounting exports. Hourly teams usually need the simple part: a quick way to send a receipt and a reliable way to get paid back.

For a café or a clinic, that means a milk run before open, a box of gloves or a food handler course. The amounts are small, but they matter to someone earning $18 an hour.

IRS accountable plan rules

The tax treatment of a reimbursement depends on how you pay it. According to IRS Publication 15, amounts paid under an accountable plan are not wages and are not subject to income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare or FUTA. To be an accountable plan, your arrangement must require employees to meet all three of these rules:

RuleWhat it meansSafe harbor timing
Business connectionThe expense was paid or incurred while working for you, and the payment is not money that would otherwise have been paid as wages.Advances paid within 30 days of the expense
SubstantiationThe employee accounts for the expense to you, usually with a receipt and what it was for.Within 60 days after the expense
Return of excessAny advance or allowance beyond the substantiated amount comes back to you.Within 120 days after the expense

The IRS says these periods depend on the facts. The timings above are what it generally treats as reasonable; a quarterly statement asking employees to account for or return outstanding amounts within 120 days also works. If an employee does not substantiate a cost or return the excess, that amount is treated as paid under a nonaccountable plan and becomes taxable wages. That is why the receipt photo matters: it is the substantiation. Our guide on how to read a pay stub shows how Emily’s $86.00 stays out of taxable wages.

Federal wage rules: “free and clear”

Federal law does not require you to reimburse every work cost. It does require that wages be paid “free and clear.” Under 29 CFR 531.35, if you require employees to provide tools of the trade, there is a violation when the cost of those tools cuts into the minimum or overtime wages the Fair Labor Standards Act requires.

An example: an employee earns the federal minimum of $7.25 an hour for 30 hours, or $217.50. If you require $40 of tools and don’t pay it back, they keep $177.50, about $5.92 an hour. That is below the minimum wage. At $17 or more an hour the federal floor holds, but state law may still require repayment.

State reimbursement laws

Some states go well beyond the federal rule. Two you will hear about most:

  • California. Labor Code section 2802 requires employers to indemnify employees for all necessary expenditures or losses incurred in direct consequence of their job duties. California courts have applied it to the work use of personal cell phones, holding that the employer owes a reasonable percentage of the bill even on an unlimited plan. Awards for unpaid reimbursements also carry interest from the date the employee paid the cost.
  • Illinois. Section 9.5 of the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act (820 ILCS 115/9.5) requires reimbursement of necessary expenditures that are required for the job and primarily benefit the employer, when the employer authorized or required them. Employers may set written guidelines and limits, but a policy cannot provide for no or only trivial reimbursement. Employees generally submit expenses within 30 days, unless your written policy allows longer.

A handful of other states and some cities have their own reimbursement rules, so check yours before you write a policy. This guide is general information, not legal advice. Tax and reimbursement rules change, so check with the IRS, your state labor department, a tax professional or an employment lawyer for your situation.

A sample expense policy outline

Your expense policy is what makes the plan accountable and what tells employees what they can buy. Keep it short enough that people read it. A one-page outline:

  1. Purpose. We pay back reasonable, necessary costs employees pay while working for us.
  2. Who can approve. Your direct manager approves claims. Ask before any single purchase over [amount].
  3. What we cover. Supplies for your shift, required training and certifications, [share] of a personal phone bill if you use your phone for work, and other costs your manager approves in advance.
  4. What we don’t cover. Personal items, commuting to your regular workplace, fines and anything not approved.
  5. How to claim. Photograph the receipt, pick a category, add a note on what it was for and send it in the Workzenly app within [30] days.
  6. Missing receipts. Tell your manager in writing what you bought, when, where and why.
  7. Advances. Account for any advance within 60 days and return what you didn’t spend within 120 days.
  8. When you get paid. Approved claims are paid with the next paycheck as a reimbursement, separate from wages.

Put the policy in your employee handbook so new hires read it during onboarding.

Paying reimbursements with payroll

You can pay reimbursements by check, bank transfer, petty cash or with the regular paycheck. The paycheck is easiest to track, because the money arrives on a date employees know. The catch: payroll must treat it as a reimbursement. Type $86.00 in as a bonus and it gets taxed.

How to choose expense reimbursement software

Run one real claim through every tool you are considering. Take a recent receipt and follow it from the employee’s phone to the pay stub. Then ask:

  • How fast can an employee send a claim? Over a minute on a phone, and receipts pile up in pockets again.
  • Does the receipt stay with the claim? You need it for substantiation.
  • Can managers approve from a phone? Shift managers are rarely at a desk.
  • How does the money get back? On the paycheck or by separate payment, and kept apart from wages?
  • Is it one more app? A receipt tool separate from the time clock and pay stubs is one more login.

What is expense reimbursement software?

Expense reimbursement software lets employees submit work costs they paid themselves, with a photo of the receipt, and lets a manager approve or send back each claim. Approved claims are then paid back. In Workzenly, employees photograph the receipt, pick a category and send it from their phone, and approved claims are paid with the next paycheck.

Are expense reimbursements taxable income?

Not when they are paid under an accountable plan. The IRS says amounts paid under an accountable plan are not wages and are exempt from income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare and FUTA. The plan must require a business connection, substantiation within a reasonable period and the return of any excess. Reimbursements that fail those rules are treated as wages.

Do employers have to reimburse employee expenses?

Federal law requires it only when an unreimbursed cost the employer requires, such as tools of the trade, would push pay below the minimum wage or required overtime. Some states go further. California Labor Code section 2802 and section 9.5 of the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act require employers to reimburse necessary work expenses.

How do employees submit an expense in Workzenly?

In the Workzenly app on their phone. They photograph the receipt, enter the amount, pick a category, add a short note and tap send. Their manager sees the claim with the receipt photo attached and can approve it from a phone or a laptop.

When do employees get their money back?

With the next paycheck. Approved claims flow into payroll and appear on the pay stub as a reimbursement line, separate from wages. At Northfield Coffee, Emily Carter's $86.00 kitchen supply receipt was approved during the November 1 to 14 pay period and paid on November 20 alongside her hours.

What records should we keep for reimbursements?

Keep the receipt or other proof of what was bought, when, how much and why, plus the approval and the payment. The IRS tells employers to keep records of expense reimbursements, including substantiation.
