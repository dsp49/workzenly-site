The buyer’s guide to HR document management software

Most small employers keep employee paperwork wherever it landed: I-9s in a binder, offer letters in email, certifications in a drawer. That holds up until an inspector asks for every I-9. This guide covers what HR document management software does, what belongs in a personnel file, how long US law says to keep each record, and how to pick a system for a shift-based team.

What HR document management software does

It gives every employee a digital folder and puts each document in the right one. Good systems also collect documents where they are created (the signed offer letter, W-4 and I-9 go straight into the file), make everything searchable, control who can open what, publish the handbook to employees’ phones and keep certifications with their dates in one list.

Standalone document tools do the storage part. Workzenly does it inside the same app your team uses for hiring, onboarding, schedules, time clocks and payroll, so documents arrive in the folder as a side effect of the work rather than as an extra filing step.

What goes in digital personnel files

No single federal law lists what a personnel file must contain. In practice, most employers keep these documents for each person:

  • The signed offer letter (see our job offer letter templates).
  • Form W-4, plus a state form where your state taxes wages. See our W-4 form guide.
  • A record that the employee received the handbook.
  • Pay rate changes, promotions, reviews and warnings.
  • Certifications the job requires, and separation paperwork.

Two kinds of records usually do not belong in the main file: Forms I-9 and medical information. More on both below. For the full list of forms to collect at hire, read our guide to new hire paperwork.

How long to keep employee records

Federal agencies set minimum retention periods for different records. The longest rule that applies wins, and states often add their own. Here is the federal floor most small employers work from:

RecordKeep for at leastRule
Payroll records (hours, pay rates, wages paid)3 yearsFLSA, U.S. Department of Labor
Time cards, wage rate tables and work schedules2 yearsFLSA, U.S. Department of Labor
Form I-93 years after hire or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is laterUSCIS
Personnel and employment records (applications, hiring, promotion, discipline, termination)1 year, or 1 year from the date of an involuntary terminationEEOC
FMLA leave records3 yearsU.S. Department of Labor (29 CFR 825.500)
Employment tax records, including W-4s4 years after filing the fourth-quarter return for the yearIRS

If someone files a discrimination charge, the EEOC requires you to keep the relevant records until the charge and any lawsuit are fully resolved, however long that takes. Many employers simply keep everything for at least four years after a person leaves, which covers each period in the table. Our I-9 form guide walks through the retention math for a real hire and termination date.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Retention rules change and states can require longer periods, so check your state labor department or an employment lawyer for your situation.

Keep I-9s and medical information separate

Forms I-9. USCIS lets you keep I-9s on paper or electronically, and if a federal agency gives notice of an inspection you have 3 business days to produce them. Most employers keep all I-9s together, apart from personnel files, so they can hand them over without also handing over reviews and pay letters. For electronic I-9s, USCIS expects the system to keep forms accurate and readable, printable, limited to authorized people and logged for each access or change. Ask any vendor how theirs does that.

Medical information. The Americans with Disabilities Act requires medical information about applicants and employees to be collected on separate forms, kept in separate medical files and treated as confidential. FMLA rules say the same for medical certifications and medical histories. Our FMLA leave guide covers what those certifications contain.

In software, “separate” means separate access, not just a separate tab. Check who can open the I-9 folder and medical documents before you buy.

Certification tracking for shift teams

Many hourly jobs need a card: food handler cards in cafés, CPR and BLS in clinics, forklift training in warehouses. Requirements come from state and local rules and your own policies, so check what applies where you operate.

In software, each certification should be stored with the person, show the dates from the card and appear in one team-wide list. In Workzenly you add the card to the person’s folder, enter the dates, and it shows up in the certification list. If renewal reminders matter to you, ask about them on the demo call.

Moving paper personnel files to digital

  1. Start with the people list. Send us your employee list as a spreadsheet and we import it during setup, so every person has a folder from day one.
  2. Pull I-9s first. Scan I-9s for current staff and recent leavers into a folder of their own.
  3. Scan active employees next. Each document goes into that person’s folder.
  4. Publish the handbook. Put your current policies in the app. If you need a starting point, our employee handbook templates cover the common policies.
  5. Keep the paper until it expires. Hold originals until each retention period ends, and check your state’s rules on electronic copies.

How to choose HR document management software

Run one real new hire through every system you are considering. Send an offer letter, have the new hire sign it and complete the W-4 and I-9 Section 1 on their phone, then complete Section 2 as the manager. Check where each document ends up, how fast search finds it and who else can see it. Then check whether the employee can find the sick day policy on their phone in under a minute.

Questions to ask each vendor:

  • Do documents from hiring and onboarding file themselves, or does someone download and upload them?
  • Can I keep I-9s and medical documents apart from personnel files, with separate access?
  • Can employees see their own documents and search the handbook in the app?
  • How are certifications and their dates stored, and how do we see them across the team?

What is HR document management software?

HR document management software stores employee files such as offer letters, tax forms, I-9s, certifications and the employee handbook in one place, filed under each person and searchable. Access is limited to the people who should see each file. Workzenly keeps these documents in the same app your team uses for schedules, time clocks, PTO and payroll.

How long do employers have to keep employee records?

It depends on the record. Under federal rules, keep payroll records for 3 years and time cards for 2 years (FLSA), each Form I-9 for 3 years after hire or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later, personnel records for 1 year (EEOC), FMLA records for 3 years and employment tax records for at least 4 years (IRS). States can require longer, so check yours.

Should I-9 forms go in the employee’s personnel file?

Most employers keep Forms I-9 together in their own file, apart from personnel files. That way you can hand every I-9 to an inspector within the 3 business days allowed without exposing reviews, pay letters or other records. Medical information is different: the ADA and FMLA rules require it to be kept confidential in separate medical files.

Can employees see their own documents and the handbook?

Yes. In the Workzenly app each employee sees only their own record, and anyone can search the employee handbook to find a policy without asking a manager where it is. Managers and payroll see what their permissions allow, and pay letters stay with the people you give payroll access to.

Can Workzenly track certifications like food handler cards or CPR?

Yes. Each certification is stored with the person it belongs to, along with the issue and expiry dates on the card, and all of them appear in one searchable list. Checking who renews next takes a minute instead of a trip to the filing cabinet. Ask us on the demo call how your team would handle renewal reminders.
