The buyer’s guide to hiring software for small business

Most small employers hire with a sign in the window, an inbox full of résumés and a Word template for the offer. That works for one opening a year, not one a month. This guide covers what hiring software does, the steps of a small business hire, the legal basics to get right and how to pick a tool.

What hiring software does for a small business

Hiring software gives each open role a home. Applications for that role arrive in one list. You move people through stages, such as applied, interview, offer and hired, so you can see at a glance who needs a reply. Interviews, notes and the offer letter all live on the applicant’s record.

The part small businesses often miss is what happens next. A café or a clinic does not have separate recruiting, HR and payroll departments. When hiring sits in the same app as employee onboarding, scheduling and payroll, the name, pay rate and start date you typed into the offer are the same ones on the schedule and the first pay stub.

Legal basics for small business hiring

Federal anti-discrimination law applies to hiring, not just to employees already on the payroll. According to the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), the laws it enforces cover most businesses with 15 or more employees, and 20 or more for age discrimination. The Equal Pay Act covers virtually all employers, and state or local laws often reach smaller businesses too. A few rules every hiring manager should know:

  • Ask about the job, not the person’s traits. The EEOC says information about race, sex, national origin, age and religion is irrelevant to whether someone is qualified. Protected traits under federal law also include color, disability, genetic information, pregnancy, sexual orientation and gender identity.
  • No disability questions before the offer. The EEOC says employers are explicitly prohibited from making pre-offer inquiries about disability. You can describe the job and ask whether the person can do it.
  • Keep the records. The EEOC says covered employers must keep all personnel or employment records, including applications, for one year. If a charge of discrimination is filed, keep everything related to it until the matter is over.
  • Criminal history and background checks. Many states and cities have “ban the box” or fair-chance rules that delay criminal history questions until after a conditional offer. If you use a background screening company, the Fair Credit Reporting Act applies: the Federal Trade Commission says you must tell the applicant in a stand-alone document that you may get a report and get their written permission, then send a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report before deciding not to hire based on it. Ask us on the demo call how a background check step fits around your hiring board.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so check your state labor department or an employment lawyer for your situation.

Pay ranges and salary history

A growing list of states require a pay range in job postings, and some also require you to give the range to applicants on request or to current employees. As of late 2026, states with posting or disclosure laws include California, Colorado, Washington, New York and Illinois, along with others such as Hawaii, Maryland, Minnesota, New Jersey, Vermont and Massachusetts, plus Washington, D.C. Some cities have their own rules, and there is no federal pay transparency law. Check your state labor department before you post.

Many states and cities also bar employers from asking about pay history or using it to set pay. One habit covers both rules: put a range on every role and make the offer inside it. Here, Northfield Coffee posts its barista role at $17.00 to $18.50 an hour and offers $17.75.

From offer letter to first day

An offer letter should state the job title, start date, pay rate and pay frequency, exempt or non-exempt status, the expected schedule, any conditions and an at-will statement, plus a deadline to accept. Our job offer letter templates have filled-in versions for hourly, salaried, part-time and seasonal hires.

Electronic signatures are fine for offer letters: the federal ESIGN Act says a signature or contract may not be denied legal effect only because it is electronic. Once the letter is signed, Form I-9 sets the clock. The new hire completes Section 1 no later than the first day of work, and you review their documents within three business days of the start date. Our employee onboarding guide covers the first 90 days.

Hiring software vs applicant tracking software

Applicant tracking software, often called an ATS, is built only for recruiting. Larger products add recruiter workflows for teams that hire dozens of people a month, while recruiting software for small business tends to be lighter.

Hiring tools inside a workforce app cover the basic steps: post a role, review applications, book interviews and send the offer. Then they hand the hire to onboarding, the schedule and payroll without an export. If you hire a few people a month and run hourly shifts, that hand-off usually matters more than recruiter features. If you hire at high volume, compare dedicated tools in our list of the best applicant tracking systems for small business.

How to choose hiring software

  1. Run a real opening through it. Recreate your last hire, from the role to the offer.
  2. Check the phone experience. Most hourly applicants apply and sign on a phone. Sign a test offer yourself on yours.
  3. Look at the offer letter. Can you start from your own wording, and does it include pay, status, schedule and at-will terms?
  4. Follow the hire past “yes.” See whether the new hire’s pay rate and start date reach onboarding, the schedule and payroll without retyping.
  5. Ask what it does not do. If you need background checks or job board posting, find out how you would handle them alongside the tool.

How is it different from an applicant tracking system?

What questions should I avoid in an interview?

Ask about the job, the schedule and the skills it needs. The EEOC says information about race, sex, national origin, age and religion is irrelevant to whether someone is qualified, and federal law bars questions about disability before a job offer. Many states and cities also limit questions about salary history and criminal history.

Does Workzenly run background checks?

No. Workzenly's hiring tools cover posting a role, applications, interviews and the offer letter. If you use a background screening company, the Fair Credit Reporting Act applies: you need the applicant's written permission on a separate disclosure before ordering a report, and a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report before you decide not to hire based on it.

What happens after a candidate signs the offer letter?

How long should I keep job applications?

The EEOC says employers covered by federal anti-discrimination laws must keep all personnel or employment records for one year, and applications are part of those records. If a discrimination charge is filed, keep everything related to it until the matter is resolved. Some states require longer, so check your state's rules.
