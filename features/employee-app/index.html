The buyer’s guide to an employee self service app

Most small employers already have self service of a kind: a schedule photo in the group chat and a manager who answers the same five questions every day. This guide covers what an employee self service app does, who changes what, the privacy and legal points to check, and how to roll one out to hourly staff.

What an employee self service app does

Employee self service (ESS) means employees look up and update their own work information instead of asking someone in charge. An ESS app puts that on the phone. For an hourly team the core jobs are:

  • Time. Clocking in and out, breaks, and seeing hours this week. Workzenly’s employee time clock app handles this, with GPS and geofencing.
  • Schedule. Seeing the week as soon as it is published, picking up open shifts and swapping with a coworker.
  • Time off. Requesting PTO and seeing the balance. Workzenly’s PTO tracking keeps balances accruing on their own.
  • Pay. Reading pay stubs on payday and following the hours, PTO and expenses behind them.
  • Paperwork. Signing the offer letter, filling out the W-4 and I-9, adding direct deposit, and finding the handbook later.

Older employee self service portals were web pages built for office staff with a work email and a laptop. A shift worker needs the same functions on a phone, quick enough to use on a break. That is the difference between a portal people log into once a year and an employee app they open every shift.

ESS app vs manager portal

Every workforce system has two sides. The employee side asks and looks things up. The manager side approves, plans and pays. Both should read from the same record, or you end up reconciling two versions of the truth.

What employees change themselves vs what managers approve

The point of self service is fewer hand-offs, but anything that changes pay or coverage still needs a second pair of eyes. A sensible split for an hourly business looks like this:

Write the split into your employee handbook so nobody has to guess, especially for swaps. For punch fixes, the correction should keep the original punch next to it, with who made it and why.

Privacy and access

An employee app concentrates sensitive data: pay, bank details, tax forms, identity documents. Three rules keep it under control.

  1. Each employee sees only their own record. No shared logins, no coworker’s pay stub one tap away. In Workzenly every person has their own account.
  2. Managers see their team, not everything. A shift lead needs the schedule and time off for their people. They do not need anyone’s pay rate.
  3. Pay is limited to payroll access. Decide who that is by name, and review the list when someone changes role or leaves.

If you turn on GPS for clocking in, tell employees what is recorded and when. Workzenly records location at the clock-in, inside a geofence you draw; our guide to GPS time clock apps covers the notice and consent questions.

Rules to check before you go paperless

  • Pay stubs. Federal law does not require pay stubs, but many states do, and each sets its own rules on electronic stubs. Some allow them only if the employee can view and print them or agrees to it. Our guide on how to read a pay stub explains what a stub shows and lists the common state rules.
  • Time spent in the app. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, work that is “suffered or permitted” is work time (29 CFR 785.11). If you require non-exempt staff to finish training, read policies or fill out forms in the app, pay for that time.
  • Personal phones. California Labor Code section 2802 requires employers to reimburse necessary work expenses, and a 2014 California appeals court decision applied it to a reasonable share of personal cell phone bills. Check your state before you make a personal phone mandatory.
  • Electronic W-4 and I-9. The IRS allows employers to collect the Form W-4 electronically if the system meets its requirements. USCIS allows electronic Form I-9 storage with integrity controls, an audit trail and an index; Section 1 is due no later than the first day of work for pay, and you still review the employee’s documents yourself.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so check your state labor department or an employment lawyer for your situation.

Rolling it out to hourly staff

Hourly teams adopt an app when the first thing they do in it is useful. Start with clocking in and the schedule, which everyone needs every shift, then add pay stubs and requests.

  1. Import everyone first. Send your employee list and current PTO balances as a spreadsheet; Workzenly imports them during setup, so each person opens the app to their own balance instead of a zero.
  2. Plan for people without a phone at work. Where phones stay in lockers, or someone does not want a work app on a personal device, set up a shared tablet at the door as a time clock kiosk. They can check the rest from home.
  3. Do sign-up on the clock. Walk each shift through installing the app during a paid huddle.
  4. Pick a clean start date. Start at the beginning of a workweek and a pay period, so the first pay stub in the app covers hours that were all clocked in the app.

New hires are the easiest group. If they sign their offer letter and finish their forms in the app, as described in our employee onboarding guide, it is already the place they go for work.

How to choose an employee app

Hand a phone to your least technical employee and ask them to clock in, find next Saturday’s shift, request a day off and open last week’s pay stub. Then check that the request reaches the manager with the balance attached. Ask each vendor:

  • Does the employee app use the same record as scheduling, time off and payroll, or does it sync?
  • What does an employee see, what does a manager see, and who can see pay?
  • What happens for workers without a smartphone at work?
  • Can new hires finish onboarding forms in the app before day one?

FAQ

Questions about an employee self service app

What is an employee self service app?

An employee self service app lets workers handle their own everyday HR tasks from a phone: clocking in, checking their schedule, requesting time off, viewing pay stubs and finding company policies. Instead of asking a manager or the office, they look it up or send a request. In Workzenly the same app also covers shift swaps, expenses and onboarding forms.

Can employees see their coworkers’ pay or records?

No. In Workzenly each employee sees only their own record: their hours, shifts, PTO balance, pay stubs and documents. Managers see their team’s shifts and time off, and pay is visible only to the people you give payroll access to.

Do employees need their own smartphone?

Most will use their own phone, because the employee side is built for phones first. Where phones stay in lockers or someone does not want work apps on a personal device, a shared tablet at the door works as a time clock instead. If you require personal phones, check your state’s rules on reimbursing work use, such as California’s.

What can employees do themselves and what needs a manager?

Employees clock in and out, see their schedule, start a shift swap or pick up an open shift, read their pay stubs, search policies and fill out their own onboarding forms. Requests that affect pay or coverage, such as PTO, expense claims and missed-punch fixes, go to a manager to approve, and managers approve timesheets before they reach payroll.

Are pay stubs in an app legal?

Federal law does not require pay stubs at all, but many states do, and states set their own rules on electronic stubs. Some allow them only if employees can view and print them, or agree to receive them that way. Check your state labor department before you stop handing out paper.

How long does it take to roll out?

Most small teams are clocking in within a week. We import your employee list and PTO balances from a spreadsheet during setup, each person gets their own account, and we stay with you through the first pay run so the first pay stubs land in the app on payday.
