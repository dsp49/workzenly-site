The buyer’s guide to employee onboarding software

Most small employers onboard with a folder of printed forms. That works until two people start the same week or an I-9 deadline slips. This guide covers what onboarding software should handle, the paperwork US law requires, the rules for electronic I-9s and W-4s, and a simple first-week plan.

What employee onboarding software does

New hire onboarding software covers the stretch from a signed offer to a person working on their own. In practice that means five jobs: get the offer letter signed, collect tax and work-authorization forms, capture direct deposit details, get policies acknowledged, and track the tasks that fall to the manager and payroll. The best tools keep every document in the employee’s record and send pay details to payroll, so nothing is typed twice.

For hourly teams, what matters most is where forms get filled out. If they do not work on a phone, new hires finish them on their first shift, on the clock. For the wider process, from the welcome message to the 90-day check-in, see our guide to employee onboarding.

Electronic I-9 rules (8 CFR 274a.2)

Federal regulations at 8 CFR 274a.2 let employers complete, sign and store Form I-9 electronically, but only in a system that meets set standards. The system must protect record integrity, keep an audit trail, index forms so any one can be found and printed legibly, and capture an electronic signature showing the signer read the attestation. When the employee asks, it must give them a printed confirmation of the transaction. USCIS notes that its own fillable PDF does not meet these standards, so it must be printed and signed by hand.

Two rules apply whatever software you use:

  • Section 2 needs a real document review. You or an authorized representative must examine the original documents the employee chooses, with the employee there. An uploaded photo is not a review.
  • Remote review is only for E-Verify employers. Employers enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing at the hiring site can use the DHS alternative procedure: review copies of the documents, see the same documents with the employee on a live video call, keep the copies and check the box in Section 2.

Keep each I-9 for 3 years after the hire date or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later, ideally apart from personnel files. Our I-9 form guide walks through each section and the lists of acceptable documents. When you compare vendors, ask each one, including us, to explain in writing how its electronic I-9 meets 8 CFR 274a.2.

Electronic W-4 rules from the IRS

The IRS allows employers to receive Form W-4 electronically. According to IRS Publication 15-T, the system must:

  • Make sure what you receive is what the employee sent, and record each access that results in a submission
  • Make it reasonably certain that the person submitting the form is the employee named on it
  • Show exactly the same information as the paper form
  • Have the employee sign with an e-signature as the final entry, under penalties of perjury in the same words as the paper form
  • Produce a hard copy of any completed form if the IRS asks for one

The usual paper recordkeeping rules still apply, so keep W-4s with your employment tax records for at least 4 years. If a new hire never turns one in, the IRS says to withhold as if they were single with no other adjustments. Our W-4 form guide covers the 2026 form line by line.

This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules change, so check USCIS, the IRS, your state labor department or an employment lawyer for your situation.

A first-week onboarding plan

Here is how Northfield Coffee onboards Nina Park, a front counter hire who signs her offer on Tuesday, November 10 and starts Monday, November 16:

  1. Offer signed (Tue, Nov 10). Nina gets her checklist on her phone and finishes I-9 Section 1 that night.
  2. By Friday (Nov 13). She adds her W-4, direct deposit and handbook acknowledgment, and sees her first shifts on the schedule.
  3. Day one (Mon, Nov 16). Marcus examines Nina’s documents and completes Section 2, ahead of the Thursday, November 19 deadline. She clocks in for the first time.
  4. The first week. Training shifts, a short Friday check-in, and the new hire report filed by your state’s deadline.
  5. First payday (Fri, Dec 4). Her first pay stub, for the Nov 15–28 period, appears in the app.

How to choose onboarding software

Start with how you hire. A team hiring hourly staff a few times a month needs phone-friendly forms and a clean hand-off to payroll more than a training library. Run one real hire through each tool you are considering: send an offer, have someone fill out every form on their phone, then check where the W-4 and direct deposit end up and how many steps it takes to get to a correct first paycheck. Ask about the I-9 standards above, state forms for every state you hire in, who can see bank details, and whether you can export every document if you leave.

What is employee onboarding software?

Employee onboarding software collects a new hire’s paperwork in one place: the signed offer letter, tax and work-authorization forms, direct deposit and the handbook acknowledgment. In Workzenly, new hires do all of it on their phone before day one, and the documents land in their employee record.

Can a new hire fill out the I-9 on their phone?

The new hire can complete Section 1 of Form I-9 on their phone any time after accepting the job offer and no later than their first day of work for pay. Section 2 is the employer’s part: you examine the documents the employee chooses to show and complete it within 3 business days of that first day.

Can I check I-9 documents remotely?

Only if you are enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing at that hiring site. Those employers can use the DHS alternative procedure: review copies of the documents, see the same documents with the employee on a live video call, keep the copies and check the box in Section 2. Everyone else must examine original documents with the employee present.

Is an electronic W-4 legal?

Yes. The IRS allows employers to receive Form W-4 electronically if the system shows the same information as the paper form, makes it reasonably certain the person submitting it is the employee, has the employee sign under penalties of perjury with the e-signature as the final entry, and can produce a hard copy if the IRS asks. The usual recordkeeping rules still apply.

What happens to the forms after the new hire signs?

They are stored with the person in Workzenly, next to their offer letter, certifications and handbook acknowledgment, and are searchable. Payroll uses the W-4 and direct deposit details as entered, with no retyping.
