The buyer’s guide to PTO tracking software

A wall calendar and a spreadsheet work until two people want the same Friday or someone leaves and asks to be paid for unused vacation. This guide covers what PTO tracking software does, how accrual works, the federal and state rules your balances have to follow, and how to choose a system for a team that works in shifts.

What PTO tracking software does

PTO tracking software, also sold as time off tracking software or leave management software, handles four jobs: it takes requests, routes them to the right manager, keeps each person’s balance current, and tells payroll what to pay.

A simple vacation tracker covers the first two jobs and leaves balances and payroll to you. For hourly teams, where one absence means a shift to fill, the value is in the last two jobs. When the time clock, schedule and payroll share one record, as they do in Workzenly, nobody retypes a day off.

PTO accrual methods, with the math

Accrual is how fast employees earn PTO. All of these examples use 80 hours a year (two weeks) and a full-time year of 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). Our paid time off guide has an accrual calculator.

Per-hour accrual is the fairest method for hourly and part-time staff, and it is how most state sick leave laws work. It is also the first to break in a spreadsheet. In a PTO tracker connected to timesheets, the math happens on its own.

What federal law requires

Less than most people think. The U.S. Department of Labor says the Fair Labor Standards Act “does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations, sick leave or federal or other holidays.” Three federal points still shape how you track PTO:

  • PTO is not hours worked. Under 29 CFR 778.218, pay for vacation, holiday and sick hours does not count toward overtime. An employee who works 40 hours and takes 8 hours of PTO that week gets 48 hours of pay and no overtime premium.
  • Records. Payroll records, which include PTO paid, must be kept for at least 3 years under the FLSA.
  • Exempt employees. You can take a partial day from an exempt employee’s PTO balance, but you generally cannot cut their salary for a partial-day absence.

Holidays are usually tracked apart from PTO. See our holiday pay guide.

State payout and use-it-or-lose-it rules

No federal rule covers paying out unused PTO. In most states, your written policy decides. A few states treat earned vacation as wages, and that changes what your balances have to do.

  • California. Under Labor Code 227.3, earned and unused vacation must be paid at the final rate of pay when employment ends. The Labor Commissioner says vacation vests as it is earned, so a use-it-or-lose-it policy is not allowed. A reasonable cap that stops further accrual is allowed. A PTO plan that combines vacation and sick time follows the same rules as vacation.
  • Colorado, Montana and Nebraska also bar forfeiting earned vacation and require it to be paid at separation, according to their labor agencies and courts. Illinois, Massachusetts and several other states have their own payout rules.
  • Everywhere else, say clearly in the handbook whether unused PTO is paid out and whether it carries over. Our employee handbook template has a section for it.

The cost adds up fast. An employee paid $19.00 an hour who leaves with 44 hours of PTO is owed 44 × $19.00 = $836.00 in a payout state. A cap is a safer tool than an expiry date if you have staff in California.

Paid sick leave and FMLA

As of November 2026, 20 states and Washington, D.C. require most private employers to provide paid sick leave, and some cities add their own. The most common rate is 1 hour for every 30 hours worked.

  • California requires at least 1 hour per 30 hours worked. Employees must be allowed to use at least 40 hours or 5 days a year, and employers may cap the balance at 80 hours or 10 days.
  • New York requires up to 40 hours a year at employers with 5 to 99 employees and up to 56 hours at 100 or more.
  • A combined PTO bank can satisfy a sick leave law if it meets every requirement, including accrual rate, carryover and same-day use for illness. If not, track sick leave as its own balance.

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act covers private employers with 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks. Eligible employees get up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period. Under 29 CFR 825.207, an employee may choose to use accrued PTO during FMLA leave, and if they do not, the employer may require it. Either way the paid time runs concurrently, so it counts against the 12 weeks and comes off the PTO balance. Our FMLA leave guide covers eligibility and notices, and the bereavement leave guide covers a type of leave FMLA does not.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Leave laws change often, so check your state labor department or an employment lawyer for your situation.

Features that matter in a PTO tracker

  1. A request flow people will use. Two taps from a phone, with the balance shown before the request goes in.
  2. A team time-off calendar. Managers see who else is out that day before they approve.
  3. The schedule next to the request. An approved day off should show on the week and open the shift for cover.
  4. Accrual that matches your policy. Per hour worked, per pay period or front-loaded, with caps and carryover.
  5. Separate balances where the law needs them. Sick leave apart from vacation if your state requires it.
  6. A payroll hand-off. Approved PTO in the pay run without a CSV or an email.
  7. A history for every balance. Each accrual and day used, dated, for questions and final paychecks.
  8. Permissions. Employees see their own balance, managers their team, payroll the pay.

FAQ

Questions about PTO tracking software

What is PTO tracking software?

PTO tracking software records time off requests, routes them to a manager for approval, keeps each employee’s balance current as PTO accrues and is used, and sends approved hours to payroll. Workzenly also shows managers who else is out before they approve and gives every employee their balance in the app.

How do employees request time off?

From the Workzenly app on their phone, in two taps. They pick the dates, see their PTO balance before and after the request, add a note if they want, and send it. They get the answer in the same app, so nobody has to chase a manager by text.

Do PTO balances update on their own?

Yes. Balances accrue and update themselves, and approved hours come off the balance as soon as the request is approved. Employees see the same number their manager and payroll see, which ends most of the “how much PTO do I have left” questions.

Do we have to pay out unused PTO when someone leaves?

It depends on the state and your written policy. California treats earned vacation as wages and requires it to be paid at the final rate of pay when employment ends, and it does not allow use-it-or-lose-it policies. Many other states follow the employer’s policy. An accurate balance on the last day makes either rule easy to follow.

Can we bring PTO balances over from a spreadsheet?

Yes. Send us your employee list and current PTO balances as a spreadsheet and we import them during setup, so nobody starts from zero. It is worth checking each balance with the employee first, because spreadsheet errors tend to carry over.

Does PTO count toward overtime?

Not under federal law. Overtime is based on hours actually worked, so PTO, holiday and sick hours do not push someone over 40 hours in a workweek. An employer can choose to count them by policy, because that pays more than the law requires.
