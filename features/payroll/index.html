The buyer’s guide to payroll software for small business

This guide covers what small business payroll software does, the federal duties every US employer has, how to build a payroll calendar, and how to choose between a payroll service, payroll software and a workforce app with payroll built in.

What small business payroll software does

Every pay run follows the same steps: collect hours, work out gross pay, take out deductions and taxes, pay the net amount and give each employee a stub. After payday come tax deposits, returns and year-end forms. Our guide to how to process payroll walks through each step by hand.

Products differ most at the first step, getting the hours in, and the last, who deposits taxes and files returns. For an hourly team, getting the hours in is where most errors start: a missed clock-out, overtime counted across two weeks instead of one, a PTO day nobody told payroll about. Workzenly is built around that step. Hours, overtime, PTO and expense claims are approved where they happen and arrive in payroll already approved.

Whatever you use, the stub your employees get should show hours, rates, each deduction and net pay. Federal law does not require pay stubs, but many states do. If you are not ready for software, our pay stub templates show what a complete one includes.

Employer payroll duties and deadlines

These are the federal duties the IRS lists for most employers that file Form 941. States add their own income tax withholding, unemployment insurance and, in some places, paid leave or disability programs.

When a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so 2026 Forms W-2 and the fourth-quarter Form 941 are due Monday, February 1, 2027. Form 941 also gets 10 extra days when every deposit for the quarter was made on time. The Fair Labor Standards Act also requires payroll records for 3 years.

This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Figures are from IRS guidance as of November 2026 and change from year to year. Check IRS Publication 15, your state agencies or an accountant for your situation.

Federal tax deposit schedules

Withheld taxes are sent to the IRS on one of two schedules. The names do not describe how often you pay people.

  • How your schedule is set. The IRS looks at the taxes you reported during a lookback period: the 12 months starting July 1 of the second preceding year. For 2026 that is July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025. $50,000 or less makes you a monthly depositor; more than $50,000 makes you semiweekly.
  • Monthly. Taxes on wages paid during a month are due by the 15th of the following month.
  • Semiweekly. Taxes on Wednesday, Thursday or Friday paydays are due the following Wednesday. Taxes on Saturday through Tuesday paydays are due the following Friday.
  • $100,000 next-day rule. If you pile up $100,000 or more in taxes on any day, deposit by the next business day, whatever your schedule.
  • Under $2,500 a quarter. If your liability for the current or preceding quarter is under $2,500, you may be able to pay with a timely filed return instead of depositing.

For Northfield Coffee’s Friday, November 20 payday, a monthly depositor would deposit by December 15, and a semiweekly depositor by Wednesday, November 25. Whether software makes those deposits for you is the biggest difference between products. On a Workzenly demo, ask us who handles deposits and filings for your account.

Build a payroll calendar

A payroll calendar fixes three dates for every period: when hours must be approved, when the run is reviewed and when people get paid. Here is Northfield Coffee’s biweekly calendar for the end of 2026.

January 1 is a federal holiday, so that payday needs a decision now. Paying on Thursday, December 31 puts those wages on 2026 forms, because payroll years follow the pay date, not the work date. Paying on Monday, January 4 keeps them in 2027 but means everyone is paid late. Many states also set how often you must pay, so check your state labor department before you change frequency.

Questions to ask on any payroll demo

Use this list on every call, ours included, and ask to see each answer on screen.

  1. Where do the hours come from? If the answer is a CSV import, someone still copies hours every pay period.
  2. Is overtime counted per workweek? A 46-hour week then a 32-hour week should give 6 overtime hours, not 0.
  3. Does approved PTO reach payroll on its own? It should come off the balance and appear as PTO without a separate email.
  4. What does the employee see? Ask for the stub on a phone, not only the admin report.
  5. Who handles deposits and filings? Get a written list: federal and state deposits, Forms 941 and 940, W-2s and state returns. Ask us this too.
  6. Who pays contractors? If you pay 1099 workers, ask how. Our 1099 vs W-2 guide explains why classification matters, and exempt vs non-exempt covers who is owed overtime.

Switching payroll providers

You can switch at any time, but the start of a quarter is simplest, because each Form 941 covers three months and one system then reports the whole quarter. Switching mid-year? Keep each employee’s year-to-date totals from the old provider so W-2s are complete. Send us your employee list and PTO balances as a spreadsheet and we import them during setup; most small teams are clocking in within a week, and we stay with you through the first pay run. What does payroll software for small business do?

It turns hours, pay rates, time off and deductions into gross pay, withholds taxes, works out net pay and gives each employee a pay stub. Some products also deposit taxes and file returns. Workzenly builds the pay run from approved hours, overtime, PTO and expense claims, and puts every pay stub in the employee app on payday.

Can Workzenly run our payroll?

Yes. Approved hours, overtime, PTO and expense claims go straight into payroll, and each employee gets their pay stub in the Workzenly app on payday. If you need tax deposits and filings handled for you, ask us on the demo call exactly who handles deposits and filings for your account.

Does overtime get calculated automatically?

Overtime is worked out from the time clock punches, by workweek, so hours over 40 in a week show up as overtime before you review the pay run. Federal law requires time and a half for non-exempt employees, and PTO hours do not count toward the 40. Some states, such as California, add daily overtime rules.

Who can see pay rates and pay stubs?

You decide. Employees see only their own pay stubs and record. Managers see their team's shifts and time off and can approve hours without seeing pay. Pay rates and payroll totals are visible only to the people you give payroll access to, such as the owner or a bookkeeper.

Can we switch payroll in the middle of the year?

Yes, though the start of a quarter is simplest, because each quarterly Form 941 return covers three months. Keep year-to-date totals from your old provider so W-2s are complete. Send us your employee list and PTO balances as a spreadsheet, and we import them during setup and stay with you for the first pay run.

Can I run payroll from my phone?

Managers can approve timesheets, PTO and expense claims from a phone, which is most of the work before payday. Reviewing and approving the pay run itself is easiest on a laptop, where you can see every employee's hours, overtime and totals side by side.
