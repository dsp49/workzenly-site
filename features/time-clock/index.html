Why move your time clock app off paper

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, the US Department of Labor does not require any particular way of tracking time. A punch clock, a paper sheet or a phone app all work, as long as the records are complete and accurate. What the law does require, for every non-exempt employee, is the day and time the workweek begins, the hours worked each workday and the total hours for each workweek.

Those records have to last. The DOL says employers must keep payroll records for at least three years and time cards, work schedules and wage calculations for at least two. A shoebox of paper cards meets the rule on paper, but it is hard to read, easy to lose and has to be typed into payroll by someone every week.

That copying step is where most errors come from: a 3:31 PM out-time read as 3:01, a forgotten lunch break, a Sunday shift added to the wrong week. A digital clock in clock out app records the exact time once, and every later number (overtime, PTO, the pay stub) is built from that same punch.

Exact times also settle the rounding question. Federal rules allow rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour only when it evens out over time and never shortchanges employees. When your clock captures the real minute, you can simply pay for the real minute.

How to choose a clock in clock out app: a buying checklist

Every employee time clock app looks much the same in a screenshot. These are the questions that separate them once a real team is using one every day.

  • Can every kind of worker clock in? Staff with smartphones, staff without them and staff who are not allowed to carry them at work. Look for both personal phone and shared device options.
  • Does location checking match your sites? A GPS time clock suits field crews and multi-site teams. A geofence suits a fixed store or clinic. Ask how the app handles a site with weak signal, and what employees see when they are outside the boundary.
  • Are exact punch times kept? You want the original punch, any edit, who made it and when. That edit history is your evidence if hours are ever disputed.
  • Does it know your overtime rules? Federal overtime is weekly, after 40 hours. Some states add daily overtime; California, for example, pays daily overtime after 8 hours. Our California overtime law guide shows how much that changes a paycheck.
  • Is it tied to the schedule? Late arrivals and no-shows only make sense against the shift someone was meant to work. A clock that knows the schedule can tell a manager before a shift goes uncovered.
  • Where do the hours go next? If approved timesheets still have to be exported and re-entered into payroll, you have kept half the copying problem.
  • What does it collect, and is it disclosed? Location at clock-in should be explained to employees in writing. If a vendor uses fingerprints or face scans, check biometric privacy laws first; Illinois, for one, requires written consent and a written retention policy before collecting them.

Frequently asked questions

Does federal law require a time clock?

No. The FLSA does not require any particular timekeeping method. A time clock, a timekeeper or employees writing their own hours are all acceptable, as long as the records are complete and accurate. For non-exempt staff you must record hours worked each workday and each workweek, and keep time cards for at least two years.

Can employees clock in from their own phones?

Yes. Each employee has their own Workzenly account and can clock in and out from their phone, with GPS and a geofence confirming they are at the right site. Where phones are not allowed on the floor, you can set up a shared tablet at the door for clocking in instead.

Is a GPS time clock legal?

Recording location when an employee clocks in is common and generally lawful in the US, but state privacy and notice rules vary. Tell employees in writing what the app records and when, put it in your handbook, and check your state's rules before you roll it out, especially if staff use personal phones.

Can I round time clock punches?

Federal rules allow rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour, but only if it evens out and does not underpay employees over time. Some states and courts are stricter. Paying from the actual minutes recorded is the safest choice when your clock captures exact punch times.

What happens to the hours after people clock out?

Punches build each person's timesheet, with breaks, overtime and late arrivals worked out. A manager reviews and approves the week, in one tap if nothing needs fixing, and the approved hours flow into Workzenly payroll. Each employee then sees their pay stub in the same app on payday.
