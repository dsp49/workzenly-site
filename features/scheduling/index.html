Why staff scheduling falls apart in a spreadsheet

Most small businesses build the schedule in a spreadsheet, post a photo of it in a group text, and then manage the week by message. Someone asks to swap Thursday, someone else says yes, and the only record is a thread the manager has to scroll back through on Saturday.

The spreadsheet also cannot see anything else. It does not know that Chris already has Monday approved as PTO, that Sam was 14 minutes late three times last week, or that one more shift will push Jordan past 40 hours and into overtime. So the manager checks three places before every change, or skips the check and finds out on payday.

A shift scheduling app fixes the part the spreadsheet cannot: it keeps the schedule, the requests and the hours people actually worked in the same place, and puts the current version on every employee’s phone.

Predictive scheduling and fair workweek laws

There is no federal rule on how early you must post a schedule. One state and a group of cities have passed predictive scheduling, or fair workweek, laws for larger employers. They usually require advance notice, extra pay for late changes made by the employer, and rest between a closing and an opening shift. Here are the main ones, as published by each agency.

WhereWho is covered (summary)Schedule notice

Los Angeles County added its own retail ordinance for unincorporated areas from July 1, 2025, and a few other cities have similar rules. Thresholds, premiums and exceptions differ in each place, and changes an employee asks for are often treated differently from changes the employer makes. Oregon, for example, exempts changes made at an employee’s written request. Confirm the details with the agency for your city or state before you rely on this summary.

Even if no law covers you, the habits are worth copying. Posting two weeks out makes it easier to keep good staff, and rotating patterns such as the DuPont schedule or the 2-2-3 schedule are easier to post far ahead because they repeat.

What to look for in employee scheduling software

Use this checklist on any tool you trial, including ours.

  • Employees actually open it. The schedule has to be on their phones, current, and easy to read at a glance. If staff still ask “am I on tomorrow?”, the tool is not doing its job.
  • Swaps and open shifts leave a record. You want to see who offered a shift, who took it and when, which matters most where scheduling laws treat employee requests differently.
  • Time off is visible while you build. Approving PTO in one place and scheduling in another is how double-booking happens.
  • Scheduled hours meet worked hours. A schedule tied to the time clock shows late arrivals and no-shows, and helps you spot overtime before the week ends. Our shift differential guide explains why night and weekend shifts also change the cost of a schedule.
  • It fits your pattern. Retail and restaurants change shifts every week; clinics and plants run fixed rotations. Ask the vendor to build one of your real weeks in the demo.
  • The hours go somewhere. If worked hours still have to be exported and keyed into payroll, you have moved the spreadsheet, not removed it.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance do I have to post a work schedule?

Federal law sets no notice period. Oregon and several cities, including Seattle, Chicago, Philadelphia, Los Angeles and New York City for fast food, require covered employers to post schedules 14 days ahead, and New York City retail employers must give 72 hours. If none of these apply to you, posting a week or more ahead is still good practice.

Do fair workweek laws apply to small businesses?

Usually not to a single independent shop. Most of these laws cover larger employers in set industries, often counted by employees or locations worldwide, such as 250 or 500 employees. Franchise locations can be counted toward the totals in some cities, so a small franchisee may be covered. Check the rules with your city or state labor agency.

Can employees swap shifts in Workzenly?

Yes. Staff see the schedule on their phones and can pick up an open shift or swap one with a teammate, and the schedule updates for everyone. Where a scheduling law applies, keep a record that the employee asked for the change; Oregon, for example, exempts changes made at an employee's written request from its notice rules.

Does the schedule show who is on PTO?

Yes. Scheduling and PTO share one record, so approved time off shows up where you build shifts. When someone asks for a day off, the manager sees who else is already out that day before saying yes, and can find cover from the schedule in the same place.

Do I have to pay for a shift I cancel?

Federal law only requires pay for hours worked, so it does not require pay for a canceled shift. Some states, including California and New York, require reporting-time or call-in pay when an employee reports to work and is sent home early, and fair workweek cities add predictability pay for late changes. Check your state and city rules.

Can managers build the schedule from a phone?

Yes. Managers can build schedules, approve requests and check who is in from a phone. Many find it easier to plan a full week for a large team on a laptop, where you can see every day at once, and then handle swaps and last-minute changes from their phone during the week.
