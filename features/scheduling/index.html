The buyer’s guide to employee scheduling software

This guide covers what employee scheduling software does, the fair workweek laws that set notice rules in some places, the federal limits on scheduling minors, how to build a week that holds up, and how to pick a tool for an hourly team.

What employee scheduling software does

Scheduling software is a shared calendar of shifts. A manager assigns people and publishes the week; staff then see it on their phones, trade shifts, claim open ones and ask for time off, and every change updates the one schedule everybody reads.

Better tools also know who is on approved PTO, who is close to 40 hours and who actually clocked in. A schedule that doesn’t talk to the time and attendance record can’t warn you about gaps or overtime.

Workzenly’s schedule, time clock, PTO tracking and payroll share one record. A standalone shift scheduling app can go deeper on scheduling alone; the trade-off is the hand-off between tools.

Predictive scheduling and fair workweek laws

Federal law sets no schedule notice rule. One state and several cities have predictive scheduling, or fair workweek, laws for larger employers in certain industries, usually requiring advance notice, extra pay for late employer changes and rest between shifts. Here are the main ones, as published by each agency.

Thresholds and exceptions differ, and employee-requested changes are often treated differently. Oregon, for example, says changes made at an employee’s written request are not subject to its advance notice rule. A time-stamped swap request in the app is better evidence than a text.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Scheduling rules change and thresholds are updated, so check your city or state labor agency or an employment lawyer for your situation.

Scheduling minors under federal child labor rules

The Fair Labor Standards Act limits when the youngest workers can be scheduled. According to the U.S. Department of Labor, 14- and 15-year-olds may work outside school hours only, and no more than:

  • 3 hours on a school day, including Fridays
  • 18 hours in a school week
  • 8 hours on a non-school day
  • 40 hours in a week when school is not in session

Their shifts must fall between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., except from June 1 through Labor Day, when the evening limit moves to 9 p.m. Federal law does not limit hours for 16- and 17-year-olds, though it still bars them from hazardous jobs, and many states do set hour limits for them. When federal and state rules both apply, the stricter one wins.

Keep a list of who is under 18 and check their shifts against both sets of rules before you publish. Workzenly shows weekly hours per person as you build, but the check itself is yours.

How to build a weekly schedule that holds up

  1. Start from demand. List the shifts each day needs by role.
  2. Block out what you already know. Approved time off, standing days off and any limits for minors go on the grid before a single name does.
  3. Place regulars, then fill. Drag in steady shifts first, then part-timers. Watch weekly totals against 40 hours.
  4. Leave real gaps open. Post unowned shifts as open so people who want hours can claim them.
  5. Publish early. In fair workweek cities, it may be the law.
  6. Close the loop. Compare scheduled and worked hours weekly. Late starts, a no-call, no-show or steady overtime show what to change.

Shift patterns and the cost of a schedule

Teams that run around the clock often use a fixed rotation, which is easy to post far ahead. The 2-2-3 schedule rotates 12-hour shifts over two weeks, and office or field teams sometimes use the 9/80 work schedule to give every other Friday off.

The schedule also sets the labor bill: overtime after 40 hours, and sometimes a premium for nights and weekends. Our guide to shift differential pay explains how that works.

Features that matter in staff scheduling software

  1. Fast building. Drag and drop, color-coded roles and weekly hours per person.
  2. A real employee app. The current schedule on each person’s phone, readable in two seconds.
  3. Swaps and open shifts with a record. Who offered, who accepted, who approved and when.
  4. Time off on the grid. Approving PTO in one place and scheduling in another is how double-booking happens.
  5. Coverage and overtime warnings. Find the empty Saturday shift on Wednesday.
  6. Schedule vs actual. A schedule tied to the time clock shows late starts and missed shifts the same day.
  7. A path to payroll. Worked hours should not need retyping.

What is employee scheduling software?

Employee scheduling software is where a manager builds the week of shifts, publishes it to the team and handles the changes that follow: swaps, open shifts and time off. Workzenly also shows approved PTO while you schedule, tells you before a shift goes uncovered, and compares the schedule with the hours people actually clocked.

How far in advance do I have to post a work schedule?

Federal law sets no notice period. Oregon and several cities, including Seattle, San Francisco, Chicago, Philadelphia, Los Angeles and New York City for fast food, require covered employers to post schedules 14 days or two weeks ahead, and New York City retail employers must give 72 hours. If none of these apply to you, posting a week or more ahead is still good practice.

Do fair workweek laws apply to small businesses?

Usually not to a single independent shop. Most of these laws cover larger employers in set industries, often counted by employees or locations worldwide, such as 250 or 500 employees. Franchise locations can be counted toward the totals in some cities, so a small franchisee may be covered. Check the rules with your city or state labor agency.

Can employees swap shifts in Workzenly?

Yes. Staff see the schedule on their phones and can pick up an open shift or swap one with a teammate, and the schedule updates for everyone. Where a scheduling law applies, keep a record that the employee asked for the change; Oregon, for example, exempts changes made at an employee's written request from its notice rules.

Does the schedule show who is on PTO?

Yes. Scheduling and PTO share one record, so approved time off shows up where you build shifts. When someone asks for a day off, the manager sees who else is already out that day before saying yes, and can find cover from the schedule in the same place.

How many hours can a 15-year-old be scheduled?

Under federal child labor rules, 14- and 15-year-olds can work outside school hours only: up to 3 hours on a school day, 18 hours in a school week, 8 hours on a non-school day and 40 hours in a non-school week, between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day). Federal law does not limit hours for 16- and 17-year-olds, but many states do, and the stricter rule applies.
