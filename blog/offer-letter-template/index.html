Offer letter template: four job offer letters to copy

Copyable offer letters for an hourly shift job, a salaried exempt role, a part-time or seasonal hire and a short offer email, plus what to put in them and what to leave out.

Quick answer

  • Copy the template that fits the job, replace every [bracketed] item, and state the title, start date, pay rate, pay frequency, exempt or non-exempt status, schedule and at-will terms.
  • Make the offer contingent on anything you still need, such as a background check (with FCRA disclosure and consent) and Form I-9 on the first day.
  • Pair the letter with any state pay notice, such as California's 2810.5 notice or New York's LS 54, and have an employment attorney review your standard version.

This offer letter template set is written for US small businesses hiring hourly, salaried, part-time and seasonal staff. Each job offer letter below is complete: copy it, replace the [bracketed] items, delete the lines that do not apply and send it. After the templates you will find what every employment offer letter should include, the promises to keep out of it, the state notices that go with it, the hiring laws that affect the offer, and how to get it signed. The examples use Northfield Coffee, a coffee shop in Austin, Texas. This is general information, not legal advice. Have an employment attorney review your standard letter before you use it, especially if you hire in more than one state.

Offer letter templates to copy

Each block copies as plain text, so it pastes cleanly into Google Docs, Word or your HR system. Template 1 is the most common case for shift-based businesses. Template 4 is a short email you can send before or with the formal letter.

Template 1: Hourly, non-exempt shift job
[Company name] [Street address, city, state ZIP] [Date] [Candidate full name] [Candidate address or email] Dear [First name], We are happy to offer you the position of [Job title, e.g. Barista] at [Company name], [location, e.g. our South Lamar store in Austin, Texas]. You will report to [Manager name, title]. Start date: [Monday, November 16, 2026], at [7:00 AM]. Pay: $[17.75] per hour, paid [every other Friday (biweekly)] by [direct deposit or check]. Your first paycheck will be on [Friday, December 4, 2026], for hours worked through [Saturday, November 28, 2026]. Classification: This is a non-exempt, hourly position. You will be paid for all hours worked and will receive overtime at 1.5 times your regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek [and any other hours required by state law]. Our workweek runs [Sunday 12:00 AM through Saturday 11:59 PM]. All overtime must be approved in advance by your manager, but you will be paid for all overtime you work. Schedule: This is a [full-time / part-time] position. You can expect about [30 to 38] hours a week, usually scheduled between [6:00 AM and 8:00 PM], including some weekends. Schedules are posted [two weeks] ahead in [our employee app]. Hours and shifts can change with business needs. Tips: [Delete if not applicable.] You will take part in the store tip pool as described in our tip policy. Tips are paid [with each paycheck]. Breaks: You will receive [an unpaid 30-minute meal break on shifts over 6 hours and a paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked], in line with our break policy and state law. Benefits: You will be eligible for [paid time off accruing at 1 hour for every 30 hours worked / employee discount / meal during shifts / other] as described in our employee handbook. [Health insurance eligibility: describe or delete.] Benefits are governed by the plan documents and our policies, which may change. At-will employment: Your employment with [Company name] is at will. This means you or [Company name] may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice. Nothing in this letter or any other document creates a contract of employment for any specific length of time. Only [title, e.g. the owner] can change at-will status, and only in a signed written agreement. Conditions of this offer: This offer depends on [a background check, after you receive a separate disclosure and give written authorization / a food handler card within 30 days of hire / other], and on your completing Form I-9 and providing documents that show your identity and authorization to work in the United States, as required by federal law. You will complete Section 1 of Form I-9 no later than your first day and present your documents within three business days of starting. First day: Please arrive at [location] at [time] and ask for [name]. Wear [dress code]. Before your first day, you will receive your Form W-4, direct deposit form, employee handbook and any required state pay notice to complete. To accept this offer, please sign below and return it by [Monday, November 9, 2026]. If you have questions, contact [name] at [phone or email]. We look forward to working with you. Sincerely, [Name] [Title] [Company name] ACCEPTANCE I accept this offer of employment on the terms above. I understand my employment is at will and that this letter is not a contract for any specific length of time. Signature: ______________________ Date: ____________ Printed name: ______________________
Template 2: Salaried, exempt role
[Company name] [Street address, city, state ZIP] [Date] [Candidate full name] [Candidate address or email] Dear [First name], On behalf of [Company name], I am pleased to offer you the position of [Job title, e.g. Store Manager], reporting to [Manager name, title]. In this role you will [one or two lines on the main duties, e.g. run daily operations at the South Lamar store, hire and supervise a team of 10 to 12 employees, and own the store's schedule and budget]. Start date: [Monday, November 30, 2026]. Salary: $[58,500.00] per year, paid in equal installments of $[2,250.00] [every other Friday (biweekly)], less required tax withholding and any deductions you authorize. Classification: This is a full-time, salaried position classified as exempt from overtime under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act [and state law]. You will receive your full salary for any week in which you perform work, subject to the deductions allowed by law, and you will not be paid overtime. If your duties or salary change in a way that affects this classification, we will tell you in writing. Schedule: Our normal business hours are [6:00 AM to 8:00 PM, seven days a week]. You will typically work [five days a week, including some weekends], and we expect you to work the hours needed to meet the responsibilities of the role. Bonus: [Delete if not applicable.] You will be eligible for a discretionary [quarterly] bonus based on [store and individual goals]. Bonus eligibility, amount and timing are at the sole discretion of [Company name] and are described in the [bonus plan], which may change. You must be employed on the payment date to receive a bonus [check state law]. Benefits: You will be eligible for [health insurance after (30) days / (80) hours of paid time off per year / retirement plan / other], under the terms of the plan documents and our policies, which [Company name] may change from time to time. At-will employment: Your employment with [Company name] is at will. You or [Company name] may end it at any time, with or without cause or notice. This letter is not a contract of employment for any specific length of time, and no statement by anyone at [Company name] can change that unless it is in writing and signed by [title]. Conditions of this offer: This offer depends on [a background check, after you receive a separate disclosure and give written authorization / reference checks / your signing our confidentiality agreement], and on your completing Form I-9 and providing acceptable documents showing your identity and authorization to work in the United States. [If you are bound by an agreement with a former employer that limits your work, please tell us before you accept.] Please sign and return this letter by [date] to accept. Questions can go to [name] at [phone or email]. We are excited to have you join us. Sincerely, [Name] [Title] [Company name] ACCEPTANCE I accept this offer of employment on the terms above. I understand my employment is at will and that this letter is not a contract for any specific length of time. Signature: ______________________ Date: ____________ Printed name: ______________________

Employment with [Company name] is at will, and this email is not a contract of employment. If you have questions, reply here or call me at [phone]. We are looking forward to working with you. How to fill in the offer letter template

Fill it in after you have checked the job against federal and state wage rules, not before. A few minutes here prevents most offer letter mistakes.

  1. Pick the right version. Use Template 1 for any job paid by the hour, Template 2 only for roles that pass the exempt salary and duties tests, and Template 3 for part-time and seasonal staff. Use Template 4 as a quick heads-up, never as the only record.
  2. Confirm the classification. The federal salary level for the executive, administrative and professional exemptions is $684 a week ($35,568 a year), and California, New York, Washington, Colorado and some other states set higher thresholds. Our exempt vs non-exempt guide walks through the duties tests job by job.
  3. Check the pay against minimum wage. Texas follows the federal $7.25 minimum, but many states and cities are much higher. Use the rate that applies where the person will work.
  4. Match the pay schedule to payroll. In the Northfield Coffee example, pay is biweekly: the November 15 to 28, 2026 period is paid on Friday, December 4, so a new hire starting November 16 gets a first paycheck on December 4. Put the real first payday in the letter so the new hire is not left guessing.
  5. List only the conditions you will actually use. If you do not run background checks, delete that line. If you do, the separate disclosure covered below has to go out before you order the report.
  6. Delete every bracket you do not need. Read the letter once out loud. Anything still in [brackets] is a mistake the candidate will notice.

An example of a filled-in offer letter sample: Northfield Coffee offers a new barista, Rosa Delgado, $17.75 an hour, non-exempt, about 30 to 38 hours a week, starting Monday, November 16, 2026, with acceptance due by November 9. Her first paycheck on December 4 covers hours she works November 16 to 28. If she works 42 hours in one of those weeks, she is owed 40 × $17.75 = $710.00 plus 2 × $26.625 = $53.25, for $763.25 that week. That is the math the "Classification" paragraph promises.

What to leave out of an employment offer letter

Most employment offer letter problems come from friendly words that read like promises. Courts in some states have treated letters as contracts when they implied job security. Keep these out:

  • Guaranteed tenure. Words like "permanent position," "long-term role," "job for life" or "you will be with us for years."
  • Annual salary framed as a guarantee for the year. "$58,500 per year" is fine. "You will earn $58,500 in your first year" can be read as a promise of a full year's pay.
  • Firing only for cause. Avoid "you can only be let go for poor performance" or a promised discipline process. That language can undo at-will status.
  • A probation period that sounds like a guarantee. If you use an introductory period, say that completing it does not change at-will status.
  • Exact benefit terms. Summarize and point to the plan documents, which control and can change.
  • Pay below what the law requires, or an exempt label on a job that does not meet the tests. The letter cannot waive overtime or minimum wage.
  • Questions or comments about protected traits, such as age, pregnancy, religion or national origin, and any mention of salary history where it is banned (see below).
  • A demand for specific I-9 documents. The employee chooses which acceptable documents to show; asking for a particular one can be discrimination under federal law.

State pay notices to send with the offer letter

Some states require a separate written notice of pay at hire. An offer letter usually does not satisfy these rules on its own, so send the state form with your new-hire packet. Our new hire paperwork guide lists the full packet by state.

  • California: Labor Code 2810.5, part of the Wage Theft Protection Act, requires a written notice at the time of hire for non-exempt private-sector employees, with the pay rate, payday, employer details, workers' compensation carrier and paid sick leave information. The Labor Commissioner publishes a template in several languages.
  • New York: Labor Law section 195(1) requires a pay notice at hiring. The Department of Labor publishes form LS 54 for hourly employees and other versions for other pay types. Give it in the employee's primary language when the Department has a translation, get a signed acknowledgment, give the employee a copy and keep the original for six years.
  • Other states: Several other states have their own rules on telling new hires their pay rate, payday or hours in writing. Texas does not require a separate pay notice at hire, though the Texas Payday Law requires employers to post paydays. Check your state labor department.

Pay transparency, salary history bans and background checks

Pay transparency laws

A growing list of states require a pay range in job postings, and some also require you to give the range to applicants on request or to current employees. As of late 2026, states with posting or disclosure laws include California, Colorado, Washington, New York and Illinois, along with others such as Hawaii, Maryland, Minnesota, New Jersey, Vermont and Massachusetts, plus Washington, D.C. Some cities have their own rules. Employer size thresholds, what counts as a posting and whether benefits must be listed all vary, and new laws keep taking effect. There is no federal pay transparency law. Check your state labor department before you post, and make sure the offer you make falls in the range you advertised.

Salary history bans

Many states and cities bar employers from asking applicants about their pay history or from using it to set pay, and some extend the rule to the offer stage. Where these bans apply, base the offer on the job and the range, not on what the person earned before, and keep salary history out of the letter. Texas has no statewide ban, but if you hire remote staff in other states, their state's rule can apply.

Background checks and the FCRA

If you use a background screening company, the Fair Credit Reporting Act applies. The Federal Trade Commission says you must tell the applicant in writing, in a stand-alone document that is not part of the application, that you may get a report, and get their written permission before you order it. If you decide not to hire based on the report, you must first send a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report and "A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act," and then an adverse action notice with the screening company's contact details and the person's dispute rights. Many states and cities add "ban the box" or fair-chance rules that delay criminal history questions until after a conditional offer, which is one more reason to make the offer letter conditional.

How to send the offer letter and get it signed

Call the candidate first, then send the letter the same day. A phone call lets you answer questions about pay and schedule before anything is in writing.

  • Electronic signatures are valid. The federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN Act) says a signature or contract may not be denied legal effect only because it is electronic, and nearly every state has adopted the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act.
  • Keep the audit trail. Record who signed, the date and time, and the exact version of the letter. Give the new hire a copy.
  • Offer paper if asked. Some people do not have reliable email or prefer to sign by hand.
  • Set a reasonable deadline. Three to five business days is common for hourly roles; give salaried candidates time to resign from a current job.
  • Re-issue, do not edit by hand. If the candidate negotiates pay or a start date, send a new letter with the agreed terms rather than marking up the signed copy.

What happens after the job offer letter is accepted

The signed letter starts onboarding. Use our employee onboarding guide for the full first-week plan; the paperwork steps are:

  1. Form I-9. Section 1 is due no later than the first day of work for pay, and Section 2 within three business days after that. USCIS allows Section 1 any time after the offer is accepted. See our I-9 form guide for the document lists.
  2. Form W-4 and the state withholding form. Collect them before the first payroll. Our W-4 form guide covers each step.
  3. State pay notice if your state requires one, and state new-hire reporting, usually within 20 days.
  4. Direct deposit, emergency contact and handbook acknowledgment.
  5. Set them up in payroll and scheduling with the exact rate, classification and start date from the letter, then schedule the first shifts.

Keep the signed letter in the employee's file with the rest of their hiring records. If pay or status changes later, document the change in writing rather than editing the original letter.

Frequently asked questions

Is an offer letter legally required?

No federal law requires a private employer to send an offer letter. It is still the clearest record of the pay, schedule and status you agreed on. Some states require a separate written pay notice at hire, such as the California Labor Code 2810.5 notice or the New York LS 54, and an offer letter does not replace those forms.

Is an offer letter a binding contract?

An offer letter can be treated as a contract if it promises a fixed term, guaranteed pay for a period or firing only for cause. To keep employment at will, say so plainly, state that the letter is not a contract for any length of time, and avoid words like permanent or guaranteed. Have an employment attorney review your standard letter.

Can I take back a job offer after it is accepted?

In an at-will state you usually can, but the reason must not be discriminatory or retaliatory, and a rescinded offer based on a background check must follow the FCRA adverse action steps. Some states let a candidate sue if they relied on the offer, for example by quitting another job. Get legal advice before you rescind an accepted offer.

Can an offer letter be signed electronically?

Yes. The federal ESIGN Act says a signature or contract cannot be denied legal effect only because it is electronic, and nearly every state has adopted the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act. Keep a record of who signed and when, give the new hire a copy, and let them sign on paper if they ask.

Should the offer letter say whether the job is exempt or non-exempt?

Yes. Stating the classification tells the new hire whether they will be paid overtime and forces you to check the salary and duties tests before you commit. Calling a job exempt in a letter does not make it exempt; the actual pay and duties decide that under federal and state law.
