Quick answer

  • The California minimum wage is $16.90 an hour for every employer, regardless of size, effective January 1, 2026.
  • It rises to $17.40 an hour on January 1, 2027, as announced by the Department of Industrial Relations.
  • There is no tip credit. Many cities pay more (San Francisco and Berkeley are $19.61), fast food chains pay $20.00, and covered health care workers earn $19.28 to $25.00.

The California minimum wage is $16.90 an hour in 2026 and applies to every employer in the state, no matter how many people it employs. On January 1, 2027, it goes up to $17.40 an hour. That is the floor. If you have staff in a city or county with its own ordinance, in a covered fast food restaurant, or at a covered health care facility, a higher rate applies, and you must pay whichever rate is highest for each hour worked. This guide covers each of those rates with sources, worked pay examples, and a checklist for the January change.

California minimum wage 2026 and the 2027 increase

The California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) announced in December 2025 (release 2025-118) that the statewide rate would rise from $16.50 to $16.90 an hour on January 1, 2026. Its official minimum wage order, MW-2026, says every employer, “regardless of the number of employees,” must pay at least that amount for all hours worked.

On July 31, 2026, the California Department of Finance certified the next increase, and DIR confirmed it in release 2026-66 on August 13, 2026: the minimum wage in California will be $17.40 an hour on January 1, 2027.

PeriodState minimum wageApplies to
January 1 to December 31, 2026$16.90All employers
From January 1, 2027$17.40All employers

How the number is set: once the rate reached $15, state law requires a yearly adjustment for inflation based on the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W). DIR’s FAQ says the rate cannot go down, and any single-year increase is capped at 3.5%. The 2027 increase of $0.50 is about 3.0%.

California is one of several states with its own overtime rules too, so the minimum wage is only part of the picture. Daily overtime after 8 hours and double time after 12 are covered in our California overtime law guide.

Example with daily overtime. A cashier earns $16.90 an hour and works five 9-hour shifts, 45 hours in total. California owes time and a half for each hour over 8 in a day, so 5 hours are overtime at $25.35 ($16.90 × 1.5).

  1. Regular pay: 40 hours × $16.90 = $676.00.
  2. Overtime pay: 5 hours × $25.35 = $126.75.
  3. Total for the week: $676.00 + $126.75 = $802.75.

The same week in San Diego, where the city rate is $17.75, pays 40 × $17.75 = $710.00 plus 5 × $26.625 ($17.75 × 1.5) = $133.13, for $843.13. Tips, learners and minors

  • No tip credit. DIR states that an employer may not use an employee’s tips as a credit toward the minimum wage. A server in Sacramento gets at least $16.90 an hour from the employer, and tips are on top. City ordinances in places like San Diego and Berkeley say the same.
  • Minors. There is no separate youth wage. DIR says no distinction is made between adults and minors for the minimum wage.
  • Learners. An employee who has no similar or related experience in the job may be paid at least 85% of the minimum wage for their first 160 hours of work. Check the local ordinance before using it, because a city minimum may not include the same exception.
  • Meals and lodging. These can count toward the minimum wage only with a voluntary written agreement, and only up to the caps in MW-2026, such as $8.42 for a lunch in 2026.
  • Who is not covered. The minimum wage order does not apply to outside salespersons or to the parent, spouse or children of the employer.

Fast food and health care minimum wages

Fast food. Since April 1, 2024, covered fast food restaurant employees must be paid at least $20.00 an hour, according to DIR’s fast food minimum wage FAQ. It applies to limited-service restaurants that are part of a national chain with at least 60 establishments nationwide. The Fast Food Council can raise the rate each year by the lower of 3.5% or the change in the CPI. As of this update, DIR’s fast food order still lists $20.00; check the council’s notices before each January. Where a city rate is higher, such as Emeryville’s $20.34, the higher city rate wins.

Health care. SB 525 set a separate minimum for covered health care workers, including support staff such as janitors, food service workers and clerks at covered facilities. DIR’s supplement MW-7/2026–7/2027 lists these rates from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027:

City and county minimum wages in 2026

This is not every local ordinance. Cities including Alameda, Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Malibu, Redwood City, Richmond, San Mateo, Santa Rosa and others also set their own rates, so check the official website of every city where your staff work. Exempt salary threshold for 2026 and 2027

The CA minimum wage also sets the salary floor for most overtime-exempt employees. To pass the salary test, an exempt executive, administrative or professional employee must earn at least two times the state minimum wage for full-time work.

  • 2026: $16.90 × 2 × 40 × 52 = $70,304 a year ($5,858.67 a month).
  • 2027: $17.40 × 2 × 40 × 52 = $72,384 a year ($6,032.00 a month), per DIR release 2026-66.

City minimum wages do not change this figure; only the state rate counts. Covered health care workers have a higher test: DIR says the salary must be at least 1.5 times the health care minimum wage or two times the state minimum, whichever is greater. At $25.00 an hour that is $25.00 × 1.5 × 2,080 hours = $78,000 a year. The salary is only half the test, though. Duties matter too, as our exempt vs non-exempt guide explains.

Minimum wage posting requirements

  • State order. Post DIR’s official notice, MW-2026, next to your IWC industry or occupation wage order where employees can see it. DIR lets you print it from its website on 8.5" × 14" paper. Replace it with the 2027 version once DIR publishes it.
  • Industry supplements. Fast food and health care employers must also make the DIR supplement for their industry available to employees.
  • Local notices. Most cities with their own minimum wage require their own poster, usually in several languages. Los Angeles, Oakland, Berkeley, Sunnyvale and Santa Monica all publish official notices to download.

What employers should do before January 1, 2027

  1. List every work location. Note the city or unincorporated county for each one and look up its current and 2027 rates.
  2. Find everyone under $17.40. Update hourly rates for the first payroll period that includes hours on or after January 1, 2027, plus any higher city rate.
  3. Check exempt salaries. Anyone classified as exempt must earn at least $72,384 a year from January 1, 2027, or be reclassified as non-exempt and paid overtime.
  4. Watch for pay compression. A lead earning $17.25 today will soon earn less than a new hire at $17.40. Decide on raises before the schedule is published.
  5. Recalculate overtime and premiums. Overtime, double time, and meal and rest break premiums are all based on the regular rate, so they rise with the base wage.
  6. Swap the posters. Replace state and city notices as new versions are released.
  7. Plan for July 1 too. Los Angeles, San Francisco, Berkeley, Emeryville, Pasadena and Santa Monica change on July 1, and health care rates step up then as well.

Underpaying the minimum wage is costly. Employees can recover unpaid wages with interest and liquidated damages, and the Labor Commissioner’s Office can assess penalties. Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum wage in California right now?

The California minimum wage is $16.90 an hour for all employers, regardless of size, from January 1, 2026. It rises to $17.40 on January 1, 2027. Many cities and counties set higher local rates, and covered fast food and health care workers have their own higher minimums.

Can California employers pay tipped workers less than minimum wage?

No. California does not allow a tip credit, so tipped employees such as servers and bartenders must receive the full state or local minimum wage for every hour before tips. Tips belong to the employee and cannot be counted toward the employer’s minimum wage obligation.

Do minors get the same minimum wage in California?

Yes. The Department of Industrial Relations says there is no distinction between adults and minors for the minimum wage. The one state exception is the learner rate: an employee with no similar or related experience may be paid at least 85% of the minimum wage for their first 160 hours of work.

What is the fast food minimum wage in California?

Covered fast food restaurant employees must be paid at least $20.00 an hour, effective April 1, 2024. The rule covers chains with at least 60 establishments nationwide. The Fast Food Council can approve yearly increases, so check the Department of Industrial Relations before each new year. If a city rate is higher, pay the higher rate.

How much does a full-time minimum wage worker earn in California?

At $16.90 an hour, a full-time employee working 40 hours a week earns $676.00 a week before taxes, or $35,152 a year over 52 weeks. At the 2027 rate of $17.40, the same schedule pays $696.00 a week and $36,192 a year. City rates can make these figures higher.
