How many pay periods in a year? 52, 26, 24 or 12, and when it is 27

The count for each pay frequency, why some biweekly employers get 27 paydays in 2027, and how to pay salaried staff when it happens.

Quick answer

  • Weekly pay has 52 pay periods a year, biweekly 26, semimonthly 24 and monthly 12.
  • Years with 53 of your payday weekday add one more: 53 weekly or 27 biweekly paydays. 2027 has 53 Fridays, so a Friday biweekly payroll that pays on January 1, 2027 pays 27 times.
  • For salaried staff in a 27-period year, either pay the usual amount 27 times (about 3.85% more payroll) or divide the annual salary by 27 (about 3.7% less per check), and tell employees in writing first.

How many pay periods in a year depends on your pay frequency: 52 for weekly, 26 for biweekly, 24 for semimonthly and 12 for monthly. Those are the normal counts. Weekly and biweekly schedules follow weekdays rather than calendar dates, and a year holds 52 weeks plus one day (two in a leap year), so every few years a weekly payroll gets 53 paydays and a biweekly payroll gets 27. 2027 is one of those years for anyone paying on Fridays. This guide gives the counts, the 2027 math, the options for salaried employees, the biweekly vs semimonthly trade-offs, state pay frequency rules and a converter for per-paycheck amounts.

How many pay periods in a year, by pay frequency

Each schedule splits a 2,080-hour year (52 weeks × 40 hours) differently. The table shows the standard count, the hours a full-time employee works in one period, and what a $50,000 salary comes to per paycheck.

Semimonthly and monthly counts never change because they are tied to dates. Weekly and biweekly counts depend on how many times your payday weekday appears in the calendar year, which is where the 53rd and 27th paydays come from.

Which years have 27 biweekly pay periods, and why 2027 is one

A 365-day year is 52 weeks and one day, so the weekday the year starts on appears 53 times. A 366-day leap year starts with two weekdays that appear 53 times. 2027 begins on Friday, January 1 and ends on Friday, December 31, so it has 53 Fridays. An employer that pays every Friday runs 53 weekly payrolls. An employer that pays every other Friday gets 26 or 27 paydays depending on which Fridays it lands on:

  • 27 paydays: if your paydays include Friday, January 1, 2027, the schedule runs January 1, January 15, January 29 and so on through Friday, December 31, 2027. That is 27 checks.
  • 26 paydays: if your first 2027 payday is Friday, January 8, the schedule runs through Friday, December 24, 2027, and the next payday falls on January 7, 2028. That is 26 checks.

So the question “27 pay periods 2027?” has no single answer. Check which group you are in by counting your 2027 paydays now. Our 2027 payroll calendar lists both biweekly tracks, every semimonthly payday and the bank holidays that move them.

Two timing details catch people out. First, January 1 is a federal holiday, so banks do not settle direct deposits that day. If you move the January 1 payday to Thursday, December 31, 2026, the extra check lands in 2026 instead, and your 2026 W-2s carry 27 paychecks of wages. Wages count for tax purposes in the year they are paid, not earned. Second, paying on Mondays, Tuesdays, Wednesdays or Thursdays gives you 26 biweekly paydays in 2027; your next 27-payday year will come when your weekday appears 53 times. For Friday payers, 2027 is the first since 2021, and the next is 2032.

How to handle a 27-pay-period year for salaried staff

Hourly employees are simple: 27 paydays means 27 periods of hours, and total pay follows the hours. The decision is about salaried employees whose pay was budgeted as an annual figure divided by 26. You have two main choices.

Option 1: pay the regular biweekly amount 27 times

Nothing changes on the pay stub. A $60,000 salary keeps paying $2,307.69 every two weeks, and the employee receives $62,307.69 in the calendar year. This is the simplest option for morale and for any offer letter that states pay as a biweekly amount. The cost is one extra period of salaried payroll, about 3.85% (1 ÷ 26) of your annual salaried wages, plus the employer Social Security, Medicare and unemployment taxes on it, plus any 401(k) match calculated per check. Budget for it, because the extra check will show up in a quarter you did not plan for.

Option 2: divide the annual salary by 27

Each check shrinks by about 3.7% (1 ÷ 27) and the employee receives the same annual salary. The $60,000 employee gets $2,222.22 per check instead of $2,307.69, a difference of $85.47 per payday. Before you choose this route:

  • Check the salary basis test. Exempt employees must earn at least $684 per week under the federal rule now in effect ($1,368 per biweekly check). Dividing by 27 drops the weekly equivalent to salary ÷ 54, so anyone earning less than $36,936 falls below the federal line, and the bar is far higher in states with their own thresholds. California’s exempt minimum is twice the state minimum wage for full-time work: $1,352 per week in 2026 and $1,392 per week from January 1, 2027. Our exempt vs non-exempt guide lists the state figures.
  • Read the offer letters and any contracts. If pay is written as “$2,307.69 biweekly,” a smaller check is a pay cut you cannot make without agreement. If it is written as “$60,000 a year,” dividing by 27 honors the deal.
  • Give written notice before the first smaller check. Several states require advance written notice of any change to pay rate or payday; New York’s Wage Theft Prevention Act and California’s Labor Code 2810.5 notices are the best-known examples. Even where no law requires it, a note explaining that the annual salary is unchanged prevents a flood of questions on payday.

Deductions and benefits per period

Decide separately how the 27th check handles deductions. Benefit premiums are usually quoted as an annual or monthly cost split across 26 checks; a $2,600 annual premium is $100 per check. In a 27-period year you can take $100 from the first 26 checks and nothing from the 27th, or recalculate to $96.30 across all 27. Fixed-dollar 401(k) deferrals, garnishments with per-pay limits and employer matches need the same review. Percentage-based items such as income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare simply follow the wages on each check, though a large year-end payroll can push high earners toward the Social Security wage base ($184,500 for 2026, $192,300 for 2027) a check earlier than expected. Whatever you decide, apply it to everyone on the schedule and write it down.

Biweekly vs semimonthly: the differences that matter

Semi monthly vs biweekly is the choice most small employers end up weighing, because both produce roughly two checks a month. The two extra biweekly paydays a year change several things.

BiweeklySemimonthly
Paydays a year26, sometimes 2724, always
Paydays per month2, with 3 in two months of most yearsExactly 2
Hours in a full-time period8086.67 on average (varies by month)
Workweeks per periodExactly 2Partial weeks at the start and end
Overtime handlingFinished when the period closesSplit workweeks must be totaled across two periods
Payday weekdayAlways the sameMoves; weekends and holidays shift it
Monthly budgetingPayroll cost differs by monthSame salary cost every month

The overtime point is the one that creates errors. Under the FLSA, overtime is owed for hours over 40 in each fixed workweek, and the pay period does not change that. On a biweekly schedule with a Sunday–Saturday workweek, every period contains two whole workweeks, so a 46-hour week and a 34-hour week owe 6 overtime hours even though the period totals 80. On a semimonthly schedule, a period ending on the 15th usually ends mid-week. The employer pays straight time for the hours in that partial week, then finishes the week on the next period and pays any overtime it produced on the following check, which is allowed as long as the overtime is paid on the regular payday for the period in which the workweek ended. How to calculate overtime walks through the regular rate math for split weeks and multiple rates.

State pay frequency laws

Federal law does not set a pay frequency. The Fair Labor Standards Act requires that wages be paid on the regular payday for the period, but the minimum frequency comes from state law. The US Department of Labor’s Wage and Hour Division publishes a state payday requirements table (last revised January 1, 2023) that summarizes each state, with footnotes for the exceptions. A few examples, which you should confirm with the state agency before you commit to a schedule:

  • New York: manual workers are generally paid weekly, within seven days of the end of the week, unless the employer has approval from the New York State Department of Labor to pay semimonthly. Clerical and other workers must be paid at least twice a month. Our New York minimum wage guide covers the manual worker definition.
  • California: most wages must be paid at least twice during each calendar month on regular paydays set in advance. Under Labor Code 204, wages earned from the 1st to the 15th are due by the 26th, and wages earned from the 16th to month end by the 10th of the next month; weekly and biweekly payrolls must pay within seven calendar days of the period end. Exempt executive, administrative and professional employees may be paid once a month by the 26th. See the California minimum wage guide.
  • Texas: under the Texas Payday Law, employees who are exempt from FLSA overtime must be paid at least monthly; other employees at least twice a month. If the employer does not designate paydays, they default to the 1st and 15th. The Texas minimum wage guide explains the Texas Workforce Commission wage claim process.
  • Arizona: two or more paydays a month, not more than 16 days apart.
  • Massachusetts: hourly employees must be paid weekly or biweekly; salaried employees may be paid semimonthly, or monthly if they agree.
  • Connecticut: weekly, with longer intervals up to monthly allowed only with the labor commissioner’s approval.
  • Illinois, Ohio, Georgia: at least semimonthly, with Illinois allowing monthly pay for executive, administrative and professional staff.
  • Washington and Colorado: monthly is the minimum.
  • Alabama, Florida, South Carolina: listed with no state pay frequency requirement.

Many states also require that paydays be regular and posted, and set a maximum lag between the end of the period and the payday. A longer lag gives you more time to collect and approve hours, but your state may cap it. When a state requires a frequency your current schedule does not meet, you can always pay more often than the minimum, never less.

Which pay frequency fits hourly and salaried teams

For hourly and shift teams, weekly or biweekly is the practical choice. Both line up with the workweek, so overtime is calculated once and is final when the period closes, and a time clock that closes on Saturday night gives the manager a clean set of hours to approve. Weekly pay is popular in restaurants and construction and is mandatory for New York manual workers; it means 52 payroll runs and 52 sets of records, which is fine with software and painful on paper. Biweekly halves the runs while keeping whole workweeks.

For salaried teams, semimonthly or monthly often fits better. Salary cost is identical every month, benefit deductions divide evenly, and there is no 27-period year to plan around. The trade-off is that non-exempt salaried employees and any hourly staff on the same schedule need split-week overtime handling.

Many employers run one schedule for everyone because it is simpler to administer, and biweekly is the common compromise. If you run two schedules, keep each group’s workweek, pay period and payday written in your handbook and on the pay stub, so nobody has to guess which check a shift lands on. Whatever you choose, the steps in how to process payroll are the same; only the calendar changes.

Converting pay between frequencies, with exact math

To convert an annual salary to a per-paycheck amount, divide by the number of pay periods. To convert between frequencies, go through the annual figure first: biweekly pay × 26 = annual, then annual ÷ 24 = semimonthly. Never convert biweekly to semimonthly by adding “half a week”; the ratio is exactly 26 ÷ 24 = 1.0833. For a $50,000 salary:

  • Weekly: $50,000 ÷ 52 = $961.54
  • Biweekly: $50,000 ÷ 26 = $1,923.08
  • Biweekly in a 27-period year: $50,000 ÷ 27 = $1,851.85
  • Semimonthly: $50,000 ÷ 24 = $2,083.33
  • Monthly: $50,000 ÷ 12 = $4,166.67
  • Hourly equivalent: $50,000 ÷ 2,080 = $24.04

Rounding leaves pennies behind. 26 × $1,923.08 = $50,000.08, so most payroll systems adjust the final check of the year by a few cents. Use the converter below for any salary and frequency; it assumes a 40-hour week for the hours and hourly figures.

How a biweekly calendar works in practice: Northfield Coffee

Northfield Coffee in Austin, Texas pays its team every other Friday with a Sunday–Saturday workweek. The pay period runs two full workweeks, and payday is the Friday six days after the period closes. The lag gives manager Marcus the Sunday through Tuesday to fix missed punches, approve timesheets and submit payroll in time for direct deposit.

Pay period (Sun–Sat)WorkweeksPayday (Friday)
Nov 1 – Nov 14, 2026Nov 1–7, Nov 8–14Nov 20, 2026
Nov 15 – Nov 28, 2026Nov 15–21, Nov 22–28Dec 4, 2026
Nov 29 – Dec 12, 2026Nov 29–Dec 5, Dec 6–12Dec 18, 2026
Dec 13 – Dec 26, 2026Dec 13–19, Dec 20–26Jan 1, 2027 (holiday; paid Dec 31)

Because every period holds two whole workweeks, overtime is settled on the same check. In the November 1–14 period, Emily Carter (kitchen, $19.00 an hour) worked 72 regular hours and 6 overtime hours and took 8 hours of PTO. Her 46-hour week owes 6 hours at $28.50 regardless of the 34-hour week next to it, and the PTO hours are paid but do not count toward overtime. Jordan Lee (barista, $18.25 an hour) with two straight 40-hour weeks earns $1,460.00 for the period.

Northfield’s schedule lands exactly on the 27-payday track for 2027: January 1, 2027 is its next payday after December 18, 2026. Because January 1 is a bank holiday, Northfield pays that check on Thursday, December 31, 2026, which puts the 27th check of the cycle into 2026’s W-2s and leaves 26 paydays in calendar 2027 (January 15 through December 31). Had it chosen to pay on Monday, January 4 instead, 2027 would have 27 paydays and 2026 its normal 26. Since Northfield’s team is hourly, nobody’s pay changes either way; the only job is to tell the team which date to expect and to make sure 2026 year-end totals are pulled after that final run. A salaried employer on the same track would need to make the Option 1 or Option 2 decision above before that check.

Frequently asked questions

How many biweekly pay periods are there in 2027?

Either 26 or 27, depending on your payday. 2027 starts and ends on a Friday, so it has 53 Fridays. A biweekly payroll whose Friday paydays include January 1, 2027 pays 27 times that year (January 1 through December 31). One whose paydays start January 8 pays 26 times, the last on December 24.

How many paychecks in a year do you get on a semimonthly schedule?

Always 24: two paydays a month, usually the 15th and the last day of the month, or the 1st and the 15th. Because semimonthly paydays are tied to calendar dates rather than weekdays, the count never changes from year to year, and each check covers about 86.67 hours of a 40-hour week.

Do I have to pay salaried employees a 27th paycheck?

It depends on how their pay is defined. If an offer letter promises an annual salary, dividing it by 27 keeps the annual total the same. If it promises a biweekly amount, you owe that amount every payday, including the 27th. Either way, tell employees in writing before the first affected check and confirm the per-check amount stays above any exempt salary minimum.

Is biweekly or semimonthly better for hourly employees?

Biweekly is usually simpler for hourly teams. Each pay period holds exactly two full workweeks, so overtime is already counted when the period closes. Semimonthly periods split a workweek between two checks, so you must still total hours by workweek and often pay overtime on the following check. Check your state’s pay frequency law before choosing.

Does federal law set how often employees must be paid?

No. The Fair Labor Standards Act does not set a pay frequency; it requires that wages, including overtime, be paid on the regular payday for the period in which they were earned. Pay frequency is governed by state law, and the Department of Labor keeps a state-by-state payday table. Most states require at least semimonthly pay for most workers.

How many pay periods in a year are there for a weekly payroll?

Normally 52. A year has 52 weeks plus one extra day, or two in a leap year, so in some years a weekly payday lands 53 times. 2027 has 53 Fridays, so an employer paying every Friday will run 53 weekly payrolls in 2027. The same calendar quirk gives some biweekly payrolls 27 paydays.
