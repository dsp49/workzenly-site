Quick answer

  • Hourly rate = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × weeks per year). Full time is 40 × 52 = 2,080 hours.
  • $50,000 a year is $24.04 an hour at 2,080 hours, or $25.00 with the quick 2,000-hour rule.
  • Work 45 hours a week on a fixed salary and the real rate falls: $50,000 becomes $21.37 an hour.

To convert salary to hourly, divide your yearly pay by the number of hours you work in a year. The salary to hourly formula

The math is one division. Work out the hours in your year, then divide:

Hourly rate = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × paid weeks per year)

A full-time job is 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours. So a $50,000 salary is $50,000 ÷ 2,080 = $24.04 an hour. Keep 52 weeks even if you take vacation, as long as the vacation is paid: you still receive the full salary for those weeks. Lower the weeks only for unpaid time, such as a school-year job that pays for 40 weeks.

If you are not paid by the year, convert to annual pay first:

  • Monthly: multiply by 12. $4,500 a month = $54,000 a year.
  • Semimonthly (paid on the 15th and the last day): multiply by 24.
  • Biweekly (every other Friday): multiply by 26. Biweekly and semimonthly are not the same thing, because 26 checks are two more than 24.
  • Weekly: divide by your weekly hours directly. $1,000 for 40 hours = $25.00 an hour.

The 2,000-hour shortcut is good for quick yearly salary to hourly estimates: halve the salary and drop the last three zeros. $60,000 becomes about $30 an hour. It overstates the real rate by about 4%, because a year has 2,080 work hours, not 2,000.

Worked examples: how much is $50,000 a year hourly?

Each example matches what the calculator shows when you enter the same values.

  1. $50,000 a year, 40 hours, 52 weeks (the calculator defaults). 40 × 52 = 2,080 hours. $50,000 ÷ 2,080 = $24.04 an hour. Weekly pay is $961.54, biweekly $1,923.08, semimonthly $2,083.33 and monthly $4,166.67. On a five-day week, that is $192.31 a day.
  2. $65,000 a year on a 37.5-hour week. Many office jobs schedule 7.5-hour days. 37.5 × 52 = 1,950 hours. $65,000 ÷ 1,950 = $33.33 an hour, compared with $31.25 if you assumed 40 hours.
  3. $4,500 a month. $4,500 × 12 = $54,000 a year. $54,000 ÷ 2,080 = $25.96 an hour.
  4. $2,400 every two weeks. $2,400 × 26 = $62,400 a year. $62,400 ÷ 2,080 = $30.00 an hour. Shortcut: $2,400 ÷ 80 hours in the pay period gives the same $30.00.
  5. $60,000 a year with 50 paid weeks. If two weeks a year are unpaid, the year is 40 × 50 = 2,000 hours, so $60,000 ÷ 2,000 = $30.00 an hour. Set paid weeks to 50 in the calculator to see this.

Effective hourly rate when you work 45 or 50 hours

A fixed salary pays the same whether you work 40 hours or 55. For an exempt employee, who is not owed overtime, longer weeks quietly lower the pay per hour. The calculator’s last field shows this effective hourly rate: annual salary ÷ (hours actually worked per week × paid weeks).

If you are non-exempt, the effective rate is not the whole story, because those extra hours must be paid as overtime. The next section shows how.

Salaried non-exempt overtime: the regular rate

A salary does not cancel overtime. Under federal regulation 29 CFR 778.113, the regular rate of a salaried non-exempt employee is the weekly salary divided by the number of hours the salary is intended to cover. Overtime is then owed at 1.5 times that rate for hours over 40 in the workweek.

Example. An assistant manager is non-exempt and paid $52,000 a year for a 40-hour week. Weekly salary: $52,000 ÷ 52 = $1,000. Regular rate: $1,000 ÷ 40 = $25.00. Overtime rate: $37.50. In a 45-hour week she is owed $1,000 + 5 × $37.50 = $1,187.50.

For salaries paid monthly or semimonthly, 29 CFR 778.113(b) says to reduce the salary to its workweek equivalent first: monthly × 12 ÷ 52, or semimonthly × 24 ÷ 52. A $4,000 monthly salary is $923.08 a week, or $23.08 an hour for 40 hours, so the overtime rate is $34.62. Exempt salary thresholds in 2026

To be exempt from overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act, an employee generally must be paid a salary that meets a minimum level and must do work that passes a duties test, such as executive, administrative or professional duties. Both are required.

  • Federal: as of November 4, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor enforces a salary level of $684 a week, or $35,568 a year. In hourly terms that is $17.10 an hour at 2,080 hours, but exempt pay must be a salary, not an hourly wage (computer employees are an exception). A 2024 rule that would have raised the figure was vacated by a federal court, and the Department published a technical amendment in May 2026 restoring the 2019 levels.
  • States: several states require more. California’s threshold is $1,352 a week ($70,304 a year) in 2026, twice the state minimum wage. New York, Washington, Colorado, Alaska and Maine also set higher numbers. Our exempt vs non-exempt guide lists each state’s figure and the duties tests.

Use the table above as a quick screen. A $35,000 salary is $673.08 a week, just under the federal $684, so that employee cannot be exempt under federal rules and must get overtime. The calculator also flags whether your weekly salary is above or below $684.

Minimum wage check for salaried employees

A non-exempt salaried employee must still earn at least the minimum wage for every hour worked. The workweek is the unit for checking this, so divide the weekly salary by the hours actually worked that week and compare the result with the minimum wage. The federal minimum is $7.25 an hour, and the employer must pay the higher state or local rate where one applies.

Example. A non-exempt office worker in California is offered $30,000 a year for 40 hours a week. That is $576.92 a week, or $14.42 an hour. It clears the federal $7.25, but California’s minimum wage is $16.90 an hour in 2026, so the salary is too low before overtime is even counted. At $16.90, a 40-hour week needs at least $676.00, or $35,152 a year. See our California minimum wage guide for city rates, and the minimum wage by state list for everywhere else.

Comparing a salary offer with an hourly offer

Converting salary to hourly is the first step. The headline rate leaves out several things that change what a job is worth:

  • Overtime eligibility. A non-exempt hourly job pays 1.5× for hours over 40. An exempt salaried job does not, so long weeks lower its effective rate.
  • Paid time off and holidays. Salaried roles often include paid vacation and holidays. If an hourly job does not, each day off is unpaid. Our paid time off guide explains how PTO policies work.
  • Benefits. Employer health insurance, retirement matches and other benefits vary by employer and can be worth thousands of dollars a year. Ask for the details in writing.
  • Predictability. A salary is the same every period. Hourly pay moves with the schedule, in both directions.

An illustrative comparison. Offer A is an exempt $55,000 salary with two weeks of paid vacation, and the job usually runs 45 hours a week. Offer B is $25.00 an hour, non-exempt, also with two paid weeks off, and the same 45-hour weeks. Offer A’s effective rate is $55,000 ÷ (45 × 52) = $23.50 an hour. Offer B pays 50 working weeks × (40 × $25.00 + 5 × $37.50) = $59,375, plus 2 weeks × $1,000 of paid time off, for $61,375 a year. If Offer B had no paid time off, it would pay $59,375, still more than Offer A, but with less predictable pay and possibly fewer benefits. Frequently asked questions

How much is $50,000 a year hourly?

$50,000 a year is $24.04 an hour for a full-time schedule of 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, which is 2,080 hours. With the quick 2,000-hour rule it is $25.00 an hour. If you regularly work 45 hours a week on that salary, your effective rate drops to $21.37 an hour.

What is the formula to convert salary to hourly?

Divide the annual salary by the hours you work in a year: hourly rate = salary ÷ (hours per week × weeks per year). For a standard full-time job that is salary ÷ 2,080. Convert monthly pay to annual first by multiplying by 12, and biweekly pay by multiplying by 26.

Should I use 2,080 or 2,000 hours a year?

Use 2,080 hours for an exact figure, because 40 hours × 52 weeks is 2,080 and paid vacation and holidays are still paid weeks. The 2,000-hour shortcut is handy for mental math: halve the salary and drop three zeros, so $60,000 is about $30 an hour. It slightly overstates the true rate.

Do salaried employees get paid for overtime?

Salaried employees who are non-exempt must get overtime at 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Being exempt requires a salary of at least $684 a week under federal rules plus a duties test, and some states set higher salary levels. A salary alone does not make anyone exempt.

Is a salary or an hourly job better?

It depends on hours and benefits. An hourly, non-exempt job pays for every hour and adds overtime after 40, so it often wins when the work regularly runs long. A salaried job usually brings steadier pay and may include more paid time off and benefits. Compare total yearly pay at your real hours, not the headline rate.
