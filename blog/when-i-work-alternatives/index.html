Quick answer

  • The right When I Work alternative depends on why you are looking: payroll in the same system, PTO and onboarding, restaurant-specific tools, or GPS for crews.
  • Alternatives fall into a few groups: scheduling and time apps for hourly teams, restaurant-focused apps, mobile apps for deskless crews, and payroll suites with scheduling attached.
  • Before you switch, export schedules, timesheets and PTO balances, then run both systems side by side for one full pay period and compare every employee's hours.

If you are researching When I Work alternatives, you probably already use a scheduling app and want something that fits your team a little better. That is a normal thing to do as a business grows: a tool picked for one store and twelve people may not match three stores, a payroll change or a new state. This guide explains what When I Work is widely known for, the common reasons buyers compare other options, and how the main When I Work competitors are usually positioned. Then it gives you a comparison table by need, a switching checklist and the questions to ask on every demo. We describe vendors by their widely known positioning only. We do not test, score or rank them, and products change often, so check each vendor's own site for current features and pricing.

What When I Work is known for

When I Work is known for employee scheduling and time tracking for shift-based teams. Its own site describes shift scheduling, time tracking and team messaging for employers with hourly staff, naming industries such as restaurants, retail and healthcare. Managers build a schedule, employees see their shifts on their phones, and hours are tracked against those shifts.

That is a clear, focused job, and plenty of teams are happy with it. Looking at apps like When I Work does not mean something is wrong with your current tool. It usually means your needs have changed, or you want to confirm you are still on the right fit before renewing.

Why teams compare When I Work competitors

These are reasons a buyer might have. Some may already be covered by your current plan, so check with your current vendor too before you move.

  • Payroll in the same system. Many teams send approved timesheets from a scheduling app to a separate payroll provider. If someone re-enters hours or fixes mismatches every pay period, you may want a tool where approved hours, overtime and PTO flow straight into payroll. Our guide on how to process payroll shows every step a payroll tool should cover.
  • PTO tracking with real balances. If time off still runs through texts or a spreadsheet, you may want accruals, balances employees can see, and approvals that reach the paycheck. Our PTO tracker guide explains what to look for.
  • Onboarding and documents. Growing teams often want new hires to sign the offer letter and complete the W-4, I-9 and direct deposit on their phone before the first shift, with those records kept in the same place as the schedule.
  • Different location needs. Adding a second store, a commissary kitchen or crews who start at job sites raises new questions: per-location schedules, managers who see only their site, shared tablets at the door, or GPS and geofencing for field staff.
  • Industry-specific tools. A restaurant may want features tied to sales forecasts or tips. A clinic may care about certifications before scheduling. A general tool may or may not go as deep in those areas.
  • Fewer apps for employees. When staff open one app for shifts, another for pay stubs and a third for HR forms, a single employee app can cut questions to managers.
  • Budget and contract timing. A renewal is a natural point to compare. Ask every vendor, including your current one, for pricing in writing.

Write down your top two reasons before you look at any product. They decide which group of employee scheduling app alternatives you should shortlist from.

When I Work alternatives by type

Most When I Work alternatives fall into one of the groups below. These descriptions reflect each company's widely known positioning, not a test or a ranking. Most of these vendors offer several plans and keep adding features, so confirm current features, plans and pricing on each vendor's site.

Scheduling and time apps for hourly teams

The closest match to what When I Work does. Built for shift work: schedule building, swaps, a mobile time clock and timesheets. Good at: the weekly schedule and clocking in. Watch for: what happens after hours are approved. Some run payroll, others export to a payroll provider.

  • Homebase: known for scheduling and time clocks for hourly teams at local businesses.
  • Deputy: known for workforce management for hourly staff: scheduling, time and attendance.
  • Sling: known for employee scheduling and team communication, used across industries such as restaurants, retail and healthcare.

Restaurant-focused apps

Built around how restaurants run: front and back of house, busy and slow shifts, and staff who swap often. Good at: restaurant workflows. Watch for: fit if you also run non-restaurant locations.

  • 7shifts: known for scheduling and team management for restaurants; its site describes restaurant HR, scheduling, payroll and retention tools.

Mobile apps for deskless teams

Built for staff who are rarely at a desk: field crews, cleaners, security and service teams. Good at: a phone-first experience that combines schedules and time with communication. Watch for: how deep payroll and HR records go.

  • Connecteam: known as a mobile app for deskless teams covering scheduling, time tracking and team communication.

Payroll and HR suites with scheduling

Some teams leave a standalone scheduling app for a payroll or HR suite that also offers time tracking or scheduling, natively or through partner apps. Good at: keeping pay and personnel records in one account. Watch for: scheduling and time clock depth for hourly shift work, which varies a lot between products and plans.

  • Gusto: known as a payroll-first platform for small businesses, with benefits and HR tools built around payroll.
  • Rippling: known for combining HR, payroll, IT and finance tools around one employee record.

Switching checklist

Switching scheduling apps is mostly a data and timing job. Work through this list before you cancel anything.

  1. Pick the start date. Start on the first day of a pay period so no period is split across two systems. For Northfield, that would mean starting a new tool on a period start date, not mid-period.
  2. Export employee data. Names, contact details, roles, locations, pay rates and manager assignments.
  3. Export schedules. Recurring shifts, templates and any future schedules already published, so the first weeks in the new tool are not rebuilt from memory.
  4. Export timesheets. Download past timesheets and keep them somewhere you control. The Department of Labor's recordkeeping rules under the Fair Labor Standards Act require employers to keep payroll records for at least three years and time cards, the records wages are calculated from, for two years. Your state may require longer, so check your state labor department too.
  5. Record PTO balances. Note every employee's balance as of the cutover date, plus accrual rates and carryover rules, and have employees confirm their number before it is imported.
  6. Map your rules. Overtime settings, break rules, rounding, shift differentials and any state rules such as daily overtime in California. Our guide to calculating overtime helps you check the new tool's math.
  7. Set permissions. Decide who sees pay rates, who approves timesheets and which managers see which locations.
  8. Invite employees. Send logins early, set up shared tablets if you use them, and tell staff exactly which day to start clocking in on the new app.
  9. Run both systems for one pay period. Have staff clock in on the new tool while a manager keeps the old one as a check. Before payroll, compare total hours, overtime and PTO for every employee. Investigate every difference.
  10. Cut over and cancel. Once the numbers match, switch off the old tool at the start of the next period. Confirm your final exports before access ends.

Questions to ask on every demo

Bring a real week and ask the salesperson to run it live. These questions show what a product does on a busy Saturday, not on a slide.

  1. Build next week's schedule from a template. How long does it take, and what do employees see?
  2. Two staff swap Saturday shifts. What does each one tap, and what does the manager approve?
  3. An employee forgets to clock out. How does the manager fix it, and is the change recorded?
  4. Someone works 44 hours with a night shift differential. Where does the overtime appear, and does the regular rate include the differential?
  5. How do approved PTO hours reach the paycheck? Show me the pay stub line.
  6. If you run payroll, who deposits and files federal, state and local payroll taxes and W-2s? If you do not, how do timesheets get to our payroll provider?
  7. How do we add a second location, and can a manager see only their own site?
  8. Can a shift lead see schedules but not pay rates?
  9. What will you import for us, including PTO balances, and who checks it before the first pay run?
  10. If we leave, how do we export schedules, timesheets and pay history?
  11. What is the current price for our headcount, and can we have it in writing?

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to When I Work?

There is no single best alternative. The right choice depends on why you are looking. If you want payroll in the same system, compare tools that run payroll from approved hours. If you run a restaurant, look at restaurant-focused apps. If you need GPS for crews, test location features. Shortlist two or three and demo them with a real week.

Can I move my schedules and timesheets out of When I Work?

Most scheduling apps let admins export data such as timesheets and employee lists, but what you can export and in which format depends on your plan and settings. Check the vendor's help center before you cancel, export everything you may need for payroll records, and store it somewhere you control.

How long should I run two scheduling apps at the same time?

Plan on at least one full pay period. Have staff clock in on the new system while managers still check the old one, then compare total hours, overtime and PTO for every employee before you run payroll. If the numbers match, switch off the old app at the start of the next period.

Do I need a scheduling app and a separate payroll provider?

Not always. Some teams keep a scheduling app and send approved timesheets to a separate payroll provider, which works if the export is clean. Others want one system where approved hours, overtime and PTO flow into payroll so nobody re-enters numbers. Count how many hours a month the copy step costs before you decide.

What should I check before switching employee scheduling apps?

Check how the new tool handles your overtime rules, breaks, PTO accruals, multiple locations and manager permissions. Confirm what data the vendor will import, how employees get their logins, and who supports you on the first payroll. Ask for current pricing in writing, since plans and features change often.
