No call no show: what it means and how employers should handle it

An empty spot on the schedule and no message. Here is what to do in the first hour, when a missed shift becomes job abandonment, the absences the law protects, and a policy you can copy.

Quick answer

  • A no call no show is when an employee misses a scheduled shift without telling anyone beforehand or during the shift.
  • Check that the person is safe first, then document the time, every contact attempt and any reply, and apply your written policy the same way each time.
  • Many employers treat three consecutive no-shows as job abandonment. That is a common policy choice, not a federal law, and protected leave such as FMLA, ADA, jury duty and military service changes the answer.

A no call no show happens when an employee doesn’t come in for a scheduled shift and doesn’t call, text or message anyone to say so. For a small team it means an uncovered register, a short kitchen or a patient visit nobody can make. This guide covers what to do in the first hour, how to write a fair no call no show policy, when repeated absences count as job abandonment, which absences are protected by federal and state law, and how final pay and unemployment work afterward.

What is a no call no show?

The term describes two failures at once: the employee did not work the shift (no show), and they did not give notice (no call). Both parts matter. An employee who texts at 5:45 a.m. to say they are sick for a 6:00 a.m. shift is a late call-out, not a no-show. An employee who never appears and never answers is.

Most attendance policies put absences on a ladder. Being clear about where a missed shift sits keeps discipline consistent.

Your policy should define each of these in plain words, including what counts as notice (a call to the manager, a message in the app, a text to a listed number) and how far before the shift it must arrive.

Why employees no-show

Before you write the warning, it helps to know the usual causes, because several of them are fixable and some of them are protected.

  • Emergencies. A car accident, a hospital visit, a child’s emergency or a family death can make calling impossible.
  • Schedule confusion. The employee thought they were off, read last week’s schedule, or agreed a swap by text that never reached the manager.
  • Fear of the conversation. Some employees, often young or new, would rather say nothing than call a manager to say they are sick.
  • Hard-to-reach managers. If the only way to call out is a phone number nobody answers at 5:30 a.m., some people give up.
  • Quiet quitting, literally. The employee found another job and stopped coming in rather than giving notice.
  • Health or caregiving problems. Ongoing medical conditions can lead to sudden absences, and those may fall under FMLA or the ADA.

The first four causes are about process, and you can reduce them with a clear schedule, an easy way to call out and a swap system that managers can see. We come back to that at the end.

How to respond to a no call no show, step by step

The goal in the first hour is to cover the shift and find out whether the employee is safe. Discipline comes later, after you know what happened.

  1. Cover the shift. Call in someone from the on-call list or offer the open shift to qualified staff.
  2. Check on the employee’s safety. Call and text the employee. If you cannot reach them within an hour or two, call the emergency contact on file. Keep the tone concerned, not accusing. A no-show from someone who is never late is a reason to worry.
  3. Document everything. Write down the shift time, when you noticed, each call and text with the time, who you spoke with and what they said. Save screenshots. These records matter later for discipline, final pay and any unemployment claim.
  4. Talk when they return. Ask what happened and listen. Ask whether there is anything they need. If the answer involves a medical condition, pregnancy, a family member’s serious illness, jury service or military duty, stop and check the rules in the section below before you discipline.
  5. Apply your written policy. Follow the steps your policy sets out, and treat this employee the way you treated the last one. Inconsistent discipline is one of the most common facts in discrimination claims.
  6. Put the outcome in writing. Give the employee a written warning or a separation letter, ask them to sign it, and store it in their personnel file.

Avoid two shortcuts. Do not fire someone on the spot by text before you know whether they were in an emergency. And do not hold back pay for hours they already worked as a penalty. Wages earned are owed regardless of discipline.

A worked example at Northfield Coffee

Here is how the steps play out at our sample company, Northfield Coffee in Austin, Texas. Ben Ortiz is a weekend prep cook. He is scheduled Saturday, November 7, 2026, from 6:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.

  • 6:10 a.m. The manager, Marcus, sees Ben has not clocked in. He offers the shift to the kitchen team, and Sam Rivera agrees to come in at 7:00 a.m.
  • 6:15 a.m. Marcus texts Ben the check-in message from Template 2 below and calls once. No answer.
  • 8:00 a.m. Marcus calls again, then calls Ben’s emergency contact, his sister. She says Ben was in a car accident the night before and is in the hospital.
  • 8:10 a.m. Marcus logs each contact with the time in Ben’s file, tells his sister to have Ben call when he can, and marks the shift as an excused absence pending details.

This is no longer a discipline question. A hospital stay overnight can be a serious health condition under the FMLA if the employer is covered and Ben is eligible, and the FMLA regulations let notice wait until an employee is able to give it. Marcus sends the leave paperwork instead of a warning. Our FMLA leave guide explains coverage, eligibility and the notice forms.

Now change the facts. Ben’s sister says he is fine and has started another job. Ben misses Saturday, Sunday, November 8, and his next scheduled shift, Tuesday, November 10, and never responds. Under Northfield’s policy, three consecutive scheduled shifts without notice is job abandonment. On Wednesday, November 11, Marcus mails and texts the separation letter in Template 4 and processes final pay, covered below.

When a no call no show becomes job abandonment

Job abandonment means the employer treats an employee as having quit because they stopped coming to work and stopped communicating. No federal law defines it or sets a number of days. It is a policy choice, and the common practice is three consecutive scheduled shifts missed without notice. Some employers use two; some count working days rather than shifts.

A few practical points make a job abandonment rule hold up:

  • Count scheduled shifts, not calendar days. A part-timer who works only weekends could miss two weeks before they hit three calendar workdays. Counting scheduled shifts is clearer.
  • Write it down and get a signature. Put the rule in your handbook and have each employee acknowledge it. Our employee handbook template includes an attendance section and an acknowledgment form.
  • Try to reach the employee each day. Calls, texts and an email or letter, all logged. If the employee later says they were in the hospital, your efforts show you did not rush.
  • Close it out in writing. Send a letter that states the dates missed, the contact attempts and the separation date, and explains how to collect final pay and return company property.
  • Pause when leave may be involved. If you knew the employee was ill, injured, pregnant, caring for a family member, or called up for military or jury service, do not apply the rule automatically.

Whether you call the separation a resignation or a termination can affect final pay deadlines and unemployment, so pick one term and use it consistently in your policy and letters.

Protected absences and the limits of at-will employment

Most US employment is at will, meaning either side can end it at any time for any lawful reason. That gives employers room to enforce attendance rules, but not to fire someone for an absence the law protects. Montana is the main exception: after a probationary period, it generally requires good cause for discharge. These are the protections that most often come up after a missed shift.

  • FMLA. Covered employers (private employers with 50 or more employees, plus public agencies and schools) must give eligible employees job-protected leave for a serious health condition, a family member’s serious health condition and other listed reasons. For unforeseeable leave, the Department of Labor’s regulations (29 CFR 825.303) require notice “as soon as practicable.” Employees must normally follow your usual call-in procedure, and if they do not, without unusual circumstances, FMLA leave may be delayed or denied. Emergency medical treatment can postpone the duty to call until the employee is able to.
  • ADA. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) says employers with 15 or more employees must consider unpaid leave as a reasonable accommodation for an employee with a disability, and that no-fault attendance and maximum-leave rules may have to be modified unless that causes undue hardship. An employer may not penalize an employee for using leave as an accommodation. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act adds a similar accommodation duty for pregnancy-related limitations.
  • State and city paid sick leave. Many state and local sick leave laws limit how much notice you can demand for an unforeseeable absence and bar you from counting protected sick time against the employee. Texas has no statewide paid sick leave law, but California, New York, Washington and many others do. Our paid time off guide lists the state sick leave laws that shape a PTO policy.
  • Jury duty. Federal law (28 U.S.C. 1875) bars employers from firing, threatening or coercing a permanent employee because of service on a federal jury. Nearly every state protects state jury service too, and some require pay. A summons usually arrives weeks ahead, so ask the employee to bring it in.
  • Military service. Under USERRA, an employee should give advance written or verbal notice of military service, but no notice is required if it is precluded by military necessity or is otherwise impossible or unreasonable. A Guard or Reserve member who vanishes after a sudden call-up may be protected.
  • Workers’ compensation and retaliation. Firing someone for an absence caused by a workplace injury, or because they complained about pay or safety, can be unlawful retaliation under federal and state law.

The safe pattern: when the employee returns or makes contact, ask why they were absent before deciding anything, and if the reason touches one of these areas, treat it as a leave question first. If you are unsure, talk to an employment attorney before you terminate.

Final pay and unemployment after job abandonment

An employee who abandons the job still has to be paid for every hour already worked. The timing depends on your state. The Department of Labor notes that federal law does not require an immediate final paycheck, but many states set their own deadlines, and some depend on whether the employee quit or was fired.

StateFired or let goQuitSource
TexasWithin six calendar daysBy the next regular paydayTexas Payday Law, Texas Workforce Commission
CaliforniaImmediately at the time of terminationWithin 72 hours if no notice was given; on the last day with at least 72 hours’ noticeCalifornia Labor Commissioner (DLSE)
Other statesDeadlines range from immediately to the next regular paydayYour state labor department

In California, final wages include accrued vacation, and an employer that willfully pays late can owe a waiting time penalty of up to 30 days’ wages. In Texas, our Texas Payday Law summary explains paydays, deductions and how wage claims work.

Back to the example. If Northfield treats Ben’s job abandonment as a resignation, his final pay for hours worked from November 1 to November 6 goes out on the regular payday, Friday, November 20. If it treated the separation as a firing on November 11, the six-day deadline would mean paying by Tuesday, November 17. When you are not sure which label applies, paying by the earlier date is the safe choice. Whether unused PTO is paid out is a separate rule that varies by state and by your policy.

Unemployment. Each state decides who gets benefits. The Department of Labor describes unemployment insurance as being for workers who lose their jobs through no fault of their own. Job abandonment is often treated as a voluntary quit or as misconduct, both of which can disqualify a claimant, but the state looks at the facts. The Texas Workforce Commission, for example, notes that its rules do not define job abandonment and that a separation is usually treated as voluntary but can be found involuntary depending on what each side shows. If you receive a notice of claim, respond by the deadline with your dated contact log, the policy and the employee’s signed acknowledgment.

This policy explains how to report an absence and what happens when an employee misses a shift without notice. How to report an absence. If you cannot work a scheduled shift, tell your manager at least [2] hours before the shift starts by [calling (phone number) / messaging in the employee app]. If you cannot reach your manager, contact [backup name or number]. Asking a coworker to pass on a message is not notice. If an emergency prevents you from giving notice in advance, contact us as soon as you reasonably can. Definition. An absence without notice happens when you miss all or part of a scheduled shift and do not notify [Company name] under this policy before the shift ends. What we will do. When you miss a shift without notice, your manager will try to reach you and, if needed, your emergency contact, to make sure you are safe. When you return, we will ask you about the absence before deciding next steps. Consequences. Unless the absence is protected by law or excused under this policy: - First absence without notice in a rolling [12]-month period: written warning. - Second absence without notice in the same period: final written warning. - Third absence without notice in the same period: termination of employment. [Company name] may skip steps depending on the circumstances. Job abandonment. If you miss [3] consecutive scheduled shifts without notice, [Company name] will consider that you have voluntarily resigned. Before we close your employment, we will try to reach you by phone, text and [email / mail]. We will confirm the separation in writing and pay all final wages as required by law. Protected absences. This policy does not apply to absences protected by law, including leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act, reasonable accommodations for disability or pregnancy, paid sick leave under state or local law, jury duty, military service, and absences related to a workplace injury. If an absence may be protected, tell [HR contact name] at [email or phone] as soon as you can, and provide documentation when we ask for it as the law allows. Questions. Ask your manager or [HR contact name]. This policy does not change the at-will nature of employment[, except where state law provides otherwise].

How scheduling tools reduce no-shows

Most no-shows that are not emergencies come from confusion or friction. Fix the process and you remove much of the problem before discipline is needed.

  • One schedule, on every phone. When staff see the current schedule the moment it is published, “I thought I was off” goes away. Screenshots in a group text go stale fast.
  • Shift reminders. A reminder the day before or a few hours ahead helps people who work irregular hours. Use your scheduling tool, a calendar invite or a quick text from the shift lead.
  • Easy, visible swaps. Employees who can trade a shift with a qualified coworker have less reason to simply not show up. Swaps should land on the schedule, not in a side chat.
  • A call-out channel that someone reads. Give employees one place to report an absence, with a backup contact for early shifts.
  • Live view of who is in. Matching clock-ins to the schedule shows a missing person in minutes, so you can cover the shift and start the safety check right away.
  • Predictable schedules. Posting schedules earlier and avoiding last-minute changes cuts mistakes. Some cities and states have predictive scheduling laws that require advance notice, so check yours.

Frequently asked questions

Can you fire someone for one no call no show?

In most states, yes. Employment is usually at will, so a single unexplained absence can be grounds for firing if your policy says so and you apply it the same way to everyone. First confirm the absence was not protected, such as a medical emergency, FMLA leave, jury duty or military service, and document what happened.

How many no call no shows count as job abandonment?

No federal law sets a number. Many employers treat three consecutive scheduled shifts missed without notice as job abandonment, and some use two. Whatever number you choose, write it into your policy, have employees acknowledge it, try to reach the employee before acting, and send a letter confirming the separation.

Do you have to pay an employee who quit by not showing up?

Yes. Every hour the employee already worked must be paid, along with any final pay your state requires, such as earned vacation in some states. Federal law does not set a final paycheck deadline, but many states do. In Texas, an employee who quits is paid by the next regular payday; one who is fired, within six calendar days.

Can an employee get unemployment after a no call no show?

It depends on the state and the facts. States decide eligibility. Job abandonment is often treated as a voluntary quit or as misconduct, which can disqualify a claimant, but an employee who missed work because of a hospital stay or another emergency may still qualify. Answer the state's notice of claim with your dated records.

Is a no call no show the same as calling in sick late?

No. A no call no show means the employee gave no notice at all. Calling in sick after the shift started is a late call-out, which most policies treat as a lesser attendance problem. If the absence is covered by paid sick leave or FMLA, you may be limited in how you can count or discipline it.
