Two weeks notice letter: 5 free templates and how to write one

Five copyable letters for the common ways people quit, then the practical part: how to hand it in, what happens to your last paycheck and PTO, and what your employer can and cannot do once you give notice.

Quick answer

  • A two weeks notice letter needs three things: that you are resigning, your last day, and a line of thanks. Three to five sentences is plenty.
  • Tell your manager in person or by phone first, then send the letter by email the same day so you both have a dated copy.
  • No federal law requires notice. Your final paycheck timing and PTO payout depend on your state and your employer’s written policy.

A two weeks notice letter is a short written resignation that names your last day of work, usually fourteen days from the date you hand it in. It is not a legal requirement, but it is the standard way to leave a job in the United States, and it is the document your employer files to start your final paycheck, your PTO payout and your replacement’s schedule. Pick the template below that fits your situation, replace the bracketed text, and copy it. The rest of the guide covers how to write a two weeks notice from scratch, how to deliver it, and what to expect in the two weeks that follow.

Five two weeks notice letter templates

Each template is complete. Replace everything in [brackets], delete any line that does not apply, and keep a copy for yourself. Dates use the US format, for example November 25, 2026.

Template 1: Simple two weeks notice letter
[Your full name] [Your street address] [City, State ZIP] [Your phone number] [Month day, year] [Manager’s full name] [Manager’s title] [Company name] [Company street address] [City, State ZIP] Dear [Mr./Ms./Mx. Last name / First name], Please accept this letter as formal notice of my resignation from my position as [job title] at [Company name]. My last day of work will be [Month day, year], two weeks from today. Thank you for the opportunity to work here. I have appreciated [one specific thing: the training, the team, the chance to learn X]. During the next two weeks I will finish [current project / open shifts] and help hand over my responsibilities in whatever way is most useful. Please let me know if you need anything from me before I leave, including the return of [keys / uniform / laptop / badge]. Sincerely, [Your signature, if printed] [Your full name]
Template 2: Short two weeks notice email
Subject: Resignation – [Your full name], last day [Month day] Hi [Manager’s first name], As we discussed [today / this morning], I am resigning from my position as [job title]. My last day will be [Month day, year]. Thank you for everything over the past [number] [months / years]. I will do what I can to make the handoff easy, and I am happy to help train whoever takes over my [shifts / accounts / duties]. Could you confirm receipt of this email and let me know what I should return on my last day and when to expect my final paycheck? Thanks, [Your full name] [Personal phone number] [Personal email address]
Template 3: Grateful, professional resignation letter
[Month day, year] Dear [Manager’s name], I am writing to let you know that I have accepted a new position and will be resigning from my role as [job title] at [Company name]. My final day will be [Month day, year]. This was not an easy decision. In my [number] years here I have [learned a specific skill / grown from X to Y / worked with a team I respect], and much of that is thanks to your support. I am grateful for it. Over the next two weeks I will: - Finish [project or task] and document where it stands. - Write up my daily and weekly routines for whoever takes over. - Train [colleague’s name] on [task], if that works for you. I would be glad to stay in touch, and I hope our paths cross again. Thank you for everything. With appreciation, [Your full name] [Personal phone number] [Personal email address]
Template 4: Immediate resignation letter (no notice period)
[Month day, year] Dear [Manager’s name], Please accept this letter as notice of my resignation from my position as [job title] at [Company name], effective immediately. My last day of work is today, [Month day, year]. I understand that two weeks is the usual notice period, and I am sorry I cannot provide it. [Optional, one neutral sentence: A family matter requires my full attention. / My health requires that I stop work now. / I have relocated out of the area.] I will return [keys / uniform / badge / equipment] by [Month day] and will make sure my notes on [task] are available to the team. Please send my final paycheck to the address above [or deposit it as usual], and let me know if any paperwork needs my signature. Thank you for the time I spent with [Company name]. Sincerely, [Your full name] [Personal phone number] [Personal email address]
Template 5: Two weeks notice for retail, restaurant and hourly shift workers
[Month day, year] Hi [Manager’s first name], I am giving my two weeks notice as a [job title, for example barista / sales associate / line cook] at [Store or location name]. My last shift will be [Day, Month day, year], [shift time, for example 6:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.]. I am scheduled for [number] more shifts between now and then and I plan to work all of them. If you need me to pick up or swap a shift to help with coverage during that time, let me know and I will do what I can. A few housekeeping items: - I will return my [uniform shirts / apron / keys / name badge] at the end of my last shift. - I have [number] hours of [PTO / vacation] on my balance. Please let me know whether that is paid out on my final check under company policy. - My personal email is [address] and my cell is [number] if you need to reach me after I leave, including for my final pay stub and W-2. Thank you for the chance to work here. I have enjoyed the team and learned a lot. Thanks, [Your full name]

Here is Template 5 filled in with sample data from a small café, so you can see the finished length. Jordan Lee, a barista at Northfield Coffee in Austin, Texas, gives notice on Thursday, November 12, 2026: “My last shift will be Wednesday, November 25, 2026, 6:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. I am scheduled for eight more shifts between now and then and I plan to work all of them. I will return my two aprons and my key fob at the end of my last shift. I have 14 hours of PTO on my balance; please let me know whether that is paid out on my final check under company policy.” Four sentences, one request, no drama.

How to write a two weeks notice in five parts

Every good resignation letter has the same bones. Write these five parts in order and you are done in ten minutes.

  1. The date and the addressee. Put today’s date at the top and address the letter to your direct manager, not to HR or the owner, unless your manager is the owner. The date matters because it starts the notice clock and because some state final-pay rules count days from the day you gave notice.
  2. The statement that you are resigning. One plain sentence: “Please accept this letter as notice of my resignation from my position as [title].” Do not hint, soften or bury it in paragraph three. Your manager should know what the letter is from the first line.
  3. Your last day. Name the calendar date, and for shift work name the shift. “Two weeks from today” is not enough on its own, because payroll and scheduling need a date. Count fourteen days from the day you deliver the letter, then move to the nearest day you are actually scheduled to work.
  4. Thanks and a handoff offer. One or two sentences of genuine thanks and one sentence about what you will finish or who you will train. Keep it specific and short. This is the paragraph a future reference check will remember.
  5. Sign-off and contact details. Sign with your full name and add a personal phone number and email. After your last day your work email will be shut off, and your employer still needs to reach you about your final pay stub and your W-2 in January.

If you are writing by hand or on paper, add your address block and the company’s address at the top as in Template 1. For email, the subject line does that job: “Resignation – [Your name], last day [date].”

What to include in a two weeks notice letter, and what to leave out

The letter goes into your personnel file, and in a small business it may sit there for years. Write it for the version of your manager who reads it when a future employer calls.

IncludeLeave out
The word “resignation” or “notice” in the first sentenceReasons that criticize the company, your manager or coworkers
Your exact last day (and last shift for hourly work)Details of your new job, salary or employer
A line of thanks, ideally for something specificJokes, apologies that go on for a paragraph, or ultimatums
What you will finish or hand overA list of everything you ever did there
Questions about equipment return, PTO payout and final payDemands about pay or references (ask those in conversation)
Personal phone and email for after you leaveAnything you would not want read aloud in a meeting

A reason for leaving is optional. If you give one, keep it neutral and true: a new role, school, a move, family. If the reason is a problem at work, raise it in an exit interview or a separate conversation, not in the letter. If you are leaving because of unpaid wages, harassment or discrimination, keep your own records and talk to your state labor department or an attorney before you decide what to put in writing.

How to hand in two weeks notice

The order matters more than the format. Managers remember how they found out, not the font.

  1. Pick a quiet moment with your direct manager. Early in a shift or the start of the week is better than the middle of a rush or Friday at close. Ask for five minutes in private.
  2. Tell them in person, or by phone if you work remotely or on different shifts. Lead with the decision and the date: “I’ve accepted another job. My last day will be the 25th.” You do not owe a long explanation. If they ask why, give your neutral reason.
  3. Hand over or send the letter the same day. If you are in the room, give them the printed letter. Then send the email version anyway, or send only the email if the conversation was by phone. The email is your dated record.
  4. Ask three practical questions. What should I return and when? Will my unused PTO be paid out? When and how will I get my final paycheck? Write down the answers.
  5. Keep working your schedule. Show up for every scheduled shift, finish what you promised and leave your notes somewhere obvious. Skipping shifts after giving notice can turn a clean resignation into a no call no show in your file.

Tell coworkers after you have told your manager, not before. Managers who hear it secondhand tend to remember that part most.

What happens after you give notice

Once the letter is in, four things are set in motion: your final schedule, your final paycheck, your PTO balance and your benefits. Here is what to expect for each, using Jordan’s example from above. Northfield Coffee pays biweekly, with the November 15 to 28 pay period paid on Friday, December 4.

Your final schedule

Your manager will usually keep you on the posted schedule through your last day. Some employers shorten the notice period instead; see the section on being let go early. Ask on day one whether anything will change so you can plan around it.

Your final paycheck

Federal law does not set a deadline. The US Department of Labor’s Wage and Hour Division says employers are not required by federal law to give former employees their final paycheck immediately, and that some states require immediate payment. The deadline is a state rule, and it often differs depending on whether you quit or were let go.

  • Texas: the Texas Workforce Commission’s guidance says an employee who quits is paid on the next regularly scheduled payday after the resignation takes effect, while a discharged employee is paid within six calendar days. Jordan’s last shift is November 25, so the final check for November 15 to 25 arrives on the regular December 4 payday.
  • California: the Labor Commissioner (Labor Code section 202) requires an employee who gives at least 72 hours’ notice to be paid all wages, including accrued vacation, on the last day. With less than 72 hours’ notice, payment is due within 72 hours of quitting.
  • New York: the New York State Department of Labor says final wages are due by the regular payday for the pay period worked, and must be mailed if the employee asks.

Other states fall somewhere between “next regular payday” and “within a set number of days,” so check your state labor department’s page before your last day. When the check lands, compare the hours to your own record; our guide to how to read a pay stub shows where the final hours, PTO payout and deductions appear.

Your PTO balance

Whether unused PTO is paid out depends on your state and on the written policy. Some states treat earned vacation as wages that must be paid at separation. Others leave it to the policy, which may require notice or a clean last day to qualify. Our guide to paid time off rules covers payout state by state. Ask for your balance in writing when you give notice, and request it in the letter as Template 5 does.

Your benefits and paperwork

If you had employer health coverage, ask when it ends (often the last day of the month) and expect a COBRA or state continuation notice if the plan is covered. Update your mailing address with the employer so your W-2 reaches you in January, and download any pay stubs you want to keep while you still have access to the employee app or portal.

Is two weeks notice required by law?

No. There is no federal law that requires an employee to give notice before quitting, and no federal law that requires an employer to give notice before ending a job, outside specific situations such as large layoffs covered by the WARN Act. In every state except Montana, employment is presumed to be at will, which means either you or your employer can end it at any time, for any lawful reason, with or without notice. Montana’s Wrongful Discharge from Employment Act requires good cause for discharge after a probationary period, but it does not require employees to give notice.

Three things can still make notice matter:

  • An employment contract. Salaried and professional roles sometimes include a notice clause. Read it; breaking it is usually a contract issue, not a crime, but it can affect severance or bonus eligibility.
  • The handbook. Many handbooks ask for two weeks and state what happens if you do not give it, often that you will not be eligible for rehire or, where state law allows, that unused vacation will not be paid out. See how that language typically reads in our employee handbook templates.
  • Your reputation. Small industries talk. A clean two weeks notice letter and a worked-out notice period are what future references remember.

If you cannot give two weeks, give what you can and say so plainly, as in Template 4. A week of notice is better than none, and a courteous immediate resignation is better than disappearing.

Can an employer let you go before your two weeks are up?

In most states, yes. At-will employment runs in both directions, so an employer can accept your resignation effective immediately unless a contract or written policy says otherwise. Employers do this for roles with access to cash, customer lists or systems, or simply because they have coverage. It is not personal, and it is common.

What you are owed in that case:

  • Pay for every hour you worked, on your state’s final-pay deadline. Some states use the quicker “discharge” deadline when the employer ends the job early, so the check may arrive sooner than it would have.
  • Pay for the unworked notice period only if the employer chooses to pay it or a policy or contract promises it. Many employers do pay it out to keep the separation friendly, but federal law does not require it.
  • PTO payout on the same terms as any other separation in your state.

Unemployment benefits are where this gets careful. The Department of Labor describes unemployment insurance as being for people who lose work through no fault of their own, and every state disqualifies most voluntary quits unless the worker had good cause as that state defines it. When an employer ends the job before your announced last day, some states look at who made the final decision. California’s Employment Development Department, for example, says the separation becomes a discharge if the worker loses wages because of the early end, but remains a voluntary quit if the employer pays wages through the announced leaving date. Other states have their own rules. If this happens to you, apply anyway, answer the separation questions truthfully, and let the state agency decide. Benefits for a short gap between jobs can be real money.

Counteroffers: what to decide before you hand in the letter

Some managers respond to a resignation with more money, a title change or a schedule fix. Decide how you would answer before you give notice, because you will be asked on the spot.

  • Separate the reason from the raise. If you are leaving for pay alone, a counteroffer can work. If you are leaving because of the schedule, the commute, the manager or the work itself, more money rarely fixes that for long.
  • Get anything you accept in writing before you withdraw your resignation, including the new rate, the effective date and any changed hours.
  • Remember the other side. If you accepted a new job in writing, backing out has its own cost. Be honest with both employers and decide once.

If you decline, say so simply: “I appreciate it, but my decision is final, and I’d like to make these two weeks as useful as I can.” Then do that.

For employers: accepting a resignation and using the notice period

When one of your people hands you a two weeks notice letter, your job is to make the exit clean, the records complete and the handoff fast. Here is the short version.

  1. Acknowledge it in writing the same day. A short email that confirms the resignation, the last day, the return date for equipment, how final pay will be delivered and whether PTO will be paid out under your policy. Attach a copy of the resignation letter and file both in the employee’s record, alongside their other HR documents, so they are there when a reference check or an unemployment claim arrives.
  2. Decide about the notice period on day one. You can let the employee work it, pay it out and end access early, or end it immediately without pay where your state and policy allow. Each choice has a cost: coverage, goodwill, and in some states the way the separation is classified for unemployment. Pick the one you can apply consistently.
  3. Calendar the final-pay deadline for your state. It may differ from the deadline for a termination, and the clock may start from the notice date, not the last day. Confirm the PTO balance and whether it is payable, then set up the final check so it lands on time.
  4. Use the two weeks to hand off, not to hope. Have the departing employee document routines, train a coworker on the hard parts, and list open items. Post the job the same week so the gap is short; a role that pulls applicants, interviews and offers into one pipeline through hiring software cuts days off the search.
  5. Get the replacement started before day one. Send the offer, W-4, I-9 Section 1, direct deposit form and handbook acknowledgment to sign on their phone before they arrive, so the first shift is training rather than paperwork. That is what onboarding is for. Our new hire paperwork checklist lists every required form with current edition dates.
  6. Close out the record. Collect keys and equipment, remove system and app access on the last day, send any state-required separation notice, and note the separation as voluntary with the last day worked. Clean records make rehiring, references and unemployment responses simple.

One more habit worth keeping: a five-minute exit conversation. People who are leaving tell you the truth about schedules, pay and management that current employees will not, and the pattern across three or four exits often points to one fixable thing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a two weeks notice letter required by law?

No. No federal law requires an employee to give notice before quitting, and in at-will states either side can end the job at any time. Two weeks is a professional custom, not a legal rule. Check your offer letter, employment contract and handbook, because those can ask for notice and tie things like rehire eligibility to it.

Can I give two weeks notice by email?

Yes. An email is a valid written resignation for almost every job. The best approach is to tell your manager in person or by phone first, then send the email the same day so there is a dated record. Put your last day in the first sentence and keep a copy for yourself.

Do I have to say why I am leaving in a two weeks notice letter?

No. A resignation letter only needs the fact that you are resigning and your last day. A reason is optional, and a short neutral one (a new role, a move, school) is fine. Leave complaints out of the letter; it goes in your file and may be read by future reference checkers.

Can my employer make me leave the same day I give notice?

In most states, yes. At-will employment runs both ways, so an employer can accept your resignation effective immediately unless a contract or policy promises otherwise. You are owed pay for the hours you worked. Whether you are paid for the unworked notice period depends on company policy, and the early end can affect how your state treats an unemployment claim.

When will I get my last paycheck after I quit?

It depends on your state. Federal law sets no deadline, so many states pay on the next regular payday, while some set a specific number of days and California pays on your last day if you gave at least 72 hours of notice. Your pay stub will show the hours and any PTO payout, so check it against your own record.

Can I use PTO during my two weeks notice?

Usually only with approval. Most employers expect you to work the notice period and may deny vacation requests that fall inside it, though sick leave protected by state or city law still applies. Whether unused PTO is paid out when you leave depends on your state and the written policy, so ask before your last day.
