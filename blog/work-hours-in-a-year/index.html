Quick answer

  • A full-time year is 2,080 work hours: 40 hours × 52 weeks. It counts paid hours, including paid holidays and vacation.
  • The federal government converts salaries with 2,087 hours, the 28-year average published by the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM).
  • 2027 has 261 weekdays (2,088 hours) and 250 working days (2,000 hours) after the 11 federal holidays.

So, how many work hours in a year? For almost every pay calculation in the United States the answer is 2,080 hours: a 40-hour week times 52 weeks. That number is the basis for converting a salary to an hourly rate, for setting PTO accrual, and for budgeting labor. It is also slightly off from the real calendar, which has 260 to 262 weekdays depending on the year. This guide gives you the standard figure, the federal government’s 2,087-hour version, the exact count for 2027 and the rest of 2026, and a calculator for the hours a person actually works after PTO and holidays. We computed every date figure below in Python from the calendar and checked the holidays against OPM’s published list.

How many work hours in a year: the short answer

The 2,080 figure assumes a year has exactly 52 weeks. It does not: 365 days is 52 weeks plus one day, and a leap year adds a second. Those one or two extra days land on different weekdays each year, which is why some years have 261 or 262 weekdays instead of 260. Even so, 2,080 is the convention because it is simple and because it matches 26 biweekly pay periods of 80 hours each.

How many working days in 2027

2027 is not a leap year, so it has 365 days, and January 1, 2027 is a Friday. That means 52 full weeks plus one extra day, and the extra day is a Friday. So 2027 has 261 weekdays, which is 2,088 hours at eight hours a day. (2028 is a leap year that starts on a Saturday; its two extra days are Saturday and Sunday, so 2028 drops back to exactly 260 weekdays and 2,080 hours.)

Private employers are not required to close on federal holidays, but most offices use OPM’s list as the reference. OPM publishes 11 federal holidays for 2027; three fall on a weekend and are observed on the nearest weekday: Juneteenth on Friday, June 18, Independence Day on Monday, July 5, and Christmas on Friday, December 24. Remove those 11 days and 2027 has 250 working days, or 2,000 hours. We checked the observed dates on opm.gov, and our holiday pay guide lists all 11 with the 2026 dates alongside.

2,080 vs 2,087 vs 2,096: why the divisor matters

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management explains the 2,087 figure in its fact sheet on computing hourly rates of pay. A 1981 Government Accountability Office study looked at the 28-year cycle after which the calendar repeats and found that it contains 7 years with 260 workdays (2,080 hours), 17 years with 261 workdays (2,088 hours) and 4 years with 262 workdays (2,096 hours). The weighted average is (2,080 × 7 + 2,088 × 17 + 2,096 × 4) ÷ 28 = 2,087.14 hours. Before 1984 the federal divisor was 2,080; Public Law 97-253 switched it to 2,087 and Public Law 99-272 made the change permanent in 1986. Today 5 U.S.C. 5504(b) requires federal hourly rates to be the annual rate divided by 2,087, rounded to the nearest cent.

For a private employer, none of this is law. But the divisor you pick changes the hourly rate you quote, and it changes what a salaried worker effectively earns per hour in a long year. Here is an illustrative $52,000 salary converted three ways, and a $25.00 hourly rate paid for every weekday.

Neither is wrong; just use the same basis for your budget and your payroll, and say which one you used when you quote a rate.

How to convert an annual salary to hourly, and back

The conversions both lean on the same hours figure, so decide first whether you want paid hours (2,080 for full time) or the hours a role actually works.

  • Salary to hourly: hourly rate = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × 52). A $45,000 salary at 40 hours a week is $45,000 ÷ 2,080 = $21.63 an hour. At 37.5 hours it is $45,000 ÷ 1,950 = $23.08. Our salary to hourly calculator does this for any schedule and shows weekly and biweekly pay too.
  • Hourly to salary: annual pay = hourly rate × hours per week × 52. Maya Torres at Northfield Coffee earns $17.50 an hour; at 40 hours a week that is $17.50 × 2,080 = $36,400 a year. At 30 hours a week it is $17.50 × 1,560 = $27,300. The hourly to salary calculator adds monthly, biweekly and overtime figures.
  • Quick mental check: double the hourly rate and add three zeros. $20 an hour is about $40,000. That shortcut assumes 2,000 hours, so the true 2,080-hour figure is 4% higher ($41,600).

The hours figure also matters for exemption. The Department of Labor’s federal salary threshold for the executive, administrative and professional exemptions is $684 a week ($35,568 a year), which works out to $17.10 an hour over 2,080 hours. Several states set higher thresholds; see our exempt vs non-exempt guide.

PTO and holidays reduce hours worked, not hours paid

The 2,080 figure is hours paid. If the employer pays for holidays and vacation, those hours are already inside the 52 weeks and you do not subtract them when converting a salary. You do subtract them when you want to know how much work gets done, how much coverage you need, or how to set a staffing budget.

Take a full-time employee with 10 PTO days and the 11 federal holidays paid, at eight hours a day:

  1. Paid hours: 40 × 52 = 2,080.
  2. PTO: 10 days × 8 hours = 80 hours.
  3. Paid holidays: 11 days × 8 hours = 88 hours.
  4. Hours worked: 2,080 − 80 − 88 = 1,912 hours, about 92% of paid hours.

Add a week of sick leave and the worked figure drops to 1,872. That 8 to 10% gap is why a team of ten full-timers delivers roughly nine people’s worth of hours. It is also why PTO accrual is usually expressed against 2,080: 80 hours a year ÷ 2,080 = 0.0385 hours earned per hour worked. Our guide to paid time off covers accrual methods and the state payout rules, and PTO tracking in Workzenly keeps balances current from the same punches that feed payroll.

One point employers often miss: under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) there is no federal requirement to pay for time not worked. The Department of Labor states that the FLSA does not require payment for vacations, sick leave or holidays; those are matters of agreement between employer and employee, plus any state or local paid-leave law. Whatever you offer, write it down and apply it the same way for everyone.

Full time, part time and overtime

There is no single legal definition of full time, so the annual hours behind the label depend on who is asking.

  • Most employers: 40 hours a week, 2,080 a year. Some use 37.5 (1,950) or 35 (1,820).
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics: full-time workers usually work 35 or more hours a week; part-time workers fewer than 35. That is a statistical definition, not a rule for employers.
  • Affordable Care Act (IRS): a full-time employee averages at least 30 hours of service a week, or 130 a month, which is 1,560 hours a year. This matters for applicable large employers who must offer coverage.
  • Overtime (FLSA): non-exempt employees must be paid 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Working 2,080 hours in a year does not trigger overtime; working 41 hours in one week does.

Our guide to how many hours is full time walks through each definition and what it means for benefits. For the overtime side, how to calculate overtime shows the regular-rate math, and the overtime calculator gives a weekly total in seconds.

Overtime also changes the annual picture. An employee who averages 45 hours a week works 2,340 hours a year, 260 of them at time and a half. At $18.00 an hour that is 2,080 × $18.00 + 260 × $27.00 = $37,440 + $7,020 = $44,460, against $37,440 for a straight 40-hour year.

What employers should do with these numbers

The annual hours figure is only useful if everyone in the business uses the same one. A few habits keep the math honest.

  • Pick a basis and write it down. Use 2,080 for salary conversions, accrual rates and offer letters unless you have a reason to use something else. If you quote an hourly equivalent for a salaried role, say it is based on 2,080 hours.
  • Budget hourly labor on the real calendar. When a manager asks how many work hours in a year to plan for, the answer for hourly staff is the weekday count, not 2,080. 2026 and 2027 each have 261 weekdays. If your hourly staff work every weekday, budget 2,088 hours per person, not 2,080, or you will be one day short.
  • Track paid hours and worked hours separately. Payroll needs paid hours. Scheduling, coverage and overtime need worked hours. A time clock that records actual punches and codes PTO and holidays separately gives you both without a spreadsheet.
  • Watch 27-pay-period years. Biweekly payroll normally has 26 pay dates a year, but every 11 or 12 years a calendar year has 27. Salaried employees paid 1/26th of their salary each check would be overpaid that year unless you adjust; hourly employees are simply paid for the hours they worked. Check your pay calendar before January.
  • Keep the records. The FLSA requires payroll records to be kept for at least three years and time cards for at least two.

Northfield Coffee’s manager Marcus budgets kitchen staff at 2,088 hours for 2027 because the kitchen is open every weekday, and front-counter staff at scheduled hours, because their weeks vary. Both figures come from the same time clock data, so payroll and the budget always agree.

Frequently asked questions

How many work hours are in a year?

The standard answer is 2,080 hours: 40 hours a week times 52 weeks. It counts every paid hour, including paid holidays and vacation. The actual calendar has 260 to 262 weekdays, so a year of eight-hour weekdays runs 2,080 to 2,096 hours, and the federal government averages that to 2,087.

How many working days are in 2027?

2027 has 261 weekdays, because it starts on a Friday and has 365 days: 52 full weeks plus one extra Friday. At eight hours a day that is 2,088 hours. Take out the 11 federal holidays observed by OPM and you have 250 working days, or 2,000 hours.

Why does the federal government use 2,087 hours?

Because the calendar does not repeat every year. Over the 28-year cycle after which it does repeat, OPM counts 7 years with 260 workdays, 17 with 261 and 4 with 262. The weighted average is 2,087.14 hours, so 5 U.S.C. 5504 sets 2,087 as the divisor for federal hourly rates.

How many work hours are in a month?

On average, 173.33 hours: 2,080 divided by 12. Real months swing between 160 hours (20 weekdays) and 184 hours (23 weekdays). In 2027, February has 160 weekday hours while March and December have 184. For a 37.5-hour week the monthly average is 162.5 hours; for 35 hours it is 151.67.

Does 2,080 hours include PTO and holidays?

Yes, if they are paid. 2,080 is paid hours, not hours worked. A full-time employee with 10 PTO days and 11 paid holidays is paid for 2,080 hours but works 2,080 minus 80 minus 88, or 1,912 hours. Use paid hours for salary conversions and hours worked for staffing, cost and overtime planning.

How many hours a year is full time?

There is no single legal number. Most employers set full time at 40 hours a week, which is 2,080 a year. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 35 or more hours a week as full time (1,820 a year), and the IRS treats 30 hours a week, or 130 a month, as full time for Affordable Care Act purposes (1,560 a year).
