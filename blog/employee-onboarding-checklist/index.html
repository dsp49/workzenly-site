Employee onboarding checklist: every step from offer to day 90

Seven copyable checklists: five phases from the offer to the day 90 review, plus short versions for hourly shift hires and for remote or office hires, with the federal and state paperwork deadlines in the right places.

Quick answer

  • Copy the five phase checklists below (before acceptance, preboarding, first day, first week, 30/60/90 days) and keep one copy per new hire.
  • The hard deadlines: I-9 Section 1 by the first day of work, Section 2 within 3 business days, a W-4 before the first payroll, and the state new hire report within 20 days or your state's shorter deadline.
  • California and New York also require a written pay notice at hire (Labor Code 2810.5 and LS 54 for hourly staff). Give every item an owner and a date.

This employee onboarding checklist covers everything a US small business does for a new hire, from the day you make the offer to the day 90 review. It is split into five phases so each person knows what is due and when, and it includes two short versions: one for hourly shift hires at a coffee shop, store or clinic, and one for remote or office hires. Copy the phase lists into a document, a spreadsheet or your HR system, replace the [bracketed] items, and cross off what does not apply in your state. The checklist names each legal form and its deadline but does not re-explain them; follow the links to our guides on new hire paperwork and the Form I-9 for the detail.

Each block copies as plain text with checkboxes, so it pastes cleanly into Google Docs, Word or a task app. Lines marked LEGAL carry a federal or state deadline.

How to use this onboarding checklist template

An employee onboarding checklist only works if every line has a name and a date next to it. Here is the routine that keeps it moving.

  1. Start a copy when you make the offer. Fill in the header and write the due date beside every LEGAL line on the same day. Two dates drive almost everything: the first day of work for pay, which starts the three-business-day I-9 clock, and the hire date your state uses for the new hire report.
  2. Trim it for your state once. Replace the state lines with your real forms: your withholding certificate (or "none" in states without wage income tax, such as Texas and Florida), your pay notice, and your state's new hire notices. Save that as your master copy so the next hire starts from it.
  3. Push paperwork into preboarding. Everything in checklist 2 can be done on the new hire's phone before the first shift. That leaves day one for people and training, and it gives payroll time to set up withholding before the first pay run.
  4. Pick the right version. Use the full list for your first few hires or a new role. Once the routine is set, the short hourly or remote and office version is enough for repeat hires into the same job.
  5. Close it at day 90. File the finished checklist with the employee's records and note anything that went wrong, so you can fix the template.

Common onboarding checklist misses

These are the lines that get skipped most often in small businesses, and what to do instead.

  • Section 2 slips past day three. The clock starts on the first day of work for pay, not when the manager has a free afternoon. Put the due date on the checklist in preboarding and examine documents on day one when you can.
  • Asking for specific I-9 documents. The employee chooses which acceptable documents to show. Send the list of acceptable documents rather than asking for a driver's license and Social Security card.
  • The new hire report is forgotten. It is easy to miss because nothing breaks if you skip it. File it in week one, and remember that rehires usually count as new hires too.
  • No pay notice in California or New York. The notice is due at hire, not at the first paycheck. Make it part of the offer packet for every hire in those states.
  • Training hours left off the timesheet. Orientation, required training and shadow shifts are paid hours worked for non-exempt staff and count toward overtime. Check the first week's timesheet before payroll runs.
  • An offer letter that does not match payroll. If the letter says $18 and non-exempt but payroll says salaried, the first paycheck is wrong. Our offer letter template puts status and rate on the page, and our exempt vs non-exempt guide helps you decide.
  • No schedule after day one. Hourly hires who do not know when they work next often do not come back. Publish two weeks of shifts before the start date.
  • The handbook was never sent. If you do not have one yet, start with our employee handbook template and its acknowledgment form.
  • Check-ins that never happen. Book day 30, 60 and 90 during preboarding. A meeting already on the calendar is far more likely to happen.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a new hire checklist?

At minimum: a signed offer letter, Form W-4, any state withholding form, Form I-9 Sections 1 and 2, the state new hire report, any state pay notice, direct deposit, and a handbook acknowledgment. Add the practical items too: schedule, time clock access, equipment, a buddy, first-week training and check-ins at day 30, 60 and 90.

When does the I-9 have to be completed?

The employee completes Section 1 no later than the first day of work for pay, and can do it any time after accepting the offer. The employer examines documents and completes Section 2 within three business days of that first day. If the job lasts less than three days, Section 2 is due on the first day.

How many days do I have to report a new hire to the state?

Federal law sets 20 days from the hire date as the outer limit, and states can set a shorter deadline. Texas, for example, uses 20 calendar days and some states require fewer. Check your state new hire reporting site, and consider filing during the first week so the report is never late.

Can new hires complete onboarding paperwork before their first day?

Yes, once they have accepted the offer. They can sign the offer letter, complete the W-4 and state withholding form, set up direct deposit, read the handbook and fill in I-9 Section 1. You still examine their original I-9 documents and complete Section 2 within three business days of the first day of work.

How long should an onboarding checklist cover?

Plan for about 90 days. The legal deadlines all fall in the first three weeks, but training, independence and the first review take longer. Keep the checklist open until the day 90 review is done and signed, then file it with the employee's records and use what you learned to update the checklist for the next hire.
