The average hourly wage in the US was $38.00 in October 2026 for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Employment Situation released November 6, 2026. That figure is a mean, and it includes managers and high earners. The typical worker earns less: the BLS occupational survey puts the median at $24.51 an hour. This page collects the latest average hourly pay figures by industry, occupation and state, each linked to the BLS table it comes from.

National average hourly earnings

BLS measures average hourly earnings every month in the Current Employment Statistics (CES) survey of employer payrolls. The figures below are seasonally adjusted, and October 2026 is preliminary.

  • Average hourly earnings for all private employees rose 14 cents, or 0.4%, to $38.00 in October 2026. Over 12 months they rose 3.1%, from $36.85 in October 2025.
  • Production and nonsupervisory employees, the workers below supervisory level, averaged $32.75, up 12 cents in the month. (BLS Table B-8)
  • Workzenly calculation: production and nonsupervisory pay was 86.2% of the all-employee average, a gap of $5.25 an hour. Their pay rose 3.3% over the year, slightly faster than the all-employee figure.
  • Average weekly earnings for all private employees were $1,307.20 in October 2026, up from $1,260.27. Workzenly calculation: a 3.7% rise, faster than hourly pay because the average workweek also lengthened. (BLS Table B-3)
  • For production and nonsupervisory employees, average weekly earnings were $1,106.95, up from $1,068.29.

BLS will publish November 2026 data on December 4, 2026, and will revise October then.

What this means for employers: A 3% annual rise in average pay is the baseline your hourly staff compare offers against. If your rates have not moved in a year, they have fallen behind the market. To compare an hourly rate with a salaried offer, use our hourly to salary calculator.

Mean vs median hourly pay

Different BLS surveys answer different questions, so "the average wage" can mean several numbers. The CES average divides total payroll by total hours. The Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) survey reports both a mean and a median by job. The Current Population Survey (CPS) asks households about usual weekly pay.

  • OEWS covered 155,495,730 jobs in May 2025, with a mean annual wage of $69,770. (OEWS Table 1)
  • Workzenly calculation: the OEWS mean was $9.03 above the median, or about 37% higher. A small number of very high earners lifts the mean.
  • BLS notes that eight of the 10 largest occupations had below-average wages in May 2025, with annual wages ranging from $32,150 for fast food and counter workers to $46,590 for customer service representatives. (OEWS news release)
  • The CES average ($38.00) runs higher than the OEWS mean ($33.54) partly because CES covers only the private sector and is 17 months more recent. The two surveys use different samples and methods, so they will not match.

What this means for employers: When you benchmark pay, compare against the median for the specific job and area, not the national average. The $38.00 headline would overstate the market rate for most hourly roles.

Average hourly pay by occupation

The OEWS survey gives wages by job. These are mean hourly wages for major occupational groups in May 2025, from OEWS Table 1, released May 15, 2026.

  • Food preparation and serving was the lowest-paid group, with a mean of $17.86 and a median of $16.85 across 13,680,750 jobs.
  • Office and administrative support was the largest group, 17,753,430 jobs, with a median of $22.81.
  • Healthcare support (median $18.43) and personal care and service (median $17.51) sit near the bottom, while healthcare practitioners earned a median of $41.60.
  • Construction and extraction workers had a median of $28.63; transportation and material moving, $21.32.

What this means for employers: Most hourly roles in food service, retail, care and warehousing pay well under the national average. Benchmark each role against its own occupation, and turn hourly offers into take-home figures with the hourly paycheck calculator before you post a job.

Wage growth vs inflation

Average hourly earnings for all private employees have risen every year since 2019. These are October values, seasonally adjusted, from BLS series CES0500000003.

  • Workzenly calculation: average hourly earnings rose $9.76, or 34.6%, from October 2019 to October 2026.
  • Year-over-year growth peaked at 5.4% in October 2021 and has slowed each year since: 4.0% in 2024, 3.9% in 2025 and 3.1% in 2026 (Workzenly calculations).
  • Real average hourly earnings, adjusted with the CPI-U, were unchanged from September to October 2026, as a 0.4% rise in pay matched a 0.4% rise in prices. (BLS Real Earnings, Nov 10, 2026)
  • Over a full year, real average hourly earnings fell 0.5% from September 2025 to September 2026 (preliminary) and 0.3% from August 2025 to August 2026. In 1982 to 1984 dollars, they were $11.26 in October 2026. (Real Earnings Table A-1)
  • BLS cannot publish an October 2025 to October 2026 real earnings or CPI comparison, because it could not collect October 2025 CPI data during the 2025 lapse in appropriations.
  • In household data, median weekly earnings of full-time workers rose 1.5% from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, against a 3.5% rise in the CPI-U. (BLS Usual Weekly Earnings)

What this means for employers: Nominal raises near 3% have not kept pace with prices over the past year, so many hourly employees feel a pay cut even after a raise. Be clear about what a raise is worth in take-home pay; our guide to gross pay vs net pay explains the difference.

Pay gaps by sex, race and education

The CPS reports median usual weekly earnings of full-time wage and salary workers. These figures are for the third quarter of 2026, not seasonally adjusted, from the Usual Weekly Earnings release of October 21, 2026.

  • 122.2 million full-time wage and salary workers had median weekly earnings of $1,232.
  • Women's median was $1,127, or 83.4% of the $1,352 median for men.
  • By race and ethnicity: Asian $1,633, White $1,261, Black $1,012 and Hispanic $981.
  • Among workers age 25 and over: $819 without a high school diploma, $993 for high school graduates with no college, and $1,752 with at least a bachelor's degree. Workzenly calculation: degree holders earned 1.76 times the median of high school graduates.

What this means for employers: Gaps in medians reflect differences in jobs and hours as well as pay for the same work. The practical step is to set a written pay range for each role and apply it the same way to everyone, which also prepares you for pay transparency laws in several states.

About this data

These average hourly wage statistics come from four BLS programs. The Current Employment Statistics survey is published monthly; the latest two months are preliminary and are revised. State CES data arrive a few weeks after the national data. Real Earnings is monthly, with the next release on December 10, 2026. OEWS is annual; May 2025 is the latest. Usual Weekly Earnings is quarterly.

CES averages come from employer payrolls and cover private-sector employees. OEWS covers wage and salary jobs by occupation, including government. CPS earnings come from household interviews and are weekly. Where we compute shares, changes or ratios, the text says "Workzenly calculation."

Frequently asked questions

What is the average hourly wage in the US?

Average hourly earnings for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls were $38.00 in October 2026 (preliminary, seasonally adjusted), up 3.1% from a year earlier, according to the BLS Employment Situation released November 6, 2026. Production and nonsupervisory employees averaged $32.75.

What is the median hourly wage in the US?

The median hourly wage for all occupations was $24.51 in May 2025, according to the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey released May 15, 2026. Half of workers earned more and half earned less. The mean in the same survey was $33.54, pulled up by high earners.

Which industry has the highest average hourly pay?

Utilities had the highest average hourly earnings in October 2026 at $56.33, followed by information at $55.70 and financial activities at $49.74, according to BLS Table B-3. Leisure and hospitality was lowest at $23.90, and retail trade averaged $26.55.

Which state has the highest average hourly wage?

Among states, Washington had the highest average hourly earnings for private employees in September 2026 at $43.80, then Massachusetts at $43.75 and California at $42.58. The District of Columbia was higher still at $57.86. Mississippi was lowest at $29.36. These are preliminary, not seasonally adjusted BLS figures.

Are wages keeping up with inflation in 2026?

Not quite. BLS real earnings data show real average hourly earnings fell 0.5% from September 2025 to September 2026 (preliminary). BLS cannot publish the October comparison because October 2025 CPI data were not collected during the 2025 lapse in appropriations.

What is the average hourly wage for full-time workers?

BLS reports full-time pay weekly, not hourly. Median usual weekly earnings of full-time wage and salary workers were $1,232 in the third quarter of 2026, 1.5% higher than a year earlier, according to the BLS Usual Weekly Earnings release of October 21, 2026.
