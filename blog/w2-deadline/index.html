Quick answer

  • When are W2s sent out? Employers must furnish 2026 Forms W-2 to employees and file them with the Social Security Administration by Monday, February 1, 2027. The usual January 31 date falls on a Sunday, so the IRS instructions move it to the next business day.
  • The 2026 W-2 has new box 12 codes: TT for qualified overtime (the half portion of time-and-a-half) and TP for cash tips reported to the employer, plus box 14b for the tipped occupation code.
  • If your W-2 has not arrived, ask your employer first. If you still have nothing by the end of February, the IRS says to call 800-829-1040; Form 4852 lets you file with estimated figures.

When are W2s sent out? For wages paid in 2026, your employer must get your Form W-2 to you by February 1, 2027. That is the W-2 due date in the IRS General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (2026): January 31 is the statutory date, but January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so both the employee copies and the Copy A filed with the Social Security Administration (SSA) are due the next business day. Most employers mail or post W-2s earlier in January, as soon as the last 2026 payroll closes and the totals are reconciled. This guide covers the deadline from both sides: what employees should expect and do, and what employers must file, when, and what it costs to be late.

The W-2 deadline 2027: when do employers have to send W2s?

Three things are due on the same day, Monday, February 1, 2027:

  • Furnish Copies B, C and 2 to each employee. The IRS treats the form as furnished on time if it is properly addressed and mailed on or before the due date, or posted electronically by then for employees who consented (more on that below).
  • File Copy A with the SSA, on paper or electronically. Electronic filers get no extra time; the e-file and paper deadlines are the same.
  • File Form W-3, the transmittal that totals all your W-2s, with the paper Copy A. If you e-file through the SSA, the W-3 is generated for you.

Two things that are not true, despite being repeated every January:

  • There is no automatic extension. Form 8809 asks for one 30-day extension to file with the SSA, but the IRS grants it only in extraordinary circumstances or catastrophes, and the request must be in before the due date. It does not extend the date for giving employees their copies; that needs a separate request on Form 15397, usually for no more than 15 days.
  • "Sent" means furnished by the deadline, not received. A W-2 mailed on February 1 that arrives February 4 is on time. One mailed on February 3 is late.

State copies are separate. Texas, where our sample employer Northfield Coffee runs its Austin cafe, has no state income tax and no state W-2 filing. Many states with an income tax want an annual reconciliation or W-2 data, often by January 31, on their own schedule. Check your state revenue department.

Looking ahead: 2027 W-2s (filed in early 2028) go back to the normal date, because January 31, 2028 is a Monday.

Who gets a W-2, and who gets a 1099-NEC instead

You must file a Form W-2 for every employee from whose wages you withheld federal income, Social Security or Medicare tax during 2026, and for every employee you paid $2,000 or more even if nothing was withheld. The 2026 instructions raised that no-withholding threshold from $600 to $2,000 under the 2025 tax law. The amount of work does not matter: a high-school student who worked three Saturdays in December and had a few dollars of Social Security tax withheld gets a W-2.

Independent contractors do not get a W-2. They get Form 1099-NEC if you paid them $2,000 or more for services in 2026, and the 1099-NEC has the same deadline: furnish to the contractor and file with the IRS by February 1, 2027, paper or electronic. If you are not sure which side of the line a worker sits on, read our guide to 1099 vs W-2 workers before January; reclassifying someone in the last week of the month is painful.

Former employees who left mid-year get a W-2 on the same schedule as current staff, unless they ask for it early (see corrected W-2s and former employees).

What the W-2 shows, box by box

The W-2 form 2026 is one page with six copies: Copy A goes to the SSA, Copy 1 to the state or city, Copy B is for the employee's federal return, Copy C is the employee's record, Copy 2 is for the employee's state return and Copy D stays with the employer. Here is what the numbered boxes report. For how the same numbers show up on a regular paycheck, see how to read a pay stub.

2Federal income tax withheld, driven by the employee's Form W-4.

What is new on the 2026 W-2: box 12 codes TT and TP

The 2025 tax law, Public Law 119-21, created temporary deductions for qualified overtime pay and qualified tips for tax years 2025 through 2028. Employees claim them on their own returns, but they need the figures from the employer, so the IRS added three box 12 codes and a new box to the 2026 form:

  • Code TT, total qualified overtime compensation. Only the premium counts: for time-and-a-half, that is the extra "half", not the whole overtime check. The employee can deduct up to $12,500 ($25,000 married filing jointly), but the employer reports the full qualified amount even if it is higher. The 2026 Form W-4 already lets employees reduce withholding for this deduction; our W-4 guide covers that side.
  • Code TP, total cash tips reported to the employer. The deduction is capped at $25,000 for the employee. Whenever you use code TP you must also enter the worker's Treasury tipped occupation code in box 14b, up to two codes, and "000" if any tips came from a non-qualifying occupation. The code list is at IRS.gov/TippedOccupations.
  • Code TA, Trump account contributions. From July 4, 2026, an employer may contribute up to $2,500 a year to an employee's or dependent's Trump account under a section 128 program, excluded from the employee's income.

A worked example of code TT. Emily Carter works in the kitchen at Northfield Coffee at $19.00 an hour. In the pay period of November 1 to 14 she worked 72 regular hours plus 6 overtime hours. Her overtime rate is $28.50, so her overtime pay is 6 × $28.50 = $171.00. The premium portion is 6 × $9.50 = $57.00, and $57.00 is what that pay period adds to her code TT total. If she averaged 6 overtime hours every two weeks for the whole year, her 2026 code TT amount would be about 26 × $57.00 = $1,482.00 (illustrative). The point for employers: your payroll system needs to track the premium half of every overtime hour all year, not just total overtime pay. Our how to calculate overtime guide shows how the regular rate changes when bonuses or shift differentials are involved, which changes the premium too.

Electronic W-2 delivery: the consent rules

Posting W-2s in an employee app or payroll portal is allowed and common, but it is not automatic. Treasury regulation 31.6051-1(j), summarized in IRS Publication 15-A, sets the conditions:

  • Affirmative consent, in a way that proves access. The employee must consent electronically in a manner that reasonably demonstrates they can open the W-2 in the format you will use (for example, by consenting inside the same portal). Paper consent counts only if the employee confirms it electronically.
  • A disclosure statement first. Before or when they consent, employees get a clear statement covering: that they will receive a paper W-2 if they do not consent; the scope and duration of the consent; how to get a paper copy and whether asking for one withdraws consent; how to withdraw consent and how you confirm it; when electronic delivery stops (for example, at termination); how to update their contact details; and the hardware and software needed.
  • Notice when it is posted. You must tell the employee the form is available, by mail, email or in person, with instructions for accessing and printing it. The notice must contain the phrase "IMPORTANT TAX RETURN DOCUMENT AVAILABLE", and if sent by email that phrase must be the subject line. If the email bounces and you cannot find a good address, send the notice by mail or in person within 30 days.
  • Keep it available. A W-2 posted on a website must stay accessible through October 15 of the following year (October 15, 2027 for 2026 forms).
  • No consent, no e-delivery. Employees who never consented, or who withdrew consent, get paper. If someone withdraws consent after the deadline, a paper copy sent within 30 days of the withdrawal is still timely.
  • Same deadline. Electronic W-2s are due February 1, 2027, like paper ones. A corrected W-2c for an electronically furnished W-2 generally must also be furnished electronically.

For small employers, the practical move is to collect consent during new hire paperwork, alongside the W-4 and I-9, and keep a record of who declined.

What to do if you have not received your W-2

Most people asking when are W2s sent out are really asking because theirs has not arrived. If February 1, 2027 passes and you have no W-2, work through these steps, which follow IRS Tax Topic 154:

  1. Check where it might be. Log in to the payroll app or portal your employer uses; many W-2s are posted there. Confirm your employer has your current mailing address, especially if you moved or left the job during 2026.
  2. Ask the employer or former employer. The payroll contact or owner can reissue a copy or confirm the mailing date. This fixes most missing W-2s within days.
  3. Call the IRS if it is still missing at the end of February. The IRS says to call 800-829-1040 with your name, address, birthdate and Social Security number, and the employer's name, address and EIN if you have it (the EIN is on last year's W-2 or your pay stub). The IRS contacts the employer and mails you Form 4852.
  4. File with Form 4852 if needed. Form 4852, Substitute for Form W-2, lets you report estimated wages and withholding, usually from your final 2026 pay stub, so you can file by the April deadline. If you prefer to wait, file Form 4868 for an extension of time to file (not to pay).
  5. Fix it later if the real W-2 differs. If the W-2 arrives after you file and the numbers are different from your estimate, the IRS says you must file Form 1040-X, an amended return.

The IRS also posts W-2 data to your online Wage and Income transcript once the SSA has processed the employer's filing, but that usually happens well into the filing season, so treat it as a backup rather than a first stop.

Corrected W-2s (W-2c) and former employees

Corrections. A wrong SSN, a misspelled name, wages posted to the wrong box or a missed code TT amount is fixed with Form W-2c, Corrected Wage and Tax Statement, and a Form W-3c transmittal. There is no fixed deadline for a W-2c; the IRS instructions say to file it and give the employee a copy as soon as possible after you discover the error. If the original W-2 had to be e-filed, the W-2c must be e-filed too. If you catch the error before you file Copy A with the SSA, you can simply mark the employee's copy "Void" and issue a fresh W-2 instead; a W-2c is only needed once the SSA has your original. Two details worth knowing: an error in only the employee's address does not require a W-2c (you can hand the employee a corrected copy or a new one marked "Reissued Statement"), and the current Form W-2c is dated January 2026.

Former employees. Anyone who worked for you in 2026 and left is owed a W-2 by February 1, 2027, like everyone else. The exception runs the other way: if a terminated employee asks for their W-2 early and you do not expect to rehire them that year, you must furnish it within 30 days of the request or 30 days of the final wage payment, whichever is later. So a barista who quit in June and asked for a W-2 in July should have had it by August, not January. Collect a forwarding address and an electronic-delivery decision as part of the exit process. For employees you cannot reach, keep the undeliverable copies for four years (or be able to reproduce them electronically through April 15 of the fourth year); never send them to the SSA.

Employer penalties for late or wrong W-2s

The IRS charges two separate penalties, one for filing late or incorrectly with the SSA (Internal Revenue Code section 6721) and one for failing to furnish correct copies to employees by February 1, 2027 (section 6722), and they stack. The per-form amounts below come from the 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 and the IRS information return penalties page for returns due in 2027. A "small business" is one with average annual gross receipts of $5 million or less for the three most recent tax years.

When you file or furnishPenalty per W-2Annual maximum, small businessAnnual maximum, other
Within 30 days after the due date (by March 3, 2027)$60$244,500$698,500

Because the filing and furnishing penalties are separate, one W-2 that is a month late to both the SSA and the employee costs $60 + $60 = $120. For Northfield Coffee's eight hourly staff, a full month's delay on everything is 8 × $120 = $960 (illustrative). Filing on paper when you are required to e-file is treated as a failure to file unless you have an approved waiver (Form 8508). Penalties can be waived for reasonable cause, but not for a forgotten calendar.

The e-file threshold. If you file 10 or more information returns of any kind in a calendar year, counting W-2s, 1099-NECs, 1099-MISCs and the rest together, you must e-file all of them. Corrected returns do not count toward the 10. A cafe with eight W-2s and three 1099-NECs is at 11 and must e-file both sets. The SSA's Business Services Online is free: W-2 Online lets you key in up to 50 W-2s per report, print the employee copies as PDFs and submits the W-3 for you; larger employers upload a wage file. You need a Login.gov or ID.me credential, and new users must request an activation code by mail, so register in December, not on January 29.

The W-2 due date is one of several that land in the first week of February. These are the federal filing dates for 2027 from the IRS employment tax due dates page and the Form 941 instructions, with the weekend rule applied (a due date on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next business day). One check that saves a W-2c later: the wage and tax totals on your four 2026 Forms 941 should equal the totals on your Form W-3. The SSA and IRS compare them, and a mismatch triggers a notice.

How employers get W-2s out on time

January is short. Most of the W-2 work can be done in December, and the rest is a matter of running the steps in order. Here is the sequence we recommend for a small employer; it slots into the year-end step of how to process payroll.

  1. December 1 to 15: verify employee data. Names and Social Security numbers must match the Social Security card; the SSA's free Social Security Number Verification Service (SSNVS) in Business Services Online checks them. Confirm mailing addresses, especially for people who left during 2026, and record each employee's electronic-delivery consent or paper preference.
  2. December 15 to 31: record every taxable item. Year-end bonuses, taxable fringe benefits (personal use of a company vehicle, group-term life over $50,000), third-party sick pay, and the box 12 amounts: 401(k) deferrals, health coverage cost for code DD, HSA contributions, and the new code TT overtime premium and code TP tips with occupation codes. Anything paid in 2026 belongs on the 2026 W-2 even if the work was done in December 2025; anything paid on January 2, 2027 does not.
  3. By the last 2026 payroll: reconcile. Total wages, federal withholding, Social Security and Medicare on your payroll register should match the sum of the four 941s, and will become the W-3 totals. Fix discrepancies now, before any W-2 is printed.
  4. Early January: generate, review, furnish. Spot-check a few W-2s against the register, including one tipped employee and one with overtime. Then post electronically (with the required notice) or mail, and keep proof of the mailing date.
  5. By February 1, 2027: file. E-file Copy A and the W-3 with the SSA, file your 1099-NECs with the IRS, and file Forms 941 (Q4) and 940. Keep Copy D and your transmittal confirmations with your employment tax records for at least four years.
  6. February onward: handle the stragglers. Reissue copies on request, mark them "Reissued Statement", and file W-2c forms as soon as you find an error.

All of this is simpler when the time clock, overtime calculation, tip records and pay runs live in one system, because the W-2 is just the sum of the year's pay stubs. Frequently asked questions

When are W2s sent out for 2026?

Employers must furnish 2026 Forms W-2 to employees by February 1, 2027, the same date they must file Copy A with the Social Security Administration. January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday, so the IRS instructions move the due date to Monday. Many employers send W-2s earlier in January once the final 2026 payroll has run.

Is the W-2 deadline January 31 or February 1 in 2027?

February 1, 2027. The law sets the W-2 due date at January 31, but when that date lands on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday the deadline moves to the next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 list February 1, 2027 for both furnishing and filing.

What should I do if my employer sends my W-2 late?

Ask your employer or former employer first, and confirm they have your current address and whether the form was posted online. If you still have nothing by the end of February, the IRS says to call 800-829-1040 with your details and the employer's name, address and EIN. The IRS contacts the employer and sends you Form 4852 so you can file using estimated figures.

Can my employer give me my W-2 only online?

Only if you agreed to it. Under Treasury regulation 31.6051-1(j), an employer may furnish the W-2 electronically only to employees who affirmatively consented after receiving a disclosure statement. Anyone who did not consent, or who withdrew consent, must get a paper copy. Electronic delivery has the same February 1, 2027 deadline as paper.

What are box 12 codes TT and TP on the 2026 W-2?

They are new for 2026 forms under the 2025 tax law. Code TT reports total qualified overtime compensation, meaning only the half portion of time-and-a-half pay. Code TP reports total cash tips reported to the employer, with the worker's Treasury tipped occupation code in new box 14b. Employees use these amounts to claim the overtime and tips deductions on their returns.

Do employers have to e-file W-2s?

Most do. If you file 10 or more information returns of any type in a calendar year, counting W-2s together with 1099s and similar forms, the IRS requires you to e-file all of them. The SSA's Business Services Online lets employers key in up to 50 W-2s per report for free and generates the W-3 automatically.
