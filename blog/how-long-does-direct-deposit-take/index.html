Quick answer

  • How long does direct deposit take? Until payday. Since September 18, 2026, Nacha rules require your bank to make a standard ACH credit available by 9:00 a.m. local time on the settlement date, and most banks post it overnight.
  • The money is in motion before payday: the employer or payroll provider sends the ACH file one to two banking days early. Some banks post it as soon as the file arrives, which is why coworkers get paid on different days.
  • The first direct deposit at a new job can take one or two pay cycles because of the account test (prenote). Weekends, Federal Reserve holidays, a wrong account number and a late payroll approval are the other common delays.

How long does direct deposit take? For a regular paycheck, the honest answer is “until payday, and often a little before.” Your employer submits the payroll file one or two banking days ahead, the ACH network settles it early on the morning of the pay date, and your bank posts the credit either when it receives the file or on the settlement date itself. If you are starting a new job, add one pay cycle for your bank details to be verified. The rest of this guide explains each step, so you know when direct deposit hits, what time, and what to do when it does not.

How long does direct deposit take? The short answer

Three timelines cover most cases.

  • Regular payday: the deposit is available on payday, by 9:00 a.m. your bank’s local time for a standard ACH credit. Many banks post it between midnight and early morning, and some post it one or two days early.
  • First paycheck at a new job: one pay cycle is normal, two is not unusual. If the employer sends a prenote to confirm your account, Nacha’s rules require a three-banking-day wait before the first live payment, and most payroll teams close the pay run several days before payday.
  • Off-cycle or corrected payment: next banking day if the employer sends a standard ACH credit, or the same day if it uses Same Day ACH before the afternoon cut-off, which not every employer or payroll provider offers.

These are network rules and common bank practice. Your pay date itself is set by your employer’s pay schedule and, where it applies, your state’s payday law; see our 2027 payroll calendar for the schedule side.

How a payroll direct deposit actually moves

A direct deposit payroll payment is an ACH credit. ACH stands for Automated Clearing House, the batch network that moves money between US bank accounts. Nacha writes the operating rules, and two operators, the Federal Reserve (FedACH) and The Clearing House, process the files. Nacha’s own fact sheet says about 93% of American workers are paid through the ACH network.

Here is the path for one paycheck, using Northfield Coffee in Austin, Texas, which pays biweekly on Fridays. The November 1–14 pay period is paid on Friday, November 20, 2026.

  1. Payroll is approved. Marcus, the manager, approves timesheets on Monday, November 16, and the payroll run is finalized on Tuesday, November 17. Our guide to how to process payroll walks through the steps before this point.
  2. The file goes to the bank. On Wednesday, November 18, the payroll provider sends a batch file to its bank (the originating bank, or ODFI in Nacha terms). Every payment in the file carries the same settlement date: Friday, November 20.
  3. The operator sorts and distributes. The ACH operator sorts the payments by receiving bank and distributes them. Barista Jordan Lee’s bank may receive the file on Wednesday evening or Thursday. The money has not moved yet; the bank just knows it is coming.
  4. Settlement. Early on Friday, November 20, the Federal Reserve settles the batch: Northfield’s account is debited and each receiving bank is credited. Nacha’s fact sheet notes the network settles several times each banking day, and never on weekends or federal holidays because the Fed’s National Settlement Service is closed.
  5. Funds are available. Jordan’s bank must make the credit available for withdrawal by 9:00 a.m. its local time on November 20. Most banks post it well before that.

Two things follow. The employer’s money leaves on payday, not before. And the receiving bank knows the amount a day or two before settlement, which is what makes early deposit possible.

What time does direct deposit hit?

What time direct deposit goes through depends on your bank, within two network rules.

  • Standard ACH credit: available by 9:00 a.m. in your bank’s local time on the settlement date. Nacha’s funds availability rule for non-Same Day credits took effect on September 18, 2026 and removed an older condition that applied the 9:00 a.m. deadline only to files received by 5:00 p.m. the prior day, so the deadline now covers every standard payroll credit.
  • Same Day ACH credit: Nacha runs three Same Day processing windows each banking day; the last has a submission deadline of 4:45 p.m. ET and settles at 6:00 p.m. ET. Same Day payments are capped at $1 million each (rising to $10 million on September 17, 2027, per Nacha). Credits in the later windows must be available by the end of the settlement day, so a Same Day correction can land in the late afternoon or evening.

Most large banks post standard payroll credits in an overnight batch; credit unions and smaller banks may post in a morning batch closer to 9:00 a.m. If the deposit is not there by 9:00 a.m. on the pay date, the problem is almost always upstream: the file went late or the account details are wrong.

Why some banks post direct deposit early

“Direct deposit early” is a bank feature, not something the employer controls. When a bank receives the payroll file a day or two before the settlement date, it already knows the exact amount and date. Some banks choose to credit the account right away and carry the risk until settlement. Others wait for the money to actually arrive. Both approaches follow the rules; Nacha sets a latest time for availability, not an earliest.

That is why Jordan at an online bank may see the November 20 paycheck on Wednesday night, while Maya Torres at the front counter, who banks with a local credit union, sees hers at 6 a.m. Friday. Same employer, same file, same settlement date. Three points:

  • Early posting depends on when the employer’s file is sent. If payroll is approved the day before payday, even an “early pay” bank has nothing to post early.
  • Early deposit does not change the pay date on your pay stub or your W-2. The employer’s records use the scheduled settlement date.
  • Banks can change or limit early posting. Read your bank’s terms, and never schedule a bill payment around an early deposit that is not guaranteed.

What delays a direct deposit

When direct deposit is late, one of these is almost always the cause.

1. Weekends and Federal Reserve holidays

The ACH network settles only on banking days. Nothing settles on Saturdays, Sundays or days the Federal Reserve Banks are closed. The Federal Reserve’s 2027 holiday schedule, published on federalreserve.gov, lists these dates:

Federal employees observe Juneteenth on Friday, June 18, 2027 and Christmas on Friday, December 24, 2027, but the Federal Reserve Banks are open on both Fridays, so ACH settles normally. The Sunday holiday works the other way: July 4 is observed on Monday, July 5, and the Fed is closed. Columbus Day and Veterans Day catch people out because many businesses are open while the Fed is not. Our list of federal holidays in 2027 has the observed dates for employers, and the holiday pay guide covers how these dates affect pay, as opposed to deposit timing.

2. A wrong routing or account number

A mistyped digit sends the payment to an account that does not exist, or worse, to one that does. If the account is closed or invalid, the receiving bank returns the credit, usually within two banking days, and payroll reissues it when the money comes back. If the number matches someone else’s open account, the employer’s bank has to request the funds back, which can take weeks. Always copy the numbers from a voided check or the bank’s own direct deposit letter, never from a debit card.

3. New hire prenote

Many payroll providers send a prenotification, a zero-dollar ACH entry that confirms the routing and account numbers before any money moves. Prenotes are optional under Nacha’s rules, but once sent, the first live payment must wait three banking days. Dana Brooks, hired at Northfield on Monday, November 2, 2026, added her account in the app that day; a prenote on Tuesday, November 3 cleared by Friday, November 6, well ahead of the November 17 payroll approval, so her first paycheck on November 20 went straight to her bank. Had she joined on November 16, that first check would likely have been paper.

4. Payroll approved late

If the employer or its payroll provider misses the submission cut-off, the file arrives a day late and so does the money, unless the employer pays a Same Day ACH fee. Payroll providers generally ask for approval two to four banking days before the pay date. Check your provider’s cut-off and set a reminder on the payroll calendar.

5. Bank holds and account problems

Rarely, the receiving bank holds a deposit because the account is new, frozen or overdrawn. If everyone else was paid and you were not, call your bank before you call payroll.

If payday falls on a weekend or holiday

Because the ACH network does not settle on weekends or Federal Reserve holidays, a pay date on one of those days has to move. Most employers move it earlier so that wages are not late. Some state payday laws require wages to be paid on or before the regular payday, so moving it later is risky; check your state labor department’s rule before you do. Here is the usual practice:

The January 1, 2027 case hits every employer on a biweekly Friday schedule that includes Friday, November 20, 2026, Northfield Coffee included. Its December 18 payday is followed by Friday, January 1, a Federal Reserve holiday, so Northfield pays on Thursday, December 31, 2026 and submits the file by Tuesday, December 29. One side effect: wages paid on December 31 count as 2026 wages for Form W-2 purposes, giving Northfield 27 paydays in 2026, so benefit deductions and any salaried amounts set “per paycheck” need a second look. Plan these dates at the start of the year, tell employees in writing, and pay a day early rather than a day late.

How to set up direct deposit

Setting up direct deposit payroll takes an employee five minutes and the employer one pay cycle. The steps:

  1. Get your bank details. You need the bank’s nine-digit routing number and your account number, plus whether it is a checking or savings account. Take them from a voided check, your bank’s app (most have a “direct deposit” page that shows exactly these numbers) or a direct deposit letter from the bank. Debit card numbers are not account numbers.
  2. Complete the direct deposit authorization. Employers use a direct deposit form, on paper or in an onboarding app. It authorizes the employer to credit your account and to reverse an erroneous credit. In Workzenly, new hires enter it on their phone during onboarding, alongside the W-4 and I-9 covered in our new hire paperwork guide.
  3. Attach a voided check if asked. Write VOID across a blank check from the account. If you do not have checks, the bank’s direct deposit letter does the same job.
  4. Choose a split if you want one. Most payroll systems let you send a fixed dollar amount or a percentage to a second account, for example $200 of each paycheck to savings and the rest to checking. Each account goes through the same prenote and the same timing.
  5. Watch for the test. You may see a $0.00 entry from your employer or its payroll provider a few days after you enroll. That is the prenote. If you see nothing by the first payday, ask payroll whether the account was set up in time.

For employers, collect direct deposit details before day one and enroll them at least a week before the payroll approval date. A mid-year bank change follows the same path: the new account needs its own prenote, so a change made close to payday usually means one more payment to the old account or by check.

Can an employer require direct deposit?

Sometimes, within limits, and the limits come from both federal and state law. This section is general information, not legal advice.

Federal rule. Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.10(e)(2)), which implements the Electronic Fund Transfer Act, says an employer may not require an employee to receive wages by direct deposit at a particular institution. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s official interpretation adds that an employer may require direct deposit if employees can choose the receiving bank, or may offer a designated bank alongside another method such as a check or cash.

State rules vary. Two verified examples show how far apart they are:

  • New York requires the employee’s advance written consent. Labor Law section 192 says no employer may deposit an employee’s net wages in a bank without it, with an exception for executive, administrative and professional employees earning more than $1,300 a week. New York Department of Labor regulations also require written notice of payment options, and consent cannot be a condition of hiring or continued employment.
  • Texas lets an employer require direct deposit for employees who already maintain a qualifying bank account, if it gives written notice at least 60 days before the system starts (Texas Labor Code section 61.017). The statute does not clearly let an employer force someone without an account to open one, and the Texas Workforce Commission recommends having a backup payment method ready.

Other states fall somewhere between those two, and several add rules for pay cards. Before you make direct deposit the default, check your state labor department’s wage payment rules, give written notice, keep the signed authorizations, and keep a paper check or pay card option for anyone who needs it.

Where to find your pay stub when you are paid by direct deposit

With direct deposit, the paycheck and the pay stub arrive separately. The money goes to the bank; the stub comes from the employer. Depending on where you work, that means a paper stub, a PDF emailed to you, a payroll portal login, or a notification in your employer’s employee app. In Workzenly, the stub is published to each employee’s app on payday, so Emily Carter in the kitchen can open the November 20 stub on her phone and see 72 regular hours, 6 overtime hours, 8 hours of PTO and an $86 expense reimbursement adding up to $1,777.00 gross, then compare the net pay line with the amount her bank posted.

Check every stub on payday: pay period, hours, rate, deductions, and that net pay equals the deposit. Our guide on how to read a pay stub explains each line. If the deposit does not match the stub, or did not arrive, tell payroll the same day; corrections are faster while the bank’s return window is still open.

Frequently asked questions

How long does direct deposit take for a first paycheck?

Usually one pay cycle, sometimes two. Your employer or its payroll provider may send a zero-dollar prenote to test your account, and Nacha rules require a wait of three banking days before the first live payment. If you add your account a few days before payroll is approved, the first check is often still a paper check or pay card.

What time does direct deposit go through?

For a standard ACH payroll credit, your bank must make the money available by 9:00 a.m. in the bank's local time on the settlement date, which is normally payday. Many banks post it between midnight and about 6 a.m. Some post it one or two days early when they receive the file. Your own bank sets the exact time.

Why does my coworker get paid before me?

Your employer sends one payroll file with a single settlement date. Your coworker's bank chooses to post credits as soon as the file arrives, often a day or two early, while your bank waits for the official settlement date. Both are allowed. The employer did not pay one of you first.

Does direct deposit go through on weekends or holidays?

No. The ACH network settles only on banking days, so nothing settles on Saturdays, Sundays or Federal Reserve holidays. If payday falls on one of those days, most employers move the pay date to the previous banking day. A bank may still show a pending deposit over a weekend if it received the file early.

Can my employer make me use direct deposit?

It depends on your state. Federal Regulation E says an employer cannot require you to use a particular bank, but can require direct deposit if you choose the bank. Some states, such as New York, require your advance written consent. Others, such as Texas, let an employer require it with written notice if you already have an account.

What happens if I entered the wrong account number?

If the account does not exist, the receiving bank returns the payment, usually within two banking days, and your employer reissues it once the money comes back. If the number belongs to someone else's open account, recovery takes longer and involves both banks. Tell payroll immediately and double-check the routing and account numbers.
