Quick answer

  • Good employee onboarding software collects the signed offer letter, W-4, state forms, I-9 Section 1, direct deposit and handbook acknowledgment on the new hire's phone, then sends that data into payroll without retyping.
  • Electronic I-9s must meet DHS standards in 8 CFR 274a.2 (signatures, audit trail, index, security). Software does not remove your duty to examine documents in person, unless you use E-Verify and the DHS remote alternative procedure.
  • Pick by how you hire: a standalone tool for heavy hiring, an HRIS or payroll module if you already use one, or a workforce app if most of your team is hourly and works from phones.

Employee onboarding software replaces the clipboard of forms you hand a new hire on day one. It sends the offer letter for e-signature, collects tax and work-authorization forms, captures direct deposit details, assigns first-week tasks and stores everything in the employee's record. For a small business, the value is simple: fewer missing forms, fewer typos in payroll, and a new hire who shows up on the first shift ready to work instead of filling out paper. This guide covers what to look for, the federal rules that decide whether an electronic form counts, and how the main types of onboarding tools compare.

What onboarding software does

Most onboarding tools cover the same stretch of the employee lifecycle: from the moment a candidate accepts an offer until they are working on their own. If you want the full process first, read our step-by-step guide to employee onboarding. In software terms, the work breaks into seven jobs.

  • Offer letter e-signature. Send the offer from a template, get a legally binding electronic signature, and file the signed copy. Our offer letter templates show what the letter itself should say.
  • Federal forms. The new hire completes the W-4 for federal tax withholding and Section 1 of the Form I-9 for employment eligibility, with the employer completing Section 2.
  • State forms. Most states with an income tax have their own withholding certificate, and many states require their own notices at hire. Texas, for example, has no state income tax, so there is no state withholding form there. Software should know which forms apply to the work location.
  • Direct deposit. The employee enters a routing and account number (and any split between accounts) and authorizes the deposit.
  • Handbook acknowledgment. The employee reads the handbook and signs that they received it. If you don't have one yet, start with our employee handbook template.
  • Tasks and checklists. Assign jobs to the new hire, the manager and whoever orders uniforms, keys or accounts, with due dates and reminders.
  • Training and equipment. Assign required training, track certifications, and log the laptop, keys, badge or uniform you issue so you get them back later.

Behind those jobs sits the part buyers forget to check: where the data goes next. A W-4 that sits in an onboarding tool as a PDF still has to be typed into payroll. A W-4 whose answers flow into payroll is done.

The full list of required forms is in our new hire paperwork guide, and the task-by-task version is in the employee onboarding checklist.

Must-haves and nice-to-haves in new hire onboarding software

Plenty of employee onboarding software looks alike in a demo. Separate what you need to stay compliant and get people paid from what is pleasant but optional.

Must-haves

  • Mobile completion. Hourly hires often do not have a laptop. If the forms do not work well on a phone, people will finish them on the first shift, on the clock, or not at all.
  • Current federal forms. The W-4 must match the current IRS form, and the I-9 must be a version USCIS currently accepts. USCIS told employers using electronic I-9 systems to move to the version with a 05/31/2027 expiration date by July 31, 2026.
  • A compliant electronic I-9 process that meets DHS standards, covered in detail below, plus support for the employer's Section 2 review.
  • State form coverage for every state where you have employees.
  • Payroll handoff. Tax setup, pay rate, direct deposit and start date land in payroll without anyone retyping them.
  • Deadline tracking. Reminders for I-9 Section 1 (by the first day of work for pay) and Section 2 (within 3 business days after that), and for state new hire reporting.
  • Role-based access so the person who orders uniforms cannot see Social Security numbers.
  • Exports. You can pull every signed document and audit record out of the system if you leave or get inspected.

Nice-to-haves

  • Welcome pages, team intros and photos.
  • A built-in learning library with quizzes.
  • Pre-boarding surveys and 30-60-90 day check-ins.
  • Built-in E-Verify case creation, if you use E-Verify.
  • Equipment and account provisioning for office or IT-heavy roles.
  • Branded templates by location or job type.

A nice-to-have becomes a must-have when your business depends on it. A clinic that cannot schedule a nurse without a current license needs certification tracking. A coffee shop with eight people does not need a learning library.

The legal rules any onboarding tool must follow

Software makes forms faster to complete. It does not change what the law requires. Ask how each vendor handles these four rules.

Electronic I-9s must meet DHS standards

Federal regulations at 8 CFR 274a.2 let employers complete, sign and store Forms I-9 electronically, but only in a system that meets specific standards. In summary, the system must have:

  • Controls for integrity, accuracy and reliability, and controls that prevent and detect unauthorized or accidental changes or deletion.
  • An audit trail, with a secure and permanent record of each time a form is created, completed, updated or changed, showing the date, the person and the action.
  • An indexing system so any record can be found and retrieved, and the ability to produce legible printed copies.
  • Electronic signatures that are applied at the time of the transaction, linked to the form, recorded with a way to verify the signer's identity, and confirmed in print if the employee asks.
  • Documentation of how the system creates, changes and keeps forms, which you must be able to hand over at an inspection.

USCIS also says its own fillable PDF of Form I-9 does not meet these standards. You can type into it, but the employee and employer must print it and sign by hand. So "we have a fillable I-9" is not the same as "we have a compliant electronic I-9." Ask vendors to describe how they meet 8 CFR 274a.2 in writing.

Section 2 still needs your document review

Onboarding software can collect Section 1 before the first day, but Section 2 is the employer's job. You, or an authorized representative, must examine the documents the employee chooses to present. Uploading a photo of a driver's license is not a review. The only remote option is the DHS alternative procedure, which is open to employers enrolled in E-Verify and in good standing at the hiring site: you review copies of the documents, then see the same documents with the employee on a live video call, keep the copies and check the box in Section 2. Our I-9 form guide covers the details.

Electronic W-4s have their own IRS requirements

IRS regulations allow electronic Form W-4s if the system shows the employee the same information as the current paper form, puts the same penalties of perjury statement right before the electronic signature, makes it reasonably certain the person signing is the employee, and can produce a hard copy if the IRS asks. Check that the vendor updates the form when the IRS releases a new version.

E-signatures and the ESIGN Act

The federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN Act) says a signature, contract or record cannot be denied legal effect only because it is electronic, and most states have adopted the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act. That covers offer letters and handbook acknowledgments in general. A few documents, like the I-9 and W-4, carry the extra agency rules above, and some state notices have their own delivery rules, so confirm with your state labor department.

Data security and access in onboarding tools

Employee onboarding software collects the most sensitive data you hold on anyone: Social Security numbers, bank accounts, birth dates, immigration documents and home addresses. Treat security as a buying criterion, not a footnote.

  • Least access. Managers need to see that forms are done, not what is on them. Pay and bank details should be visible only to people with payroll access.
  • Separate I-9 storage. Keep I-9s and their copies apart from personnel files, so an inspection or a lawsuit does not expose performance notes or medical information.
  • Retention rules. Keep each I-9 for 3 years after the hire date or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later. Ask whether the tool can flag forms that are past that date so you can purge them on schedule.
  • Sign-in security. Ask about multi-factor sign-in for admins, encryption, and how the vendor handles a former manager's account.
  • Your data, your exit. Confirm in the contract that you can export every document and audit record in a usable format.

Ask vendors to put any security certifications or audits in writing, and check them yourself rather than relying on a sales slide.

Buying checklist for onboarding software

Use this list when you shortlist employee onboarding software. Anything you mark "no" on the first eight items should be a reason to keep looking.

  1. A new hire can finish every form on a phone in one sitting.
  2. The W-4 matches the current IRS form, and the vendor updates it when the IRS does.
  3. The I-9 is a currently accepted edition, and the vendor will describe in writing how the system meets 8 CFR 274a.2.
  4. The tool supports your Section 2 process, including the E-Verify remote alternative procedure if you use it.
  5. State withholding forms and notices are covered for every state where you employ people.
  6. Tax, pay and direct deposit details reach payroll without retyping.
  7. Access can be limited by role, and bank and Social Security details are hidden from managers.
  8. You can export all documents and audit records.
  9. Templates for offer letters and checklists can vary by job and location.
  10. Reminders go to the new hire and the manager before each deadline.
  11. Signed handbook acknowledgments are stored with the employee record.
  12. Training, certifications and equipment can be tracked if your work needs it.
  13. Setup and data import are included, or the cost is clear before you sign.

A worked example

Northfield Coffee in Austin hires a new barista, Priya Shah (an illustrative example), who starts Monday, November 16, 2026. With onboarding software, the timeline looks like this:

  • Offer accepted, Friday, November 6: Priya e-signs the offer letter and gets a link to the rest of her forms.
  • Over the weekend: she completes her W-4 and I-9 Section 1, adds direct deposit and signs the handbook acknowledgment on her phone. Texas has no state income tax, so there is no state withholding form.
  • Monday, November 16: the manager examines the documents she brings and completes Section 2 that morning. The deadline would be Thursday, November 19, three business days after her first day.
  • First paycheck: her hours for November 15 to 28 are paid on Friday, December 4, using the W-4 and bank details she entered, with nothing retyped.

The employer still reports Priya to the Texas new hire reporting program. The federal rule is 20 days after the hire date, and some states set shorter deadlines, so check your state's new hire reporting site.

Questions to ask in an onboarding software demo

Demos are scripted. Bring your own questions, and ask the vendor to show, not tell.

  • "Send me the new hire link now. Can I finish it on my phone in under 15 minutes?"
  • "Show me where the W-4 answers end up in payroll. Who types anything?"
  • "How does your electronic I-9 meet 8 CFR 274a.2? Can you send that in writing?"
  • "Walk me through Section 2 for an in-person review and for the E-Verify alternative procedure."
  • "Which state forms do you cover for our states, and who updates them when they change?"
  • "What can a shift manager see on a new hire's record? What can't they see?"
  • "What happens to our documents and audit trail if we cancel?"
  • "What does setup involve, and who imports our current employees?"
  • "Is onboarding included in the plan you are quoting, or is it an add-on?"

Frequently asked questions

Can a new hire sign the I-9 electronically?

Yes, if the software meets the DHS standards for electronic Form I-9 systems in 8 CFR 274a.2. The signature must be applied at the time of signing, the system must record who signed, and the employee can ask for a printed confirmation. The fillable PDF on uscis.gov does not qualify, so it must be printed and signed by hand.

Does onboarding software let me check I-9 documents remotely?

Software alone does not. You or an authorized representative must examine the employee's documents in person, unless you are enrolled in E-Verify, in good standing, and use the DHS alternative procedure: review copies, then see the same documents on a live video call. The software can store the copies and the record.

Is an electronic W-4 valid?

Yes. IRS regulations allow employers to accept Form W-4 electronically if the system shows the same information as the current paper form, includes the same penalties of perjury statement right before the signature, verifies the employee's identity, and can produce a hard copy if the IRS asks for one.

Do small businesses need dedicated onboarding software?

Not always. A business hiring a few people a year can manage with good templates and a checklist. Once you hire every month, run more than one location, or keep losing forms, software that collects e-signed forms on the employee's phone and feeds payroll usually saves more time than it costs to set up.

What is the difference between onboarding software and an HRIS?

Onboarding software handles the stretch from accepted offer to productive employee: forms, signatures, tasks and training. An HRIS is the ongoing system of record for every employee. Many HRIS and payroll products include an onboarding module, so the real question is whether new hire data flows into payroll without retyping.
