Quick answer

  • For most teams, the monthly shift roster is the one to use: employees down the side, days 1 to 31 across, a shift code in each box, and counts that fill in.
  • Overtime is never monthly. The overtime check splits the month into workweeks, which is how the FLSA counts hours over 40.

Monthly hours and overtime: what to know

Monthly schedules make two mistakes easy. The first is treating 160 hours as a full-time month. A 40-hour week averages about 173.3 hours a month (40 × 52 ÷ 12), and an actual month runs from about 160 to 184 weekday hours, depending on how many weekdays it has. December 2026 has 23 weekdays, so 184 hours.

The second is checking overtime against the month. Federal overtime is based on a fixed workweek of 168 hours, and the DOL says hours may not be averaged over two or more weeks. December 2026 starts on a Tuesday and ends on a Thursday, so with a Monday workweek it touches five workweeks, two of them shared with November and January. Count overtime in each one, including the days outside the month. That is why the overtime template shows 35 days.

How to use a monthly employee schedule template

  1. Start with demand. Fill in the staffing plan: how many people each shift needs on each day of the month.
  2. Collect time off and availability early. Put approved PTO, pending requests and unavailability on the time off calendar before the month is built.
  3. Build the roster or the rotation. Use the shift roster for flexible teams, or the rotating calendar if crews follow a pattern. Check the coverage rows against the plan.
  4. Check each workweek. Copy hours into the overtime check, or look at weekly totals, before you post.
  5. Post it, then confirm weekly. Post the month as early as you can, and use weekly check-ins for changes. Our weekly schedule templates work well for that.
  6. Save each month. Name files by month and keep them at least two years.

A monthly spreadsheet becomes hard work when one change in week 1 ripples through the rest of the month. When that happens weekly, a scheduling app that repeats patterns and handles swaps will save the time.

US rules to know for monthly schedules

General principles checked on federal and state agency sites. Laws vary by state and city and change, so confirm with your state labor department.

  • Overtime is weekly, not monthly. Non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek; averaging over two or more weeks is not permitted. Hospitals and residential care facilities may use the 8-and-80 system with a prior agreement.
  • On-call can be paid time. On call at the employer’s premises is work time; on call at home generally is not, unless the restrictions are heavy. Time spent handling calls or going in counts as work time.
  • Holidays and vacation are not required by federal law. The DOL says the FLSA does not require pay for time not worked, such as vacations or holidays. Many states and cities do require paid sick leave; see our guide to paid time off.
  • Minors have hour limits. 14- and 15-year-olds may work no more than 18 hours in a school week and 3 on a school day, between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day).
  • Advance notice laws exist in some places. Oregon and Seattle require covered large retail and food service employers to post schedules 14 days ahead, and Oregon includes hospitality. A monthly schedule helps you meet those rules.
  • Keep schedules two years. The DOL lists work and time schedules among the records employers keep for two years, and payroll records for three.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a monthly work schedule in Excel?

Download the monthly employee schedule template and type the first day of the month at the top. The dates and weekdays fill in, and days 29 to 31 go blank in shorter months. Enter a shift code for each person and day, and the sheet counts shifts, hours and PTO per person and staff per shift per day.

Is overtime calculated monthly?

No. Under the FLSA, overtime is owed for hours over 40 in each workweek, and hours cannot be averaged across weeks. A month usually has four full workweeks plus parts of one or two more, so check each workweek. The monthly schedule with overtime check template totals all five workweeks that touch the month.

How many hours is full time in a month?

There is no single legal number. A 40-hour week averages about 173 hours a month, because a year has 52 weeks, or about 4.33 weeks per month. Counting the weekdays in a given month gives a range of roughly 160 to 184 hours. For overtime, only the hours in each workweek matter.

Should I post a monthly or a weekly schedule?

Post monthly when shifts follow a pattern, as in rotations, on-call rotas and healthcare units, because people can plan around them. Post weekly when demand changes week to week, as in restaurants and retail. Many teams plan a month and confirm it weekly. Check whether a local scheduling law sets the notice you must give.

Do employers have to pay holidays in the schedule?

Not under federal law. The Department of Labor says the FLSA does not require pay for time not worked, such as holidays or vacations. Paid holidays are a policy choice or part of a contract. Mark them on the monthly schedule so payroll codes them as holiday hours, not PTO.

Can I use these monthly schedule templates in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. The date formulas, shift counts and overtime totals work there. Dropdown lists and color rules usually carry over too; if one does not, type the codes and the totals still calculate.
