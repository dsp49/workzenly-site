3. Restaurant employee handbook template

Best for restaurants, bars, cafés and catering companies

BLUEBIRD KITCHEN EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK (excerpt) Austin, Texas TIPS AND TIP POOLS Tips belong to the employees who receive them. Managers, supervisors and owners do not keep any part of employees' tips, including from a tip pool. Servers and bartenders are paid a cash wage of $2.13 an hour and we take a tip credit of $5.12 an hour, as allowed by federal and Texas law. If your cash wage plus tips does not reach $7.25 for every hour in a workweek, we pay the difference. Tip pool: servers contribute 3% of their food sales at the end of each shift. The pool is shared by bussers and food runners on that shift, by hours worked. Credit card tips are paid on the next payday. FOOD SAFETY Wash hands before starting work, after breaks, after handling raw food and after using the restroom. Get a food handler card within 60 days of hire, the Texas state rule, or sooner if the local health department requires it. Do not work while you have vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice, or a sore throat with fever; tell the manager on duty. ALCOHOL SERVICE Only employees who are at least 18 and have completed TABC seller-server training may serve alcohol. Check ID for anyone who appears under 30. SCHEDULES, SHIFT SWAPS AND STAFF MEALS Schedules are posted Wednesday for the following week. You may swap a shift with a qualified coworker in the app if a manager approves it. Employees on shift get one free staff meal. UNIFORMS Wear a black shirt, black pants and non-slip shoes. We provide aprons. Any uniform cost you pay will never reduce your pay below the minimum wage or reduce overtime pay.
Download Word (.docx) Printable PDF

Word and PDF. The core handbook policies plus tips and tip pools, food safety, alcohol service, schedules and shift swaps, staff meals, uniforms and employees under 18.

Tips cause more restaurant wage claims than anything else, so this handbook spells them out. Under the FLSA, an employer, manager or supervisor may never keep employees’ tips, even through a pool, and an employer taking a tip credit must pay at least $2.13 an hour in cash wages and make up any shortfall to the full minimum wage. Many states set a higher cash wage or ban the tip credit, so replace the Texas figures in the sample with your state’s. Food handler card and alcohol training rules also vary by state and county.

How to write your handbook from these templates

  1. Check your size and states. Count employees, including part-time, and list every state where someone works. Both decide which laws apply.
  2. Start from the right template. The small business handbook, or the restaurant or retail version, and the outline to check for gaps.
  3. Fill in every bracket. Workweek, payday, PTO numbers, who to report to and how to call out. Match your attendance policy and no call, no show rules to what managers actually do.
  4. Add your state’s rules. Minimum wage, overtime, breaks, sick leave, final pay timing and protected categories. Our minimum wage by state guide is a starting point; your state labor department is the final word.
  5. Get a legal review. An hour with an employment attorney in your state is worth it for a document every employee signs.
  6. Distribute and collect signatures. Give it to everyone, collect the acknowledgment form, and keep a copy of each version you issue.
  7. Review every year. Use the tracker, and tell employees about changes in writing.

The handbook should match your systems. If it says employees can see their PTO balance and request time off in an app, make sure they can; if you still track balances in a spreadsheet, our PTO tracker templates keep the numbers consistent with the policy.

US rules to know before you publish a handbook

These are general principles checked against federal and state agency sources. They are not legal advice; laws vary by state and city and change often.

  • No federal law requires a handbook, but if you are covered by the FMLA and have eligible employees, the Department of Labor says the FMLA general notice must be included in the handbook, or given to each new employee at hire if you have none. Our FMLA leave guide explains who is covered.
  • Some states require a written harassment policy. California requires employers to have and distribute a written harassment, discrimination and retaliation prevention policy, with a complaint process that does not require going to the direct supervisor. New York requires every employer to adopt a sexual harassment prevention policy and give it to employees at hire and at each annual training.
  • Do not ban pay discussions. The NLRB says a rule that prohibits employees from discussing wages, or requires permission first, is unlawful for most private employers, even if it is never enforced.
  • Overtime and breaks. Under the FLSA, non-exempt employees get 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Federal law does not require breaks, but short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes that you do give count as paid work time, and bona fide meal periods of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid only when the employee is fully relieved of duty. Several states, such as California, add daily overtime; see our California overtime law guide.
  • Tips belong to employees. The DOL says employers, managers and supervisors may not keep employees’ tips for any reason, whether or not a tip credit is taken.
  • At-will is not universal. Montana’s labor department says the state is not at-will once the probationary period ends, so the at-will paragraph must be replaced there. Elsewhere, keep it and add that the handbook is not a contract.

Frequently asked questions

Is an employee handbook required by law?

No federal law requires a handbook. But some policies must be in writing in some places: California and New York require a written harassment prevention policy, and covered FMLA employers with a handbook must include the FMLA general notice in it. A handbook is usually the easiest place to keep these written policies together.

What should an employee handbook include?

At minimum: a welcome, a statement that the handbook is not a contract, at-will wording where it applies, equal opportunity and anti-harassment policies with reporting steps, work hours and timekeeping, overtime, breaks, pay, time off and leave, attendance, conduct, safety, leaving the company, and a signed acknowledgment form.

Do employees have to sign the employee handbook?

No federal law requires a signature, but a signed acknowledgment is strong proof that an employee received the handbook and knew the rules. Ask for it at hire and after every major update, keep it in the personnel file, and accept an electronic signature if you distribute the handbook online.

How often should an employee handbook be updated?

Review it at least once a year and whenever a law changes. Many state minimum wage and leave laws change on January 1 or July 1, and opening a location in a new state or growing past a size threshold, such as 15 or 50 employees, can bring new obligations. Tell employees about changes in writing.

Can a handbook say employees may not discuss pay?

No. The National Labor Relations Board says a rule that bars employees from discussing wages with each other, or requires permission first, is unlawful for most private employers, union or not. Many handbooks now state the opposite: that employees may discuss pay, benefits and working conditions.

Is a free employee handbook template enough?

It is a strong start for a small business, because most policies are the same everywhere. What a template cannot know is your state and city law: sick leave, breaks, final pay and protected categories differ. Fill in those sections from your state labor department and have an employment attorney review the finished handbook.
