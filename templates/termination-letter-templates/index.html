Quick answer

  • For most at-will separations, use the simple termination letter: the end date, final pay, benefits and property return, with no reason you have to defend later.

Best for any at-will separation where you do not need to state a reason

Final pay and benefits - Final paycheck: You will receive your final pay, including all hours worked through November 6 and your tips owed, by direct deposit on Thursday, November 12, 2026. - Health insurance: Your coverage ends on November 30, 2026. Company property Please return your server book, apron and door key by Tuesday, November 10, to Daniel Reyes at the restaurant. If you have questions about your final pay or benefits, contact Laura Chen at (512) 555-0187 or payroll@bluebirdkitchen.com. We thank you for your work with us and wish you the best. Sincerely, Laura Chen General Manager

One-page letter: effective date, final pay, health insurance end date and COBRA line, retirement plan, other benefits, property return, a contact for questions and an acknowledgment of receipt.

This is the template most employment lawyers would start you with. It states only what you control and can prove: the date, the pay and the benefits. Have the conversation in person first, then hand over the letter; it should confirm the meeting, not replace it.

2. Termination letter for poor performance

Best for ending employment after documented warnings or an improvement plan

Your employment with Buckeye Supply Co. as Order Picker ends effective November 6, 2026. The reason is that your job performance has not met the standards we discussed with you. Word and PDF: confidential header, effective date, the standards not met with measurable facts, the dates of earlier warnings and the improvement plan, and the full final pay, benefits and property section.

Use this letter only when the file backs it up. Every fact in the letter should match a record you already have: warning notices, an improvement plan, time clock data or sales reports. 3. Termination letter for misconduct or a policy violation

Best for immediate termination for theft, violence, harassment, safety or serious policy breaches

On October 31, the register you closed was $240.00 short, and the security video shows you removing cash from the drawer before the count. This violates our cash handling policy in section 4.2 of the employee handbook, which you acknowledged on March 2, 2026. We reviewed the video and the closing report and met with you on November 4 to hear your account before making this decision. Final pay and benefits - Final paycheck: Your final check, for all hours worked through today and 14.0 hours of accrued vacation, is enclosed with this letter. - Health insurance: Your coverage ends on November 30, 2026. Also enclosed: the EDD pamphlet “For Your Benefit: California’s Programs for the Unemployed” (DE 2320) and a notice of separation. Company property Please return your keys and alarm code card today. Word and PDF: immediate effective date, one or two factual sentences on what happened, the policy and when it was acknowledged, the investigation step, and the final pay, benefits, state notice and property section.

Describe what happened, not what you think of the person. “The video shows you removing cash” is a fact; “you are a thief” is a label that can create a defamation claim. Investigate before you decide, even briefly, and write down that you heard the employee’s side. 5. Layoff letter for a position elimination

Best for closings, reorganizations and cuts that are not about performance

Word and PDF: business reason and “not a reflection of your performance” line, last day, optional notice pay, severance and recall paragraphs, final pay and benefits, and where to apply for unemployment.

A layoff letter should make clear the job is ending, not the person’s standing. If severance depends on signing a release and the employee is 40 or older, federal law gives them at least 21 days to consider it (45 days in a group layoff) and 7 days to revoke, which the example reflects. Larger layoffs can trigger the federal WARN Act or a state version, which generally requires 60 days’ notice for covered plant closings and mass layoffs at employers with 100 or more employees. In New York, the letter also gives the exact date benefits end, which the state requires in writing.

6. Termination letter during the probationary period

Best for new hires who are not working out in their first weeks

Coastal Realty Partners 2400 E Commercial Blvd, Fort Lauderdale, FL 33308 November 6, 2026 Dear Lauren, Thank you for meeting with me today. As discussed, your employment as Office Assistant will end effective November 6, 2026, during your 90-day introductory period. The role requires managing the closing calendar without missed deadlines, and over the past six weeks it has not been the right fit. Employment with Coastal Realty Partners is at will, both during and after the introductory period. Final pay and benefits - Final paycheck: Pay for all hours worked through today will be deposited on our regular payday, Friday, November 13, 2026. - Health insurance: You were not yet enrolled in the health plan, so no coverage is affected. Company property Please return your office key and parking pass today. We appreciate the effort you put in and wish you success in your next role. Sincerely, Michelle Grant Office Manager
Download Word (.docx) Printable PDF

Word and PDF: short letter with the effective date, the introductory period, an optional one-sentence reason, a reminder that employment is at will during and after the period, and final pay and property lines.

Call it an “introductory” period, and say employment stays at will after it ends; otherwise employees can read “probation” as a promise of job security once they pass it. Montana is different: under its Wrongful Discharge from Employment Act, good cause is needed for a discharge after the probationary period, so the period has real legal weight there. Keep the reason to one honest sentence or leave it out.

What to include in an employee termination letter

Whichever termination letter template you start from, the finished letter should cover these points and nothing more. Short letters are easier to defend.

  • Effective date. The exact last day of employment.
  • Reason, only if you choose to give one. Facts that match the file, or no reason at all for an at-will separation.
  • Final paycheck. What it includes (hours, overtime, tips, PTO if owed) and the exact date and method.
  • Benefits. The date health coverage ends and that a COBRA or state continuation notice will follow, plus retirement and other plans.
  • Company property. What to return, by when and how.
  • State notices. For example, California’s DE 2320 pamphlet, or New York’s exact benefit end date.
  • A contact. One name and phone number for pay and benefit questions.
  • Acknowledgment of receipt. A signature line that confirms receipt, not agreement.

Leave out opinions, apologies that admit fault, comments about age, health, family or any protected trait, and anything you have not documented.

How to use these templates

  1. Check the file first. Make sure warnings, write-ups and time records support the decision. Our employee write-up forms help you build that file before you need it.
  2. Pick the template and fill in every bracket. Choose the simple letter unless you have a documented reason you want on record. The files open in Word and Google Docs.
  3. Prepare the final paycheck. Use the checklist’s Final pay sheet, and confirm your state deadline and whether accrued vacation must be paid out.
  4. Meet in private, with a witness. Keep it short: the decision, the date, the pay and benefits. Hand over the letter at the end.
  5. Close access and collect property the same day, and remove the person from future schedules.
  6. File the signed copy and send the COBRA notification to your plan administrator within 30 days.

Reread the termination letter template once more before the meeting and remove anything that sounds like an opinion. If the person is in a protected group, recently complained, recently took leave or has a contract, talk to an employment lawyer before you act. US rules to know before you send a letter of termination

These are general principles, checked against federal and state agency sources. Rules differ by state and change often, so confirm with your state labor department or a lawyer.

  • At-will employment, with exceptions. The Department of Labor says that a termination not based on discrimination, whistleblowing or a complaint under a law it enforces is subject only to any contract between employer and employee. Montana requires good cause after the probationary period. Discrimination and retaliation laws apply everywhere.
  • Final paycheck timing varies by state. The DOL says federal law does not require immediate payment. California’s Labor Commissioner says a discharged employee is owed all wages, including accrued vacation, immediately, with waiting time penalties of up to 30 days of pay for willful delays. Texas requires final pay within six calendar days of a discharge.
  • COBRA. Group health plans of employers with 20 or more employees must offer continuation coverage. The employer generally has 30 days to notify the plan of a termination, the plan has 14 days to send the election notice, and the employee gets at least 60 days to elect coverage, which can last up to 18 months. Many states have continuation laws for smaller employers.
  • State written notices. California requires immediate written notice of a discharge, layoff or leave of absence and the DE 2320 pamphlet. New York requires written notice of the exact termination date and benefits end date within five working days.
  • Severance releases for workers 40 and over. The EEOC says a waiver of age discrimination claims needs at least 21 days to consider (45 in a group termination) and 7 days to revoke after signing.
  • Keep the records. The EEOC requires personnel records of an involuntarily terminated employee to be kept for one year from the termination date, and the FLSA requires payroll records for three years.

Frequently asked questions

Is an employer required to give a termination letter?

No federal law requires one, but some states require written notice. California requires immediate written notice of a discharge or layoff plus the EDD For Your Benefit pamphlet, and New York requires written notice of the exact termination date and benefits end date within five working days. A letter is good practice everywhere.

Do you have to give a reason in a termination letter?

In most states, no. At-will employment means you can end employment without stating a cause, so the simple template states only the end date and final pay details. If you do give a reason, it must be accurate and match your records, because an unemployment claim or lawsuit will compare the letter with the file.

When is a final paycheck due after termination?

It depends on the state. The Department of Labor says federal law does not require immediate payment. California requires all wages immediately when an employee is discharged, and Texas requires final pay within six calendar days of a discharge. Other states use the next regular payday. Check your state labor department before the meeting.

Should the employee sign a termination letter?

Ask them to sign that they received it, not that they agree. The templates include an acknowledgment line that says exactly that. If the employee refuses, write “Employee declined to sign” with the date and have a witness initial it. Give the employee a copy either way and keep one in the file.

What is the difference between a layoff letter and a termination letter?

A layoff letter ends employment because the job is going away, not because of anything the employee did, and usually says so. It often mentions notice pay, severance, recall rights and unemployment benefits. A termination letter for cause explains a performance or conduct reason. Both include final pay, benefits and property return details.

How long should I keep termination records?

The EEOC requires covered employers to keep personnel records for one year, and for an involuntarily terminated employee, one year from the termination date. FLSA payroll records must be kept for three years. Many employers keep the whole file longer because state rules and claim deadlines can run past those periods.
