How to use these onboarding checklists

  1. Choose your set. The master checklist for every hire, plus the hourly or remote list, and the 30-60-90 plan for roles that take longer to learn.
  2. Edit once. Open each onboarding checklist template and add your business name, your state’s forms and notices, your tools and your training. Delete what does not apply.
  3. Name one owner. One person is responsible for the whole checklist, even when others do the tasks.
  4. Start before day one. Send the offer letter (our job offer letter templates help), forms and first-week schedule in advance, so the first day is about people, not paperwork.
  5. Check deadlines daily. Use the tracker for I-9 and state reporting dates.
  6. Hold the check-ins. Put the 30, 60 and 90-day meetings on the calendar on day one.
  7. File the records. Keep the signed checklist in the personnel file, and keep I-9s for the required period.

US onboarding deadlines and rules to know

These are general principles checked against federal agency sources (USCIS, IRS, the HHS Office of Child Support Services and the Department of Labor). They are not legal advice, and states add their own rules, so check your state labor department.

  • Form I-9, Section 1: the employee completes it no later than the first day of employment. They may do it earlier, but only after accepting the job offer.
  • Form I-9, Section 2: the employer examines the documents and completes it within three business days of the first day of employment. If the job lasts less than three business days, it is due on the first day.
  • Keep the I-9: for three years after the date of hire or one year after employment ends, whichever is later.
  • Form W-4: use the 2026 version from irs.gov for new hires this year. Our W-4 guide explains each step.
  • State new hire report: federal law requires reporting within 20 days of the date of hire, with the employee’s name, address, Social Security number and date of hire, and the employer’s name, address and EIN. States may require it sooner, and some ask for more data.
  • Pay notices at hire: some states require a written notice of pay rate and payday. New York, for example, requires a signed pay notice at hire and requires the employer to keep it for six years.
  • FMLA notice: covered employers with eligible employees must give the FMLA general notice in the employee handbook, or to each new employee at hire if there is no handbook. Our employee handbook templates include a section for it.

Frequently asked questions

What should an onboarding checklist include?

Tasks for four stages: before day one (offer letter, forms, schedule, equipment), the first day (welcome, paperwork, time clock, safety), the first week (I-9 Section 2, training, first paycheck), and the first 90 days (state new hire report, check-ins, a 90-day review). Give each task an owner and a due date.

What paperwork does a new employee need to fill out in the US?

Federally, a Form W-4 for income tax withholding and a Form I-9 for employment eligibility. Most states with an income tax also have a state withholding form, and the employer must send a new hire report to the state. Add your own documents, such as the offer letter, handbook acknowledgment, direct deposit form and emergency contact.

When does Form I-9 have to be completed?

USCIS says the employee must complete Section 1 no later than the first day of employment, and the employer must complete Section 2 within three business days of that first day. For a Monday start, Section 2 is due by Thursday. If the job lasts fewer than three business days, Section 2 is due on the first day.

How long does employee onboarding take?

Paperwork takes the first few days, but onboarding usually runs 30 to 90 days, until the employee works on their own and has had a first review. Hourly roles in restaurants and retail often finish core training in one to two weeks, while office and skilled trade roles commonly use a full 30-60-90 day plan.

How soon must a new hire be reported to the state?

Federal law requires employers to report new hires to the state directory within 20 days of the date of hire, and states may set a shorter deadline. The report needs the employee’s name, address and Social Security number, the date of hire, and the employer’s name, address and federal employer identification number.

Who is responsible for onboarding a new employee?

In a small business it is usually shared. HR or the owner handles forms and payroll setup, the hiring manager runs the first day, schedule and check-ins, and a buddy or trainer teaches the job. Name one person as the owner of the whole checklist so nothing falls between them.
