Tipped pay stubs confuse everyone because some of the pay never passes through the check. The Department of Labor says an employer may pay a tipped employee a cash wage of $2.13 an hour and count up to $5.12 an hour of tips toward the $7.25 federal minimum, and must make up any shortfall. Reported cash tips are taxable wages, so they raise Social Security and Medicare, but they are subtracted before net pay because the server already has the cash. Many states require a higher cash wage or allow no tip credit at all, so change the two settings cells to your state’s rates.

How to use these pay stub templates

  1. Choose one template per pay group. Hourly staff on the hourly stub, salaried staff on the salaried stub, and the state version for anyone in California or New York.
  2. Fill in the yellow cells. Employer and employee details, pay period dates, hours from the approved timesheet, rates, pre-tax deductions and income tax withholding. Green cells are formulas.
  3. Check the settings box each January. The Social Security wage base, the California SDI rate and the New York Paid Family Leave rate and cap change every year. The files carry the 2026 figures.
  4. Carry the year to date forward. Copy the year-to-date column of the last stub into the “prior year to date” column of the next one, or keep one tab per pay date.
  5. Give the employee a PDF and keep a copy. Save each stub as a PDF for the employee, and file your copy with the payroll record for that pay date.

These templates do not calculate federal or state income tax withholding, employer payroll taxes or tax deposits. US pay stub rules to know

These principles are checked against the Department of Labor, the IRS, the California Labor Commissioner and the New York State Department of Labor. Requirements vary by state, so check yours.

  • Federal law requires records, not stubs. The DOL says the FLSA requires employers to keep accurate records of hours worked and wages paid, but does not require pay stubs. The required records include hours worked each day and week, the regular rate, straight-time and overtime earnings, additions and deductions, total wages paid each period, and the pay date and period covered.
  • Many states require a wage statement. California and New York, covered above, both list required items, and other states have their own rules for content and for paper versus electronic delivery.
  • Payroll taxes for 2026. The IRS says employees pay 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $184,500 and 1.45% Medicare on all wages, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax that employers withhold on wages over $200,000 in a calendar year.
  • Overtime uses the regular rate. Non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, and the regular rate includes differentials and nondiscretionary bonuses.
  • Keep the records. Payroll records for at least three years under the FLSA; California requires three years for payroll records showing daily hours and wages.
  • Stubs must be true. A pay stub is a record of wages actually paid. Making one that shows income that was never paid is fraud, whoever creates it.

Frequently asked questions

Are employers required to give pay stubs?

Not under federal law. The Department of Labor says the FLSA requires employers to keep accurate records of hours worked and wages paid, but does not require them to provide pay stubs. Many states do require a written or electronic wage statement each payday, including California and New York, so check your state labor department.

What has to be on a pay stub?

It depends on your state. A good pay stub shows the employer and employee, the pay period and pay date, hours and rates for each kind of pay, gross pay, every deduction, net pay and year-to-date totals. California and New York list required items in their laws, and California also requires the paid sick leave balance.

How are Social Security and Medicare calculated on a 2026 pay stub?

The employee pays 6.2% Social Security on wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 and 1.45% Medicare on all wages, according to the IRS. Employers also withhold an extra 0.9% Medicare on wages over $200,000 in a calendar year. Our pay stub templates calculate the first two and stop Social Security at the wage base.

How do I make a pay stub in Excel?

Download one of the Excel pay stub templates, type the employer and employee details, the pay period, hours and rates, and the income tax withholding from your payroll calculation. The sheet works out gross pay, overtime, Social Security, Medicare, net pay and year-to-date totals. Save a copy for every pay date and give the employee a PDF.

Is it legal to make your own pay stub?

Yes, for an employer recording wages it actually paid. That is what these templates are for. Creating a pay stub that shows income that was not paid, for example to qualify for a loan or an apartment, is fraud. If you need proof of income as an employee, ask your employer for copies of your real pay stubs.

How long should employers keep pay stubs and payroll records?

Under the FLSA, keep payroll records for at least three years and the time cards behind them for two. California requires payroll records showing daily hours and wages to be kept for three years. Keep a copy of every pay stub you issue with the payroll record for that pay date, and check your state for longer rules.
