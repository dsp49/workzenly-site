Start date: Monday, November 30, 2026 Employment type: Full-time, non-exempt (paid hourly, eligible for overtime) Pay: $19.50 per hour, paid every other Friday by direct deposit Schedule: Monday to Thursday 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Friday 7:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., with a 1-hour unpaid lunch Monday to Thursday Benefits: You will be eligible for health and dental insurance on the first of the month after 60 days, under the terms of each plan. Nothing in this letter is a contract for a fixed term of employment. Please sign and return this letter by Friday, November 13, 2026 to accept. If you have questions, call me at (813) 555-0142. Word and PDF with separate compensation and benefits sections: salary and per-period amount, exempt classification, bonus, salary review, insurance, PTO, holidays, 401(k), conditions and at-will paragraph.

Only call a job exempt if it passes both the salary test and the duties test. Since the 2024 overtime rule was vacated, the Department of Labor applies the 2019 federal minimum of $684 per week ($35,568 a year), but several states set a higher floor; California’s 2026 minimum for exempt staff is $70,304 a year. A salary alone does not make someone exempt, so check our exempt vs non-exempt guide before you use this letter. Write “at the company’s discretion” for any bonus you do not want to promise.

5. Conditional job offer letter

Best for healthcare, childcare, driving, finance and other jobs that need checks first

Desert Bloom Senior Living 4400 N 32nd St, Phoenix, AZ 85018 November 3, 2026 Dear Rosa, We are pleased to make you a conditional offer of employment for the position of Certified Nursing Assistant at Desert Bloom Senior Living, starting Monday, November 23, 2026, at $21.00 per hour. This offer is conditional on the following. We will not finalize your start date until each one is complete: 1. A background check by our screening company, which we will run only after you sign the separate disclosure and authorization form we are sending with this letter 2. A pre-employment drug test at an approved lab within 5 days 3. Verification of your Arizona CNA certification on the state registry 4. Proof of a negative TB test within the last 12 months 5. Documents that prove your identity and authorization to work in the United States for Form I-9, no later than three business days after your first day of work If something in a background report might cause us to withdraw this offer, we will first give you a copy of the report and a summary of your rights, and give you the chance to respond before we make a final decision. All other terms: - Reports to: Linda Haskins, RN, Director of Nursing - Classification: Non-exempt hourly; overtime at 1.5 times your regular rate after 40 hours a week - Schedule: Three 12-hour day shifts a week, 7:00 a.m. to 7:30 p.m., including every other weekend - Benefits: See the enclosed benefits summary Employment with Desert Bloom Senior Living is at will. This means that you or the company may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice. Please sign below by Thursday, November 5, 2026 to accept this conditional offer and return the authorization form. Sincerely, Linda Haskins, RN Director of Nursing
Download Word (.docx) Printable PDF

Word and PDF with a numbered list of contingencies (background check, drug test, license, references, I-9), a pre-adverse action promise, the remaining job terms and at-will paragraph.

A contingent offer lets you lock in a candidate while checks run. The letter does three things the law expects. It runs the background check only after a separate written authorization, which the Fair Credit Reporting Act requires. It promises a copy of the report and a chance to respond before any withdrawal. And it waits for the offer before criminal history comes up, which California’s Fair Chance Act requires of employers with five or more employees. Drug testing rules vary by state, so keep that line only where it is allowed for the role.

What to include in an offer letter template

Whichever employment offer letter you start from, check it against this list before you send it. Each item answers a question that otherwise lands on the manager or payroll in the first week.

  • Job title, manager and work location. For remote staff, the state they work in.
  • Start date. For shift workers, the first shift time too.
  • Pay rate, unit and pay frequency. “$19.50 per hour, paid every other Friday,” not just a number.
  • Exempt or non-exempt. Say whether overtime applies. This one line prevents the costliest misunderstanding.
  • Schedule or hours. A range and a note that hours follow the posted schedule.
  • Benefits and when they start. Summarize, and point to the plan documents for details.
  • Conditions. I-9 documents, and any background check, license, drug test or agreement.
  • At-will statement. Unless you truly mean to offer a fixed term.
  • Deadline and signatures. A date to accept, and lines for both sides.

Leave out promises you cannot keep: guaranteed hours, “permanent” jobs, “job security,” or a bonus described as certain when it is discretionary. Courts can read those as contract terms.

How to use these templates

  1. Pick the closest job offer letter template. Match the pay type first (hourly or salaried), then the job length (regular, seasonal, intern).
  2. Fill in every bracket. Open the Word file, replace each [bracket], delete lines that do not apply, and add your logo. The files also open in Google Docs.
  3. Check pay against the law. Confirm the rate meets your state and city minimum wage (see our minimum wage by state table) and the salary meets the exempt threshold if you mark the job exempt.
  4. Make the verbal offer, then send the letter the same day. Use the offer email in template 9 and attach the letter as a PDF.
  5. Collect the signed copy. File it in the personnel file. If you agree to changes, send a revised letter rather than editing by hand.
  6. Start onboarding. Send the W-4, I-9 Section 1 and your state forms before day one. Our new hire paperwork checklist and onboarding checklist templates list everything.

If you hire more than a handful of people a year, the job offer letter template becomes the first step of a longer chain: forms, I-9, payroll setup, the first schedule. An applicant tracking system or HR software can send the letter for e-signature and carry the details into onboarding.

US rules to know before you send an employment offer letter

These are general principles, checked against federal and state agency sources. Rules vary by state and city and change often, so confirm with your state labor department or a lawyer for your situation.

  • At-will is the default in most states, with limits. Most US employment is at will, which is why the templates say so. Montana is the main exception: under its Wrongful Discharge from Employment Act, a discharge after the probationary period is wrongful if it is not for good cause. Promises in a letter can also change the at-will relationship.
  • Overtime and exempt status. Non-exempt employees must get 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Exempt status needs both a salary of at least $684 a week under current DOL rules and qualifying job duties. California’s 2026 exempt salary minimum is $70,304, and other states have their own levels.
  • Form I-9 deadlines. USCIS says the employee completes Section 1 no later than the first day of work, and the employer completes Section 2 within three business days of the first day. The employee cannot fill in Section 1 before accepting the offer. See our Form I-9 guide.
  • Background checks. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the FTC says you must tell the candidate in a separate document that you may use a background report and get written permission, then give a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report before rejecting them. California and many cities also delay criminal history questions until after a conditional offer.
  • Separate pay notices at hire. New York requires a written pay notice at hiring with the pay rate, overtime rate and regular payday, in English and the employee’s primary language, with a signed acknowledgment kept for six years. California requires its Labor Code 2810.5 notice to employees. An offer letter does not replace these forms.
  • Noncompetes. The FTC’s nationwide noncompete ban is not in effect; the FTC dropped its appeals in 2025. State law still governs. California voids most employee noncompetes, so do not add one to an offer letter there.

Frequently asked questions

What should a job offer letter include?

The job title, manager, start date, work location, pay rate and pay frequency, whether the job is exempt or non-exempt, the expected schedule, a short benefits summary, any conditions such as a background check, an at-will statement, the deadline to accept, and signature lines for the candidate and the company.

Is a job offer letter legally binding?

An offer letter can create obligations, especially if it promises a fixed term, guaranteed bonus or job security. That is why most US offer letters state that employment is at will and that the letter is not a contract for a fixed period. Promises about pay and benefits should match what you will actually provide. Have a lawyer review letters for senior or unusual hires.

Can an employer withdraw a job offer?

In most states an employer can withdraw an at-will offer, but not for a discriminatory or retaliatory reason. If you withdraw because of a background check, the Fair Credit Reporting Act requires a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report first. Some states and cities add their own fair chance steps for criminal history.

How long should a candidate have to accept an offer?

No law sets a deadline. Three to five business days is common for most roles, and 24 to 48 hours is normal for hourly and seasonal jobs where you need to fill shifts fast. Put the exact date in the letter so the offer does not stay open indefinitely, and extend it in writing if you agree to more time.

Do I need an offer letter for part-time or hourly employees?

No federal law requires one, but it is good practice for every hire. A short letter confirms the hourly rate, overtime eligibility, payday and expected hours in writing, which prevents disputes later. Some states, including New York and California, require a separate written pay notice at hire, which the offer letter does not replace.

Should an offer letter mention salary or hourly pay?

Yes. State the amount and the unit, such as $19.00 per hour or $68,000 per year, and how often it is paid. For salaried staff, also say whether the job is exempt from overtime. Avoid annual figures for hourly staff, because they read like a guaranteed salary that the schedule may not deliver.
