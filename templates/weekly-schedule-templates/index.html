How to make a weekly employee schedule

  1. Start from demand, not from people. Write down how many people you need at each hour of each day. Last year’s sales by hour, appointment books or call volume tell you.
  2. Block out what is fixed. Enter approved time off, standing unavailability and any minors’ hour limits before you place a single shift.
  3. Fill the shifts. Give each person shifts that match their availability and the hours they want, and keep an eye on the total column so nobody drifts over 40 by accident.
  4. Check cost and coverage. Look at the open shifts, the overtime column and, if you use template 4, the labor percentage. Fix them now, not on Saturday.
  5. Post it early and in one place. Write the posting date on it. One or two weeks ahead is common; some cities and states require 14 days for covered employers.
  6. Log changes and keep a copy. Save each week’s file with the date in the name. If a shift changes after posting, note who asked for it and when.

Spreadsheets work well for one location with a stable team. When you are rebuilding the same week every Sunday, texting people about changes and checking time cards against the schedule by hand, an employee schedule maker saves that time. US rules to know when you build a weekly schedule

These are general principles checked on federal and state agency sites. Rules differ by state and city and change often, so confirm with your state labor department.

  • Overtime is counted per workweek. Non-exempt employees must get at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The workweek is a fixed, recurring seven-day period that can start on any day, and the Department of Labor says hours cannot be averaged over two weeks.
  • Some states add daily overtime. California, for example, requires overtime after 8 hours in a workday and double time after 12. See our California overtime law guide.
  • Breaks depend on the state. Federal law does not require meal or rest breaks, but short breaks you give (usually 20 minutes or less) are paid time. Meal periods of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if the employee is fully relieved of duty. Many states require meal or rest breaks.
  • Minors have hour limits. Under federal law, 14- and 15-year-olds may work no more than 3 hours on a school day, 18 hours in a school week, 8 hours on a non-school day and 40 in a non-school week, and only between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day). States can be stricter.
  • Some places require advance notice. Oregon requires covered large retail, hospitality and food service employers to give written schedules 14 days ahead. Seattle requires covered retail and food service employers to post 14 days ahead and pay time-and-a-half for closing-to-opening shifts less than 10 hours apart.
  • Keep the schedules. The DOL lists work and time schedules among the records employers keep for two years, and payroll records for three. Record hours worked each day and each workweek.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a weekly work schedule in Excel?

Download the weekly employee schedule template, type the Monday date at the top so the day headers fill in, then list each employee with their in and out times for each day and their unpaid breaks. The sheet totals each person’s hours and shows overtime above 40. Save a copy for each week so you keep a record.

What should a weekly employee schedule include?

Each employee’s name and role, the date of every day in the week, start and end times for each shift, days off and approved time off, and total scheduled hours per person. Add the location, the manager’s name and the date you posted it. Labor cost and coverage counts are useful extras for managers.

How far in advance should I post a weekly schedule?

No federal law sets a deadline, so most small businesses choose one or two weeks. Some states and cities require more for covered employers: Oregon and Seattle, for example, require large retail and food service employers to post schedules 14 days ahead, with extra pay for many later changes. Check your state and city.

Is overtime calculated per week or per pay period?

Per workweek. Under the FLSA, non-exempt employees earn at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a fixed seven-day workweek, and hours cannot be averaged over two weeks. A person who works 45 hours one week and 35 the next is owed 5 hours of overtime even on a biweekly paycheck.

Can I print these weekly schedule templates?

Yes. Every template has a printable PDF version sized for US letter paper, and the Excel files are set to print one page wide: landscape for team schedules, portrait for the two personal planners. Print a blank copy to fill in by hand, or fill in the Excel file first and print it to post in the break room.

Do these templates work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the .xlsx file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. The hour totals, overtime and count formulas work there too. Dropdown lists and color highlights carry over in most cases; if one does not, the formulas still calculate correctly.
