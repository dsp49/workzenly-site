A time off request form is the written record of who asked for what time off, when they asked, and whether a manager said yes. This list is for US small-business owners, managers and HR staff who want a clear way to handle requests, and for employees who need to ask in writing. The FMLA covers private employers with 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks, and gives eligible employees up to 12 workweeks of job-protected leave in a 12-month period. The employee does not have to say “FMLA” on the form; if the reason may qualify, the Department of Labor says the employer must give an eligibility notice within five business days, and the DOL publishes optional model notices (WH-381 and WH-382). If you ask for medical certification, the DOL says the employee generally has at least 15 calendar days to return it, which is why the example sets a due date of November 20 for a November 5 request. What to include in a time off request form

Whichever template you start from, check that your form captures these items. Each one answers a question someone will ask later, usually payroll.

  • Date of request. Decides who asked first and whether the employee met your notice rule.
  • Leave type. Vacation, PTO, sick, personal, bereavement, jury duty, unpaid. Payroll needs it to reduce the right balance, and some types are protected by law.
  • First day, last day and return date. The return date removes any doubt about weekends and days off in between.
  • Hours, not just days. Shift lengths vary, so hours are what payroll pays and what a PTO balance tracks.
  • Paid or unpaid. State it on the form rather than leaving it to the person running payroll.
  • Coverage. Who does the work or works the shift.
  • Decision, signature and date. Approved, approved with changes or denied, with a short reason for changes or denials.

Leave out medical details. A time off request template should never ask for a diagnosis. If you need medical certification for FMLA leave, collect it separately and keep it in a confidential file, as covered below.

How to use these templates

  1. Pick one or two forms. Most small businesses need the simple form or the PTO request form for everyday time off, and the leave of absence request for anything longer.
  2. Add your details. Open the Word or Excel file, add your business name and logo, and change the leave types to match your policy. The files also open in Google Docs and Google Sheets.
  3. Write the rules on the form. State how far ahead to ask (for example, two weeks for up to three days, 30 days for longer), how to call out sick, and any blackout dates such as the week of Thanksgiving in retail.
  4. Answer quickly and in writing. A decision within two or three business days lets employees book travel and lets you fill the schedule. Give a copy of the signed form back to the employee.
  5. Update the balance and the schedule the same day. Record approved hours in your PTO tracker and on the schedule, so a second request for the same day is caught before it is approved.
  6. File it. Keep the form with payroll records for at least three years, as explained in the next section.

Paper works for a team of five. Past that, the weak points are lost forms, balances that do not match payroll, and two managers approving the same weekend. That is when teams move to leave management software where requests, balances and the schedule live together.

US rules to know before you approve or deny time off

These are general principles, verified on federal and state agency sites. Laws differ by state and city and change often, so check your state labor department before you set a policy.

  • Paid vacation is not required by federal law. The Department of Labor says the Fair Labor Standards Act does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations, sick leave or holidays. These benefits are a matter of agreement between employer and employee. Our guide to paid time off covers how employers set up PTO policies.
  • Paid sick leave is required in many states and cities. California, for example, requires at least 40 hours or five days of paid sick leave per year, lets employers ask for reasonable advance notice when the need is foreseeable, and limits documentation requirements. Check whether your state or city has its own law.
  • FMLA notice runs both ways. When leave is foreseeable, the DOL says employees should give at least 30 days’ notice if practicable, and must generally follow the employer’s usual and customary procedures for requesting leave, such as a written form, unless unusual circumstances prevent it. Employers then owe the eligibility notice within five business days.
  • Some states treat earned vacation as wages. California’s Labor Commissioner says vacation vests as it is earned, so use-it-or-lose-it policies are not allowed there, although a reasonable cap is, and unused vacation must be paid in the final paycheck. Employers can still set reasonable rules about when vacation is taken, which is exactly what an approval form does.
  • Keep the records. The FLSA requires employers to keep payroll records for at least three years and the time cards and records behind wage calculations for two years. FMLA records must be kept for no less than three years, and medical certifications go in a separate confidential file.
  • Deny for business reasons, consistently. You can usually decline vacation for staffing reasons, but apply the same rule to everyone and write the reason down. Never deny time off in a way that targets a protected group or punishes someone for using protected leave.

Paid holidays interact with PTO too: if the business closes for a holiday, record it as holiday hours, not PTO, as the PTO request form example shows. Our holiday pay guide explains what employers owe on holidays.

Frequently asked questions

What should a time off request form include?

At minimum: the employee’s name and manager, the date of the request, the type of leave, the first and last day off, the return date, the hours requested, and signatures with an approved or denied box. Add a PTO balance line if you track hours, and a coverage section for shift workers.

How far in advance should employees request time off?

No federal law sets a deadline for vacation requests, so your policy decides. Two weeks is common for a few days off and 30 days for a week or more. For foreseeable FMLA leave the DOL expects at least 30 days’ notice when practicable. Some state sick leave laws let employers require only reasonable notice.

Can an employer deny a time off request?

Yes, for vacation and personal time in most cases, as long as the reason is not discriminatory or retaliatory. Employers cannot deny leave protected by law, such as FMLA leave for an eligible employee or paid sick leave under a state or city law, simply because it is inconvenient.

Do employers have to offer paid vacation?

Not under federal law. The Department of Labor says the FLSA does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations, sick leave or holidays. Paid vacation is an agreement between employer and employee, but a growing number of states and cities require paid sick leave or paid leave for any reason.

How long should we keep time off request forms?

Keep them at least three years. The FLSA requires payroll records for three years and time cards for two, and the FMLA requires leave records for no less than three years. Store any medical certifications in a separate confidential file, not the regular personnel file, and check your state for longer periods.

Is a PTO request form the same as a vacation request form?

Often, yes. A PTO request form covers any paid time off from one bank, while a vacation request form is for planned vacation days, usually several in a row. If your company keeps separate vacation and sick banks, use a form with a leave type checkbox so payroll knows which balance to reduce.
