An employee warning notice is the written record that a manager told an employee about a problem, explained what must change and said what happens next. Done well, it gives a good employee a fair chance to fix the issue and gives you a clear file if they do not. This page is for US small-business owners, managers and HR leads. Below are eight free templates: a general warning form, a verbal warning record, a written warning template for performance, an attendance warning built on time clock records, a conduct warning letter, a final written warning, an employee acknowledgment and response form, and an Excel tracker. Pick by the type of issue and how many times you have already raised it. Most employers move through three steps (verbal, written, final) before termination, but can skip steps for serious misconduct.

3. Written warning letter (performance)

Best for missed targets, quality problems and work standards

Prairie Gate Insurance Services November 5, 2026 To: Alicia Moreno, Customer Service Representative From: Derek Hall, Service Team Supervisor Subject: Written warning: ticket backlog and response times This letter is a written warning about your open ticket backlog and customer response times. What happened Our standard is to respond to new customer tickets within one business day and keep no more than 15 open tickets. From October 12 to October 30, your average first response time was 2.6 business days, and on October 30 you had 41 open tickets, 18 of them more than five days old. Earlier discussion We discussed this issue on October 9 in a verbal warning. At that time we agreed you would work the oldest tickets first each morning. What happens next If the required improvement is not made by November 20, or the issue happens again, the next step may be a final written warning. This letter does not change the at-will nature of your employment. You may add a written response, which will be kept in your file with this letter. Derek Hall Service Team Supervisor, Omaha, NE

This written warning template works because it compares the standard with a number. “Your response times are too slow” invites an argument; “2.6 business days against a standard of one” does not. Give a realistic date to improve, offer something that actually helps (here, protected time to work the backlog), and book the check-ins before the meeting ends. If the problem is skill rather than effort, an interim performance review may fit better.

Describe behavior, not character. “Shouted, used profanity and threw menus” can be checked; “bad attitude” cannot. Investigate first, even briefly, and say how. Be careful when the conduct involves complaints about pay, scheduling or working conditions raised with coworkers: the National Labor Relations Act protects that kind of group activity for most private-sector employees, union or not, so warn for how something was said, never for raising the issue.

What to include in an employee warning letter

Whichever template you start from, a written warning should answer the questions a neutral reader would ask months later.

  • Who, when and which level. Employee, manager, date and the step: verbal, written, second written or final.
  • The facts. Dates, times, numbers and records. No labels or guesses about motives.
  • The rule. The policy, handbook section or standard, and when the employee received it.
  • History. Earlier warnings on the same issue, with dates.
  • What must change and by when. Specific and measurable.
  • Support. Training, a schedule change, a checklist, regular check-ins.
  • The next step. What happens if it continues, plus an at-will sentence.
  • The employee’s side. A response line or form.
  • Signatures that confirm receipt, not agreement.

Leave out anything about age, health, pregnancy, religion, national origin, family or any other protected trait, and do not tell employees they must keep the warning secret from coworkers.

How to give an employee warning notice

  1. Check the facts. Pull the time clock records, schedule, logs or sales data. Talk to witnesses if needed. Check whether any absence is protected leave.
  2. Check consistency. Look at the tracker: how did you handle the same issue for others?
  3. Fill in the template before the meeting. Leave the employee’s response blank.
  4. Meet in private. Keep it short and calm. Explain the facts, the rule and what must change, then listen. Have a second manager present for a final warning.
  5. Ask for a signature and a response. If the employee declines, write “declined to sign” and have the witness initial it.
  6. Give a copy, file the original and follow up on the date you set. Praise real improvement; it is the goal.

If the employee is union-represented, they can ask for a union representative at an investigatory meeting that they reasonably believe could lead to discipline, under the NLRB’s Weingarten rule. If an employee keeps missing the mark after a final warning, the termination letter templates help you close it out properly, and employee write-up forms cover incident-specific documentation.

US rules to know before you issue a final written warning

These are general principles, checked against federal agency sources. State and local laws add more, so check your state labor department or a lawyer for your situation.

  • No federal law requires warnings. Most US employment is at will, so warnings are a policy choice. If your handbook promises progressive discipline, follow it, or say clearly that you may skip steps.
  • Protected leave cannot count against attendance. The Department of Labor says employers may not count FMLA leave under no-fault attendance policies or use it as a negative factor in discipline, so points given for FMLA days should be removed. Many state and city paid sick leave laws protect covered sick days the same way.
  • Retaliation is the biggest risk. The EEOC lists a reprimand or a lower-than-deserved evaluation as examples of possible retaliation when given because someone complained about discrimination or took part in an investigation. Time a warning carefully if the employee recently complained.
  • Talking about pay and conditions is protected. The NLRB says most private-sector employees, with or without a union, have the right to act together about wages and working conditions, and that employers cannot discipline them for it.
  • Keep the records. The EEOC requires covered employers to keep personnel records for one year, and longer when a charge is filed. Our guide to no call, no show policies covers documenting absences.

Frequently asked questions

What should an employee warning notice include?

The employee’s name, the date, the level of warning, the facts of what happened with dates, the policy or standard involved, any earlier warnings, exactly what must change and by when, the next step if it continues, a line for the employee’s response, and signatures confirming receipt.

Does an employee have to sign a written warning?

No law forces an employee to sign. The signature only confirms they received the warning, and the templates say so. If the employee refuses, write “Employee declined to sign” with the date, have a witness sign, and give them a copy anyway. A refusal does not make the warning invalid.

How many written warnings before termination?

No federal law sets a number. Many employers use a verbal warning, a written warning and a final written warning before termination, but at-will employers can skip steps for serious misconduct. Whatever you choose, write it in your handbook and apply it the same way to everyone in similar situations.

What is a final written warning?

It is the last step before termination in a progressive discipline process. It lists the earlier warnings, describes the latest incident, sets clear expectations and says that another occurrence may lead to termination. It should also invite the employee to raise anything, such as a health issue, that is affecting their work.

Can an employee be warned for an absence covered by FMLA or sick leave?

No. The Department of Labor says employers may not count FMLA leave under no-fault attendance policies or use it as a negative factor in discipline. Many state and city paid sick leave laws protect covered sick days the same way. Remove those absences before you count attendance points.

How long does a written warning stay on file?

Keep it at least as long as the law requires for personnel records, which the EEOC sets at one year for covered employers, and longer if a charge or lawsuit is pending. Many policies say a warning stops counting toward the next step after 6 or 12 months without repeat issues, while the record stays in the file.
