How to use these attendance tracker templates

  1. Write the policy first. A tracker only works if everyone knows the rules: how to call out, how late counts as late, and what happens at each step. Start from our attendance policy guide.
  2. Choose one record and one detail sheet. For most teams that is the monthly attendance sheet (or the yearly tracker per person) plus the tardy log or the absence report.
  3. Use the same codes everywhere. V, S, P, H, U, L, E and F mean the same thing on every sheet, so totals line up and payroll can update PTO balances directly.
  4. Record on the day. Enter absences and late arrivals the day they happen, from the schedule and the time records, not from memory at month end.
  5. Review monthly and act consistently. Check totals once a month, apply the policy the same way to everyone, and write down every coaching talk or warning.

Spreadsheets are fine for one location. Once you are reconciling sign-in sheets, schedules and timesheets every week, the work moves to time and attendance software that does the comparison automatically. Our overview of time and attendance tracking explains the options.

US rules to know before you track attendance

These principles are checked against Department of Labor fact sheets and federal regulations. States and cities add their own leave laws, so check your state labor department as well.

  • Protected leave cannot count against attendance. The Department of Labor lists assessing negative attendance points for FMLA leave use as an example of an FMLA violation. The FMLA covers private employers with 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks, and eligible employees get up to 12 workweeks of job-protected leave in a 12-month period. Many state and city paid sick leave laws protect covered absences too. Read the FMLA leave guide for eligibility.
  • Pay hourly employees for time worked. Late arrivals reduce paid time only by the time not worked. The attendance tracker should not be used to dock more than that, and your state may restrict other wage deductions.
  • Exempt employees work differently. Federal rules say an exempt employee must receive the full salary for any week in which they perform any work. Deductions for personal absences are allowed only in full days: an employee out for a day and a half can be docked for the one full day only.
  • Attendance records are time records. The FLSA requires employers to keep time cards and the records behind wage calculations for two years and payroll records for three. FMLA leave records must be kept for no less than three years.
  • Keep medical details out of the tracker. Record “S” or “F”, not a diagnosis. Medical certifications for FMLA belong in a separate confidential file.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track employee attendance in Excel?

Use one row per employee or month and one column per day, and type a short code for anything other than a normal workday: V for vacation, S for sick, L for late and so on. COUNTIF formulas total each code. Our yearly attendance tracker template does this for you, shades weekends and updates the calendar when you change the year.

Can FMLA absences count against an attendance policy?

No. The Department of Labor lists assessing negative attendance points for FMLA leave use as an example of an FMLA violation. Many state and city paid sick leave laws also protect covered absences. Record protected leave with its own code, F in our templates, so it scores zero and never triggers discipline.

Can I dock pay when an employee is late?

For hourly employees, you pay for the time actually worked, so 15 minutes late usually means 15 fewer paid minutes, and state rules on rounding and deductions apply. Exempt employees are different: they must receive their full salary for any week they do any work, and personal absences can be deducted only in full days.

How long should employers keep attendance records?

Attendance records that show hours worked are time records, which the FLSA requires you to keep for two years, with payroll records kept for three. Records of FMLA leave must be kept for at least three years. Keep attendance warnings in the personnel file, and check your state for longer retention periods.

What is the difference between an attendance tracker and a timesheet?

A timesheet records the hours someone worked so you can pay them. An attendance tracker records whether they showed up as scheduled: absences, late arrivals, early departures and leave. Hourly employers usually need both. The timesheet feeds payroll, and the attendance tracker feeds your attendance policy and PTO balances.
