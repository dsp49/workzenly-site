Workzenly makes time clock, scheduling, PTO and payroll software, and it appears in this list as one option. We researched every product on its own website on November 7, 2026. No company paid to be included. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s site before you buy.

Most people looking for Gusto alternatives are happy with the idea of Gusto and want one specific thing it does not give them: a lower price, multi-state payroll on the entry plan, accounting in the same subscription, a bigger provider with 24/7 support, or a time clock built for hourly staff. This list is for US small business owners with 1 to 50 employees who are comparing Gusto competitors before switching or signing up.

First, a fair summary of Gusto. It is online payroll, benefits and HR for small US businesses, with published prices on November 7, 2026: Simple at $49 a month plus $6 per person for payroll in one state, Plus at $80 plus $12 per person with time tracking, scheduling and multi-state payroll, and Premium at $180 plus $22 per person with a named advisor and HR guidance. Every plan includes unlimited payrolls, tax filings, and employer and employee mobile apps. It is a good product, which is why every payroll like Gusto on this list is measured against it.

OnPay is the closest like-for-like Gusto alternative: an online, full-service payroll service for small US businesses with HR, benefits and time tracking you can add. The difference is structure. Instead of Gusto’s three tiers, OnPay sells one payroll plan and lets you add modules.

On November 7, 2026, OnPay’s pricing calculator listed payroll at a $49 base plus $6 per worker, the same entry price as Gusto Simple, although the page footer said pricing starts at $55, so confirm when you sign up. The key difference: OnPay says all 50 states are included at no extra cost, while Gusto Simple runs payroll in one state and multi-state payroll starts on Gusto Plus at $80 plus $12 per person. OnPay includes unlimited pay runs, multiple pay rates, W-2s, 1099s and year-end filings. HR (onboarding with e-signatures, PTO policies, org charts and document storage) is a $15 base plus $2 per worker, and time tracking is $15 plus $4 per worker. New customers get one month free and free data migration from their old provider.

Strengths

  • All 50 states included in full-service tax filing
  • One payroll plan with unlimited pay runs
  • Free data migration and one free month

Drawbacks

  • HR and time tracking cost extra
  • No dedicated mobile app mentioned on its site
  • Fewer built-in HR features than Gusto Plus or Paycor

Standard is $29 a month plus $5 per employee billed yearly, or $39 plus $6 billed monthly, against Gusto Simple’s $49 plus $6 per person. Gusto vs. the alternatives: a switching checklist

  1. Switch at a quarter boundary if you can. Starting with a new provider on January 1, April 1, July 1 or October 1 means the new provider files a full quarter of Form 941, and you avoid splitting year-to-date figures. If you switch mid-quarter, ask the new provider how it imports your year-to-date totals.
  2. Export everything from Gusto first. Download pay stubs, payroll registers, tax filings and W-2s. The Department of Labor requires payroll records for at least three years, and the IRS says to keep employment tax records for at least four.
  3. Re-check tax accounts. Your federal EIN, state withholding number and state unemployment account move with you. Each new state needs registration.
  4. Rebuild pay rules carefully. Non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek under the FLSA, and states such as California add daily overtime. Test one pay run with our overtime calculator before you go live.
  5. Move time tracking with payroll. If you used Gusto’s time tools, plan how hours will reach the new payroll. A time clock connected to payroll avoids retyping.
  6. Tell employees what changes. New logins, new pay stub layout, maybe a new direct deposit form. Our guide to reading a pay stub is useful to share, and new hires will still need a W-4 form on file.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Gusto?

For most small businesses, OnPay is the closest alternative to Gusto: it lists the same $49 base plus $6 per worker and says all 50 states are included. Zoho Payroll is cheaper at $29 plus $5 per employee for one state when billed yearly. ADP RUN and Paychex Flex suit owners who want a large provider, and Justworks suits teams that want PEO benefits.

Is there a cheaper payroll like Gusto?

Yes. Zoho Payroll lists its Standard plan at $29 a month plus $5 per employee billed yearly, compared with Gusto Simple at $49 plus $6 per person. Square Payroll is $35 plus $6 per person. For 10 employees in one state, Zoho comes to $79 a month and Gusto Simple to $109, based on prices published on November 7, 2026.

Gusto vs. ADP: which is better for a small business?

Gusto publishes its prices, runs on a simple online setup and includes employer and employee apps on every plan. ADP RUN prices by quote, offers four packages that add HR support step by step, and lists 24/7 payroll support. Pick Gusto for price clarity and ease, and ADP if you want a large provider with built-in HR help.

Is it hard to switch from Gusto to another payroll provider?

It takes planning more than effort. Export your payroll registers, tax filings and pay stubs from Gusto, give the new provider your year-to-date totals and tax account numbers, and run a test payroll. Switching at the start of a calendar quarter keeps quarterly Form 941 filings simple. Many Gusto alternatives, including OnPay, offer free data migration.

Which Gusto competitor is best for hourly employees?

Look for a service where the time clock feeds payroll directly. Square Payroll imports timecards and tips from Square POS, Workzenly builds payroll on its own time clock, and Paycor’s employee app includes clocking in. Gusto includes time tracking on its Plus plan, at $80 a month plus $12 per person.
