Workable is a full applicant tracking system with HR tools attached. It fits a small company that hires several roles a quarter, wants to post widely and source candidates who are not applying, and may later add Workable’s separate HR package for time off and employee records.

Pricing is set by company size. For 1 to 20 employees, Standard is $299 a month ($3,588 a year) and Premier is $599 a month, which adds texting, video interviews, assessments and referrals. Every plan posts to more than 200 job boards, includes a careers page builder and a jobs widget for your site, two-way calendar sync with Gmail or Microsoft 365, and self-scheduled interviews. Workable also searches passive candidate profiles and offers AI screening and sourcing that use credits; every plan includes 3,000 credits, and more cost from $600 for 5,000. The free mobile app runs on iOS and Android; its time-off features need the HR package.

Strengths

  • Posting to 200+ job boards and a careers page builder on every plan
  • Candidate sourcing and AI screening built in
  • Free iOS and Android app for the hiring team

Drawbacks

  • $299 a month is a lot for a business that hires twice a year
  • Texting and video interviews need Premier or paid add-ons
  • AI features run on credits that expire after a year

Breezy HR is an ATS priced per company rather than per user or per job. That makes it a good fit for a small business where several managers review candidates and the number of open roles goes up and down through the year.

Every plan includes unlimited open positions and unlimited users. Startup is $157 a month billed yearly or $189 month to month, and includes posting to more than 50 job boards, a branded career site, candidate automations, questionnaires, interview self-scheduling, video interviews and email and calendar sync. Growth ($273 a month yearly) adds multiple pipelines, scorecards, employee referrals, automated reference checks and e-signatures. Business ($439 a month yearly) adds offer management, approvals and HRIS integrations. Text messaging starts at $41 a month and an onboarding add-on at $49 a month. Breezy says plainly that it has no free plan.

How to choose an applicant tracking system

Count your hires. Look at how many people you hired in the last 12 months, how many were hourly, and how many managers took part. That one number decides most of this: a few hires a year points to free tools or JazzHR, steady salaried hiring points to Workable or Breezy HR, and constant hourly hiring points to Homebase or Workstream.

Buyer’s checklist for hiring software:

  • Where your applicants come from. Confirm the ATS posts to the boards your applicants use, usually Indeed and Google for hourly roles, and whether premium or sponsored posts cost extra.
  • Speed of response. Hourly applicants often take the first job that calls back. Texting, self-scheduled interviews and automatic replies matter more than scorecards for those roles.
  • Pay ranges in job posts. Several states, including Colorado, California, Washington and New York, require pay ranges in job postings, and more states keep adding similar laws. Check that the job template has a pay range field and use it everywhere.
  • Background checks and fair chance rules. Under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act, you need the candidate’s written permission before a background check through a screening company. Many states and cities also limit when you can ask about criminal history. Your ATS should keep consent forms with the candidate.
  • Records. Keep applications and hiring records for at least one year under EEOC rules. Make sure you can export them if you change systems.
  • Offer letters. If you send many offers, e-signature saves days. Several products only include it on higher plans. Our job offer letter templates work with any of them.
  • The hand-off to onboarding. The Form I-9 is completed after the offer is accepted, with Section 2 done within three business days of the start date. Plan how a hired candidate becomes an employee with a W-4, I-9 and a first shift. Our I-9 guide and employee onboarding guide cover the steps.

In the trial, post one real job, apply to it yourself from a phone, and time how long it takes to get from application to a scheduled interview. That test tells you more than any feature grid about which is the best applicant tracking system for small business hiring like yours.

Frequently asked questions

What is an applicant tracking system?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is hiring software that posts your job to job boards and your careers page, collects every application in one place, and moves candidates through stages such as screening, interview and offer. It keeps notes, emails and interview feedback with each candidate, so nothing lives in a manager’s inbox.

Does a small business need an ATS?

If you hire more than a handful of people a year, or several managers review candidates, an ATS usually pays for itself in time saved and applicants not lost. If you hire one or two people a year, free tools such as the Homebase hiring dashboard or a careful spreadsheet may be enough.

Is there a free applicant tracking system for small business?

Homebase includes free hiring tools on every plan, including its free plan: job posting to Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Google, Facebook and Craigslist, an applicant dashboard, a careers page and interview scheduling. Most of the full ATS products in this list offer free trials of 7 to 15 days instead of free plans.

How much does an ATS cost for a small business?

Published prices on November 7, 2026 ranged from $15 per user per month (Manatal Professional, yearly) and $1,000 a year (JazzHR Hero) to $157 a month (Breezy HR Startup, yearly) and $299 a month (Workable Standard for 1 to 20 employees). Workstream and Recruitee quote prices. Add costs for texting, background checks and premium job posts.

How long should I keep job applications?

EEOC rules require employers to keep personnel and employment records, which include applications and hiring records, for one year. If someone files a discrimination charge, keep every related record until the charge or lawsuit is resolved. Your ATS should let you export or retain candidate records for at least that long.
