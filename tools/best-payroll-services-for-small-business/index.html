The best payroll services for small business all do the same core job: pay employees by direct deposit, withhold and file federal, state and local payroll taxes, and send W-2s and 1099s at year-end. Where they differ is price, how they handle hourly time and tips, how much HR comes with them, and whether you can see the price before you call sales.

To find the best payroll services for small business, we checked 10 providers on their own websites on November 7, 2026. For most US teams of 1 to 50 people, Gusto is the safest all-round choice, OnPay is the simplest full-service price, and Zoho Payroll has the lowest published price for a payroll service that still files your taxes. If you run hourly shifts, look closely at how each one gets hours from the time clock into payroll, because that is where small business payroll mistakes usually start.

Gusto is online payroll, benefits and HR software built for small US businesses, and it is the name most owners hear first. It fits a team of 2 to 50 people that wants full-service payroll, an employee app, and the option to add health insurance and a 401(k) without switching providers later.

Pricing is published. Simple is $49 a month plus $6 per person and runs payroll in one state, with unlimited payrolls, tax filings and payments, and basic PTO policies. Plus, at $80 a month plus $12 per person, adds time tracking and scheduling, multi-state payroll, next-day pay and contractor invoice management. Premium, at $180 a month plus $22 per person, adds a named advisor, HR guidance and compliance tools. Every plan includes the Gusto employee app and the employer mobile app. Gusto bills US contractors at the per-person rate only in months you pay them. Add-ons include Next-Day pay on Simple ($15 a month plus $3 per person), Remote I-9 ($20 per submission) and Time & Attendance Plus ($6 per person).

Strengths

  • Published prices with no contract
  • Employee and employer mobile apps on every plan
  • Health insurance, 401(k) and workers’ comp available in the same account

Drawbacks

  • Simple covers payroll in one state only
  • Time tracking and scheduling start on the Plus plan or as an add-on
  • No free trial listed

How to choose a payroll service for your small business

All of the best payroll services for small business on this list file payroll taxes for you. The choice comes down to three things: what it costs at your headcount, how hours and tips get into payroll, and how much HR you want attached.

A buyer’s checklist for small business payroll

  1. Count your states. Each state where an employee works means state withholding, unemployment insurance and new-hire reporting. Some plans include one state and charge for more.
  2. Decide how hours arrive. Non-exempt employees must get 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek under the FLSA, and some states, such as California, add daily overtime. A time clock connected to payroll removes manual entry. Our overtime calculator lets you spot-check the math.
  3. Plan for tips. Restaurants need tip import and tip credit handling. Check that tipped employees still reach at least minimum wage each workweek.
  4. Check new-hire paperwork. Look for e-signed W-4s and I-9 support. Our guides to the W-4 form and new hire paperwork list what each new employee must complete.
  5. Choose payroll service or PEO. A payroll service runs pay and taxes. A PEO such as Justworks also co-employs your team, which can open up better health insurance at a much higher price per person.
  6. Look at the employee side. Employees should see pay stubs, W-2s and PTO balances on their phones. Our guide to reading a pay stub is a good one to share with new hires.
  7. Read the promotion terms. Free months often require a one-year agreement or a first payroll by a set date.

If you are new to running payroll, read how to process payroll first, so you know what each service is doing on your behalf.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best payroll service for a small business?

For most US small businesses, Gusto is the best all-round choice, with published prices from $49 a month plus $6 per person and mobile apps for employers and employees. OnPay suits owners who want one flat plan with all 50 states included. Zoho Payroll has the lowest published full-service price, and Square Payroll suits businesses already using Square POS.

What is the cheapest payroll service that files taxes?

Among the services we checked, Zoho Payroll lists the lowest full-service price at $29 a month plus $5 per employee billed yearly, which is $79 a month for 10 employees in one state. Billed monthly it is $39 plus $6. Square Payroll is $35 plus $6 per person. Prices were taken from each vendor’s site on November 7, 2026, before promotions.

How much do payroll services cost for a small business?

Entry-level full-service plans we checked cost a monthly base fee of $29 to $50 plus $5 to $8 per employee, or about $79 to $130 a month for 10 people. Higher tiers cost more: Gusto Plus is $200 a month for 10. ADP, Paychex and Rippling quote prices individually, and PEOs such as Justworks charge $79 or more per employee.

Do payroll services handle state and local taxes?

Full-service plans calculate, deposit and file federal, state and local payroll taxes, including state unemployment insurance in most cases. Check how many states are included: Gusto Simple and Zoho Payroll Standard cover one state, and OnPay says all 50 states are included. Basic or do-it-yourself plans only calculate taxes.

What is the difference between a payroll service and a PEO?

A payroll service pays your employees and files payroll taxes in your company’s name. A PEO becomes a co-employer, handles payroll and taxes under its own accounts, and pools your team with other clients to buy health insurance and other benefits. PEOs usually cost much more per employee but can lower benefit costs for small teams.
