The best free employee schedule maker for most teams is Sling, which lets up to 30 people see the schedule, request time off, offer shifts and pick up open shifts at no cost. An employee schedule maker is software that turns your shifts, roles and availability into a weekly schedule your employees can see on their phones. It differs from a printable template in three ways: changes notify everyone automatically, employees can swap shifts or request time off themselves, and the tool can warn you about overtime before you publish. This list is for US managers of hourly teams who want a free schedule maker to start and a clear path if they outgrow it. If you just need a one-off printable sheet, use our free employee schedule templates instead.

Sling is the free schedule maker to try first. You build the week on a desktop grid, copy it forward, and publish it to employees’ phones, where they request time off, offer shifts and claim open shifts. How to choose a shift schedule maker

The right employee schedule maker depends less on features than on the limits you will hit first. Check these before you invite your team.

  1. Headcount on the free plan. Limits range from 5 (Findmyshift) to 30 users (Sling). Count everyone you schedule, including part-timers and seasonal staff.
  2. How far ahead you can schedule. Some free plans plan only 1 or 2 weeks out. If you operate under a predictive scheduling law, that matters: Oregon requires large retail, hospitality and food service employers to post schedules 14 days ahead, and New York City requires 14 days for fast food and 72 hours for retail.
  3. Templates and repeating shifts. If most weeks look the same, you want to copy last week or apply a saved template in one click. Rotations such as the Pitman schedule or DuPont schedule are easier with repeating patterns.
  4. Overtime warnings. Non-exempt employees earn time and a half after 40 hours in a workweek under federal law. A maker that totals hours per person as you build stops overtime before it is scheduled.
  5. Swaps and time off. Letting employees trade shifts with manager approval removes most of the texting. Check that approved swaps update the schedule and notify both people.
  6. A time clock, now or later. A schedule tells you who should work; punches tell you who did. If you will add a time clock later, pick a maker that already has one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free employee schedule maker?

Sling is the best free employee schedule maker for most teams because it covers up to 30 users with open shifts, shift offers, time-off requests and private messaging at no cost. Homebase is better if you want a free time clock too at one location with up to 10 employees, and Connecteam includes every feature free for up to 10 users.

Is there a free work schedule maker with no sign-up?

Most schedule makers that send shifts to employees’ phones need an account, because each employee has to log in. If you only need a printable grid without signing up, a spreadsheet template works. Our free employee schedule templates download as Excel and PDF files you can fill in and print or share.

Can a schedule maker create the schedule automatically?

Some can. When I Work includes auto-scheduling on its cheapest plan, Homebase offers an AI scheduling assistant on Plus, ZoomShift on Premium, and Connecteam auto-assigns shifts on Expert. They fill shifts from availability, roles and rules. Free plans generally do not include auto-scheduling, so you build the first week by hand and copy it forward.

What should an employee schedule include?

Each shift needs the employee’s name, date, start and end time, role or position and location. Add unpaid break length, the total hours per person for the week, and contact or swap instructions. Showing weekly totals helps you spot anyone near 40 hours, where federal overtime at 1.5 times the regular rate begins.

How far in advance should I post the schedule?

Two weeks is a good default and is required in some places. Oregon requires 14 days for large retail, hospitality and food service employers, Chicago requires 14 days in covered industries, and New York City requires 14 days for fast food and 72 hours for retail. Elsewhere, posting a week or more ahead cuts no-shows and swap requests.
