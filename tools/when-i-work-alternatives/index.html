Workzenly makes time clock, scheduling, PTO and payroll software, and it appears in this list as one option. We researched every product on its own website on November 7, 2026. No company paid to be included. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s site before you buy.

The best When I Work alternatives for most US small businesses are Homebase if you want a free plan, Sling if you want the lowest per-user price, Connecteam for teams in the field, and 7shifts for restaurants. This list compares nine apps like When I Work on what they cost, whether they include a time clock, and whether they can run payroll.

When I Work is shift scheduling software for hourly teams. Its Essentials plan costs $2.50 per user per month and includes auto scheduling, shift swaps, OpenShifts and team messaging. Time tracking and attendance is an add-on you switch on, and there is no built-in payroll: it connects to payroll providers and promotes a partner offer with Rippling. There is a 14-day free trial but no free plan.

People look for When I Work competitors for four common reasons: they want a free plan for a small team, they want payroll in the same app as the schedule, the per-user bill grows with a large part-time staff, or they run a restaurant and want tip pooling. Homebase is the closest swap for a single-location shop that wants to stop paying per user. Its free Basic plan covers scheduling and time tracking for one location and up to 10 employees, and paid plans are priced per location with unlimited employees instead of per head. That makes the bill predictable for a store or cafe with a large part-time roster.

Where When I Work leans on partners for payroll, Homebase sells its own payroll add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid, fed by the same timesheets. The app also covers hiring, onboarding and team messaging. Clock-ins can run on a phone or a shared tablet, with PIN, photo and GPS options.

Strengths

  • Free Basic plan with scheduling and time tracking for one location and up to 10 employees
  • Paid plans are priced per location with unlimited employees
  • Payroll add-on for $49/month plus $6 per employee paid
  • Hiring, team messaging, onboarding and HR tools in the same app

Drawbacks

  • The free plan stops at 10 employees and one location
  • PTO controls, departments and permissions start on the Plus plan ($56 per location/month billed annually)
  • Payroll, Tip Manager ($25) and Task Manager ($13) cost extra each month

Sling, now part of Toast, is the budget pick. Its free plan handles shift scheduling, time-off requests, available shifts and messaging for up to 30 users, which is a much larger free team than most rivals allow. Premium costs $1.70 per user per month on annual billing, below When I Work’s $2.50 entry price.

Premium adds the time clock with geofencing, timesheets and labor cost tools such as overtime tracking and budgets. Business adds kiosk clock-in, attendance reports, PTO management and task lists. Sling does not run payroll, so you export hours to a payroll provider as you would from When I Work.

Strengths

  • Free plan for up to 30 users with shift scheduling, time-off requests and messaging
  • Paid plans from $1.70 per user/month billed annually
  • Labor cost tracking, overtime tracking and budgeting on Premium
  • Now part of Toast, the restaurant point-of-sale company

Drawbacks

  • Time clock, geofencing and timesheets need the paid Premium plan
  • Kiosk clock-in, attendance reports and PTO management are on the Business plan
  • No built-in payroll processing

Connecteam suits crews that work away from one front door: cleaning, home care, construction and field service. Its time clock records GPS-verified clock-ins, lets you geofence each job site and offers a kiosk app for a shared tablet. Scheduling includes shift templates, open shifts, swaps and AI auto-scheduling that considers availability and labor laws.

How to choose an app like When I Work

The right choice among When I Work alternatives usually comes down to billing, the time clock and payroll. Start with how you are billed. Per-user pricing (When I Work, Sling, Deputy, Buddy Punch) is cheap for small teams and grows with every seasonal hire. Per-location pricing (Homebase, 7shifts) favors one busy store with many part-timers. Flat team pricing (Connecteam) sits in between. Multiply each quote by your real headcount, including managers, before you compare.

Then work through this checklist with your own schedule open:

  • Overtime alerts. Under the federal FLSA, non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The scheduler should warn you before you publish a shift that pushes someone past 40. California also has daily overtime after 8 hours, covered in our guide to California overtime law.
  • Breaks. There is no federal meal-break rule, but several states require them. Check that the app can schedule and track unpaid breaks the way your state requires.
  • Predictive scheduling. Oregon and cities including Seattle, San Francisco, New York City, Chicago and Philadelphia have fair workweek rules for certain employers, with advance notice and premium pay for late changes. If you operate there, ask how the app records notice dates and schedule changes.
  • Time clock and records. The FLSA requires you to keep payroll records for at least three years and time cards for two. Look for GPS or geofenced clock-ins if staff start at job sites, and a shared tablet kiosk if they share a front door. Our list of free time clock apps covers clock-only options.
  • Payroll path. Decide whether hours should export to your current payroll provider or run through payroll in the same app. Fewer hand-offs mean fewer missed punches on pay day.
  • Templates. If you are still on spreadsheets, try a free weekly schedule template first to map your shifts, then load that pattern into any app on this list during its trial.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to When I Work?

Homebase and Sling are the strongest free options. Homebase’s free Basic plan covers scheduling and time tracking for one location and up to 10 employees. Sling’s free plan covers scheduling, time-off requests and messaging for up to 30 users, but its time clock needs a paid plan. Connecteam is free for up to 10 users.

When I Work vs Homebase: which is cheaper?

It depends on headcount. When I Work charges per user, from $2.50 per user per month, so 20 employees cost about $50 a month before the time tracking add-on. Homebase charges per location, with Essentials at $24 per location per month on annual billing for unlimited employees, and a free plan for up to 10 employees at one location.

Does When I Work include payroll?

No. When I Work connects to payroll and POS systems through integrations and promotes a partner offer with Rippling, but it does not run payroll itself. If you want payroll in the same app as the schedule, Homebase, 7shifts, Deputy, Buddy Punch and Gusto sell payroll as a plan or add-on, and Workzenly includes payroll with scheduling and the time clock.

Which When I Work competitor is best for restaurants?

7shifts is built only for restaurants, with tip management, labor compliance tools, a manager log book and a US payroll add-on. Its free Comp plan covers one location with up to 15 employees. Sling, now owned by Toast, and Homebase are also widely used by restaurants and cost less for very small teams.

Can I move my schedule from When I Work to another app?

Most apps let you import employees from a spreadsheet, so export your staff list and positions from When I Work first. Shift history usually does not carry over, so rebuild one typical week as a template in the new app, run both apps side by side for a pay period, and compare hours before you cancel.
