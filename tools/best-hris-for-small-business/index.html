The best HRIS for small business depends on what your HR work really is. If it is mostly employee records, time off and onboarding, a dedicated HRIS like BambooHR fits. If it is mostly payroll, start with Gusto. If you want health benefits and someone else handling compliance, look at a PEO such as Justworks. If most of your people work hourly shifts, the HRIS has to live where they clock in.

We compared seven HRIS systems for small companies in the United States, from 5 to about 100 employees. For each one we checked the pricing page, the free plan or trial, the platforms and the HR features that matter on day one: employee records, onboarding with I-9 and W-4 paperwork, PTO policies, documents and the link to payroll. BambooHR is an HRIS built around a central employee record. It suits a small business that has outgrown spreadsheets and shared folders and wants one place for personnel files, time off, hiring and onboarding, with payroll as an option rather than the center of the product.

The Core plan includes employee records, HR reporting, job posting and candidate records, offer letter templates, time-off tracking with a time-off calendar and unlimited custom policies, and benefits tracking. Pro adds performance management with 360-degree review cycles and one-on-ones. Pricing is unusual for small teams: companies with 25 or fewer employees pay a flat monthly rate ($250 for Core, $425 for Pro, $650 for Elite), and larger companies pay per employee, from $10 per employee per month on Core. Payroll is an add-on for US employees, with a 15% discount when you bundle it with benefits administration.

Strengths

  • Unlimited custom time-off policies and a shared time-off calendar on Core
  • Job postings, candidate records and offer letter templates on Core
  • iOS and Android apps for employees and managers

Drawbacks

  • The $250 flat rate applies even to a team of five or six
  • Payroll is a paid add-on and its price is not published
  • Performance management needs the Pro plan

Gusto is a payroll company that grew into a light HRIS. If your main HR job is paying people correctly and filing payroll taxes, and you want employee profiles, onboarding and PTO attached to that, Gusto is a common starting point for US small businesses.

The Simple plan costs $49 a month plus $6 per person and covers unlimited payrolls in one state, tax filings and payments, and basic PTO policies and holiday pay. Plus ($80 a month plus $12 per person) adds time tracking and scheduling, multi-state payroll and next-day pay. Premium ($180 a month plus $22 per person) adds a named advisor and HR guidance. Time-off policies can accrue by hours worked or a fixed amount, requests come in from the web or the mobile app, and approved time off syncs to payroll. For companies created on or after August 31, 2026, Simple allows only accrual-based policies, and unlimited PTO needs Plus or Premium.

Strengths

  • Payroll, tax filing and PTO accruals work from the same data
  • Published plan prices on its own site
  • Approved time off flows into the next payroll automatically

Drawbacks

  • Simple covers payroll in one state only
  • Time tracking and scheduling are on Plus and above
  • New Simple accounts cannot set up unlimited PTO policies

Justworks sells two things: payroll software with HR tools, and a PEO (professional employer organization) that co-employs your staff so a small company can offer large-group health benefits and hand off compliance work. It suits a small business that wants health insurance and HR support more than it wants a deep HRIS.

The Payroll plan is $50 a month plus $8 per employee and includes payroll, HR tools, expert support and access to time tracking. PEO Basic is $79 per employee per month with no base fee and adds HR consulting, 24/7 support, 401(k) and an applicant tracking system. PEO Plus, at $124 per employee per month, adds medical, dental and vision administration, HSA and FSA accounts and other benefits. The PEO price is all-in for the service, which makes budgeting simple, but it is high compared with software alone.

Strengths

Drawbacks

  • PEO Plus at $124 per employee per month costs more than software-only HRIS tools
  • Fewer HR configuration options than a dedicated HRIS
  • Leaving a PEO later means moving benefits and payroll at the same time

How to choose an HRIS for your small business

Start with where your HR time goes today. Write down the five tasks that take the most hours each month (new hire paperwork, PTO questions, payroll changes, chasing signatures, answering “how much vacation do I have?”) and judge every HRIS on those, not on the longest feature list.

Buyer’s checklist for HRIS software:

  • Payroll in or out. Decide whether the HRIS should run payroll or feed a provider you keep. Every manual hand-off is a chance to pay someone wrong. Our guide to how to process payroll shows where the hand-offs usually break.
  • States you pay in. If you have remote staff or a second location in another state, confirm multi-state payroll and state tax registration are included or priced. Gusto Simple, for example, covers one state.
  • Onboarding paperwork. The system should collect Form W-4 and the I-9, which the employer must complete within three business days of the start date, plus your state withholding form and e-signed policies. See our new hire paperwork checklist.
  • PTO and sick leave rules. Many states and cities require paid sick leave with their own accrual rates and carryover rules. Check that the HRIS can run more than one policy, accrue by hours worked for hourly staff, and show employees their balance. Our paid time off guide covers the common policy designs.
  • Exempt and non-exempt employees. If you have hourly, non-exempt staff, the HRIS or its time clock must track hours for FLSA overtime. Misclassifying someone can mean back pay for unpaid overtime; see exempt vs non-exempt.
  • Records you must keep. The FLSA requires payroll records for at least three years, and EEOC rules require keeping personnel records for one year. Make sure documents stay searchable and exportable if you ever leave the vendor.
  • The employee app. Deskless and hourly staff will only use an HRIS on their phone. Ask for the employee app in the demo, not just the admin screens.
  • Real total cost. Price it at your headcount a year from now: base fee, per-employee fee, add-ons such as time tracking or remote I-9, and any implementation fee.

Finally, run one real scenario in the trial or demo: hire a fake employee, onboard them, give them a PTO policy, approve a request and see it on the next pay run. The product that makes that flow shortest is usually the best HRIS for small business teams like yours, whatever its feature list says.

Frequently asked questions

What is an HRIS?

An HRIS (human resources information system) is software that stores employee records and runs the HR work attached to them: onboarding paperwork, time off, benefits enrollment, documents and reports. Many small business HRIS tools also run payroll or connect to it, so a pay change or new hire is entered once instead of in several places.

Does a small business need an HRIS?

Most US businesses start to need one at around 10 to 20 employees, when paper files, PTO spreadsheets and emailed forms start causing errors. Signs you need one: you cannot quickly answer how much PTO someone has, I-9s and W-4s live in different places, or you re-type the same employee data into payroll and benefits.

How much does an HRIS cost for a small business?

On November 7, 2026, published prices ran from free (Connecteam for up to 10 users, Homebase Basic for one small location) to $250 a month flat for BambooHR Core up to 25 employees. Payroll-first tools like Gusto start at $49 a month plus $6 per person. PEO plans cost far more because they include benefits and HR services.

What is the difference between an HRIS and a PEO?

An HRIS is software you run yourself. A PEO, such as Justworks PEO, co-employs your staff: it runs payroll and taxes under its own accounts, gives you access to its group benefits, and provides HR support. A PEO costs more per employee, and you share some employer responsibilities with it. Many small businesses start with an HRIS and add a broker for benefits.

Is there a free HRIS for small business?

Yes, with limits. Connecteam is free for up to 10 users with every feature, and Homebase Basic is free for one location with up to 10 employees but leaves out PTO balances and HR tools. Free plans rarely include payroll, so budget for a payroll service and check how hours and time off move into it.
