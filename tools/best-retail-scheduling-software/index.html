How to choose retail scheduling software

Picking the best retail scheduling software comes down to store count, how predictable your traffic is and whether a scheduling law applies to you. Use this checklist.

  1. Fair workweek and predictive scheduling. New York City requires retail employers to give written schedules at least 72 hours before the schedule starts and limits last-minute changes. Chicago’s Fair Workweek covers retailers with at least 100 employees worldwide and requires 14 days’ notice. Oregon requires 14 days’ notice from retail employers with 500 or more employees and a 10-hour rest between shifts unless the employee agrees. Covered retailers need software that tracks notice periods and premiums.
  2. Forecasting. Schedules built on sales or foot traffic stop overstaffing on quiet Tuesdays. Check whether forecasts come from your POS, door counters or history, and at what level (store, department, hour).
  3. Overtime and holidays. Hours climb in November and December. Non-exempt associates earn 1.5 times their regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek, and California adds daily overtime after 8 hours. Holiday premiums are a policy choice in most states; our holiday pay guide explains the rules.
  4. Teen workers. Federal rules limit 14- and 15-year-olds to 3 hours on a school day and 18 hours in a school week, between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (9 p.m. from June 1 to Labor Day). Your software should flag these before you publish.
  5. Swaps and call-outs. Associates swap shifts constantly. Approval rules, open-shift posting and a clear no-call, no-show policy keep coverage steady.
  6. Time clock and payroll. Clock-in at the POS or a tablet, with hours that flow into payroll, removes the weekly export. See our time and attendance software picks if attendance is your biggest gap.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best retail scheduling software?

Deputy is the best retail scheduling software for most multi-store retailers because it pairs demand-based auto-scheduling with break planning and fair workweek tools, priced per user from $5 a month. Homebase is the best free option for a single store with up to 10 employees, and Legion suits large chains that need demand forecasting across many stores.

Is there free retail scheduling software?

Yes. Homebase is free for one store with up to 10 employees, Sling schedules up to 30 users free, and Connecteam is free for up to 10 users with all features. Free plans cover building and sharing schedules. Labor cost tools, sales forecasting and multiple stores usually need a paid plan.

Does retail scheduling software handle fair workweek laws?

Some tools do. Deputy offers a fair workweek feature and Workforce.com sells a Fair Workweek solution, built for cities such as New York City and Chicago that require advance schedules and premiums for late changes. Most small-business apps do not calculate predictability pay, so covered retailers should confirm the exact rules each tool supports.

How far in advance must retail schedules be posted?

There is no federal rule. New York City requires retail schedules at least 72 hours before they start. Chicago requires 14 days’ notice for covered retailers with 100 or more employees, and Oregon requires 14 days for retail employers with 500 or more employees. Check your state and city for local rules.

How much does retail scheduling software cost?

Per-user tools run from $1.70 (Sling Premium, annual) to $9 (Deputy Pro) per user per month. Homebase starts at $30 per store per month with unlimited employees, and Square Shifts comes with Square Plus at $49 per location. Enterprise platforms such as Legion and Workforce.com price by quote after a demo.
