Workzenly makes time clock, scheduling, PTO and payroll software, and it appears in this list as one option. We researched every product on its own website on November 7, 2026. No company paid to be included. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s site before you buy.

The best Deputy alternatives for most US small businesses are When I Work for a lower per-user price, Homebase or Sling for a free plan, Connecteam for field crews and 7shifts for restaurants. This page compares eight apps like Deputy, with prices and limits checked on each vendor’s website.

Deputy is workforce management software for shift-based businesses. Lite costs $5 per user per month and includes scheduling, timesheets, time clocking, labor law compliance, shift swaps and leave management. Core, at $6.50, adds auto-scheduling, demand forecasting, wage budgets and biometric clocking; Pro is $9. Monthly plans carry a $30 minimum per invoice. US payroll, enabled by Paycor, is an add-on for annual Core and Pro customers at $8 per user per month plus a $49 base fee. There is a free trial of up to 31 days and no free plan.

Most people searching for Deputy competitors want to pay less per user, want a free plan for a small team, or want payroll without an annual commitment and a separate add-on. Each tool below says which buyer it suits.

When I Work is the closest like-for-like swap at a lower price. Essentials costs $2.50 per user per month, half of Deputy Lite, and includes auto scheduling, schedule templates, forecast tools, multi-week and multi-location scheduling, OpenShifts, shift swaps and team messaging.

Pro, at $5, adds scheduling rules, role permissions, labor sharing and custom reporting. The gaps: time tracking and attendance is an add-on you switch on, and there is no built-in payroll, only integrations. The 14-day free trial includes everything, with no credit card required.

Strengths

  • Entry plan from $2.50 per user/month with unlimited users
  • Auto scheduling, schedule templates and multi-week, multi-location scheduling on Essentials
  • OpenShifts, shift swapping and in-app team messaging on every plan
  • 14-day free trial of everything, no credit card required

Drawbacks

  • No free plan
  • Time tracking and attendance is a separate add-on you switch on per plan
  • No built-in payroll; it connects to payroll providers instead

Homebase is the free option for a single location. Its Basic plan includes scheduling and time tracking for one location with up to 10 employees, which Deputy does not offer at any price. Clock-ins work on phones or a shared tablet with PIN, photo and GPS options.

Paid plans are per location with unlimited employees: Essentials is $24 per location per month on annual billing, and Plus at $56 adds PTO controls, departments and permissions. Payroll is an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid and works on every plan, without the annual-plan requirement Deputy sets for its payroll add-on.

Strengths

  • Free Basic plan with scheduling and time tracking for one location and up to 10 employees
  • Paid plans are priced per location with unlimited employees
  • Payroll add-on for $49/month plus $6 per employee paid
  • Hiring, team messaging, onboarding and HR tools in the same app

Drawbacks

  • The free plan stops at 10 employees and one location
  • PTO controls, departments and permissions start on the Plus plan ($56 per location/month billed annually)
  • Payroll, Tip Manager ($25) and Task Manager ($13) cost extra each month

Connecteam suits businesses whose staff clock in at job sites: cleaning, home care, security and construction. It offers GPS-verified clock-ins, geofenced work areas, a shared-tablet kiosk app, and scheduling with shift templates, open shifts, swaps and AI auto-scheduling that considers availability, roles and labor laws.

Pricing is flat for the first 30 users. The free Small Business plan covers up to 10 users with all hubs, limited to one time clock and one schedule. The Operations hub then starts at $29 a month on annual billing for 30 users, plus a fee for each extra user, which can cost less than Deputy’s per-user fee for a team of about 30. Chat and HR are separate hubs.

Strengths

  • Free Small Business plan for up to 10 users with all hubs, limited to one time clock and one schedule
  • GPS-verified clock-ins, geofenced work areas and a shared-tablet kiosk app
  • Shift templates, open shifts, shift swaps and AI auto-scheduling
  • Separate hubs for team chat, HR documents and training

Drawbacks

  • Priced per hub, so scheduling, chat and HR each add a subscription
  • The free plan ends at 10 users
  • Paid plans charge a flat fee for the first 30 users plus a fee for each extra user

Sling, now part of Toast, gives the largest free team on this list: shift scheduling, time-off requests, available shifts and messaging for up to 30 users at no cost. That makes it a practical Deputy alternative for a business that mainly needs the schedule published to phones.

Premium costs $1.70 per user per month on annual billing and adds the time clock with geofencing, timesheets, overtime tracking and labor budgets. Business, at $3.40, adds kiosk clock-in, attendance reports, PTO management and tasks. Sling does not run payroll and lacks Deputy’s compliance and forecasting depth.

Strengths

  • Free plan for up to 30 users with shift scheduling, time-off requests and messaging
  • Paid plans from $1.70 per user/month billed annually
  • Labor cost tracking, overtime tracking and budgeting on Premium
  • Now part of Toast, the restaurant point-of-sale company

Drawbacks

  • Time clock, geofencing and timesheets need the paid Premium plan
  • Kiosk clock-in, attendance reports and PTO management are on the Business plan
  • No built-in payroll processing

Paid plans are per location, not per user. How to choose between Deputy alternatives

Price your real team on each finalist. Deputy, When I Work, Sling and Buddy Punch charge per user; Homebase and 7shifts per location; Connecteam a flat fee for the first 30 users. Add any payroll, HR or messaging add-ons you would need, and check minimums and annual-only terms.

  • Compliance depth. Deputy’s strength is labor law compliance. List the rules you rely on, such as overtime warnings, minor work-hour limits or break rules, and test each one during the trial.
  • Overtime. The FLSA requires 1.5 times the regular rate for non-exempt hours over 40 in a workweek, and California adds daily overtime after 8 hours. Our guide to California overtime law covers daily and seventh-day rules.
  • Predictive scheduling. Oregon and cities such as Seattle, San Francisco, New York City, Chicago and Philadelphia require advance notice of schedules for some employers. Ask how the app logs posting dates and late changes.
  • Clock-in method. Deputy offers biometric clocking on Core. Alternatives usually use phone GPS, geofencing, PIN or photo on a shared tablet. Compare options in our list of free time clock apps.
  • Records and exports. Keep payroll records for three years and time cards for two, as the FLSA requires. Download Deputy timesheets before you cancel.
  • Payroll. Decide whether payroll should live in the scheduling app or stay with your current provider fed by exported hours.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cheaper alternative to Deputy?

When I Work starts at $2.50 per user per month, half of Deputy Lite’s $5, and Sling’s Premium plan is $1.70 per user per month on annual billing. Homebase and Sling also have free plans: Homebase for one location with up to 10 employees, and Sling for up to 30 users without the time clock.

Deputy vs When I Work: which is better for small teams?

When I Work costs less per user and includes auto scheduling, forecasting and shift swaps on its $2.50 Essentials plan, with time tracking as an add-on. Deputy costs more but includes time clocking and labor law compliance on every plan and offers a US payroll add-on. Small teams with simple rules often choose When I Work.

Does Deputy have a free plan?

No. Deputy offers a free trial of up to 31 days with no credit card required, then paid plans from $5 per user per month. Monthly plans carry a $30 minimum per invoice. If you need a free plan, look at Homebase, Sling, Connecteam or 7shifts, each with its own team-size limits.

Which Deputy competitors include payroll?

Homebase sells payroll on every plan for $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid. 7shifts has a US payroll add-on that is included in Premium. Gusto runs payroll with scheduling on its Plus plan. Workzenly includes payroll with scheduling, the time clock and PTO tracking.

How do I switch from Deputy to another scheduling app?

Export your employee list, pay rates and timesheets from Deputy, then import the staff list into the new app as a spreadsheet. Rebuild one typical week as a template, run both systems for one pay period to compare hours, and keep the exported records for at least three years.
