The best HR software for small business depends on what “HR” means at your company. For a 6-person office, it is onboarding paperwork and PTO. For a 40-person restaurant group, it is schedules, time clocks, breaks and pay. For a 120-person tech company, it is an HRIS with reviews, reports and workflows. This list covers all three, so you can find small business HR software that matches the work you actually do.

To find the best HR software for small business, we checked nine products on their own websites on November 7, 2026. If you need payroll too, start with Gusto. If you want a dedicated HRIS for small business, look at BambooHR or Rippling. If you want someone else to handle HR and benefits, a PEO such as Justworks fits. And if most of your team is hourly or deskless, Homebase, Connecteam and Workzenly are built around shifts rather than desks.

Gusto is best known for payroll, but for many small businesses it is also their first HR software. It suits a team of 2 to 50 people without an HR department that wants hiring, onboarding, benefits and pay in one account.

Gusto’s HR pages list custom onboarding checklists that issue documents, enroll new hires in benefits and even provision software, plus e-signatures, performance review templates and goal tracking, engagement surveys, an employee handbook builder, an applicant tracking system, background checks and training tools. How to choose HR software for a small business

The best HR software for small business is the one that removes the HR tasks you repeat every week. Most small businesses buy it to stop chasing paper. Start by listing the HR tasks that eat your week, then match them to the type of tool.

A buyer’s checklist with US rules in mind

  1. Onboarding forms. Every new employee completes a Form I-9 and a W-4. Look for e-signatures, I-9 support and state new-hire reporting. Our new hire paperwork checklist lists every form.
  2. Time off that matches your policy. Many states and cities require paid sick leave with specific accrual rates, so the PTO tool must handle accruals, caps and carryover. See our guide to paid time off, and the FMLA leave rules if you have 50 or more employees.
  3. Hours and overtime. If you have non-exempt staff, HR and payroll must agree on hours: the FLSA requires 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, and some states require meal and rest breaks. A time clock tied to payroll keeps them in sync.
  4. Records and retention. The Department of Labor requires payroll records for at least three years and time cards for two. Choose software that stores documents per employee and lets you export them.
  5. Performance and discipline. Even a small team needs written reviews and warnings. Check for review templates, or start with our guide to employee onboarding and build from there.
  6. Price at your headcount. A flat minimum such as BambooHR’s $250 a month for 25 or fewer employees changes the math for a 6-person team. Per-location pricing, as at Homebase, favors larger teams in one place.
  7. The employee app. Hourly staff will use HR software only if it is on their phone and does something they need, like showing schedules, PTO balances and pay stubs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best HR software for a small business?

It depends on your team. Gusto is the best fit for most small businesses that want HR built on payroll. BambooHR suits companies of 25 or more people that want a dedicated HRIS. Justworks suits teams that want a PEO to handle benefits and compliance. For hourly teams, Homebase and Workzenly connect HR with schedules and the time clock.

Is there free HR software for small business?

Yes. Connecteam’s Small Business Plan is free for life for up to 10 users and includes all of its hubs, including HR documents, onboarding and time off. Homebase’s Basic plan is free for one location with up to 10 employees, but it covers scheduling and time tracking rather than HR documents and onboarding.

How much does HR software cost for a small business?

Published prices we checked range from about $2 per worker on top of a $15 base for OnPay’s HR add-on, to $10 to $25 per employee for BambooHR, to $79 to $124 per employee for Justworks PEO plans. BambooHR charges a flat $250 a month for Core at 25 or fewer employees. Rippling and some others quote prices individually.

What is the difference between an HRIS and HR software?

An HRIS (human resources information system) is a type of HR software focused on the employee record: personal data, job and pay history, documents, reports and workflows. HR software is the broader term and can also include payroll, scheduling, time tracking, recruiting and performance reviews. Small businesses often get HRIS features inside a payroll platform.

Do small businesses need HR software?

Not always, but it helps once paperwork starts slipping. Employers must keep Form I-9 for every employee, track hours for non-exempt staff and keep payroll records for at least three years. HR software stores these records, sends reminders and gives employees self-service. Many owners start using it once they have more than a handful of employees.
