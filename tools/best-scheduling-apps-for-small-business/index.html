Workzenly makes time clock, scheduling, PTO and payroll software, and it appears in this list as one option. We researched every product on its own website on November 7, 2026. No company paid to be included. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s site before you buy.

The best scheduling app for small business is Homebase for most single-location shops, cafes and salons: it is free for one location with up to 10 employees, and after that you pay per location, not per person. If you have more than 10 people and no budget yet, Sling schedules up to 30 users free. For crews who work off-site, Connecteam is free for up to 10 users with every feature included.

This guide is for US owners and managers with roughly 5 to 50 hourly employees who are tired of texting the schedule or rebuilding a spreadsheet each week. Small business scheduling apps all do the basics: build a week, publish it to phones, take availability and time-off requests. What separates them is the monthly bill at your headcount, the cap on the free plan, and whether the time clock and payroll come with it. Homebase is built for exactly the business this page is about: one or a few local locations with hourly staff, such as a cafe, boutique, salon or clinic. The free Basic plan covers scheduling and clock-in for up to 10 employees, and at 11 or more you move to Essentials at $30 per location with unlimited employees. That makes it the cheapest paid option here for a busy single site.

Homebase prices by location, not by head, so paid plans cover unlimited employees. The schedule builder warns about overtime and conflicts before you publish, and it can show labor cost against a sales forecast pulled from Square, Clover, Shopify or Toast. Employees trade, cover and pick up open shifts from the app.

Strengths

  • Free plan for one location with up to 10 employees
  • Flat per-location price with unlimited employees on paid plans
  • POS sales sync from Square, Clover, Shopify and Toast
  • Payroll add-on in the same app ($49/month plus $6 per employee)

Drawbacks

  • The AI scheduling assistant needs the $70 Plus plan
  • Free plan stops at one location and 10 employees
  • Payroll costs extra on every plan

Sling is the strongest free scheduling app for small business if your problem is the schedule itself rather than timekeeping. Its free plan schedules up to 30 users with open shifts, shift offers, time-off requests and private messaging. When you want a time clock or labor cost alerts, Premium is $1.70 per user per month on annual billing, so 15 people cost about $25.50 a month.

Sling, now owned by Toast, lets employees on the free plan see their shifts, request time off, offer shifts to coworkers and pick up open shifts. Premium adds unavailability, shift swaps, group messaging, a mobile time clock with overtime tracking, and labor cost alerts when a shift breaks the budget. Business adds shift tasks, kiosk clock-in and labor reports.

Strengths

  • Free scheduling for up to 30 users
  • Low per-user price on paid plans
  • Labor cost and budget alerts as you schedule
  • Open shifts and time-off requests on the free plan

Drawbacks

  • Swaps, availability, time clock and labor cost need a paid plan
  • Kiosk clock-in only on Business ($3.40 per user, annual)
  • Sling recommends a desktop for building schedules

How to choose scheduling software for small business

To find the best scheduling app for small business teams like yours, work through these questions in order. Most owners can narrow the list to two apps in ten minutes.

  1. How many people, and how many locations? Under 10 people at one site: Homebase, Connecteam or Camelo are free. Up to 30: Sling is free. More than one location: per-location pricing (Homebase) or per-user pricing (When I Work, Deputy) usually wins over a flat-fee plan.
  2. Do you need a time clock? Free plans often leave it out. If you pay hourly staff, you need accurate punches anyway, because the FLSA requires you to keep records of hours worked for non-exempt employees. Compare our picks for the best employee time clock apps if timekeeping is the bigger problem.
  3. Will it warn you about overtime? Non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek, and California also pays daily overtime after 8 hours. A warning before you publish is cheaper than a surprise on payday. Our overtime calculator shows what an extra shift costs.
  4. Breaks and teenagers. Short breaks of 20 minutes or less must be paid under federal rules, many states require meal breaks, and 14- and 15-year-olds may work only 3 hours on a school day, between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (9 p.m. from June 1 to Labor Day). If you hire students, test how the app handles this.
  5. How does it reach payroll? Check whether hours export to your payroll provider, or whether payroll is built in. Re-typing hours is where most small-business payroll errors start; see how to process payroll for the steps.
  6. Will your team use it? During the trial, have two employees swap a shift and request a day off from their phones. If that takes more than a minute, keep looking.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best scheduling app for small business?

Homebase is the best scheduling app for most small businesses with one location, because it is free up to 10 employees and then $30 per location per month with unlimited employees. Sling is the best free option up to 30 users, Connecteam suits crews on job sites, and When I Work fits teams planning to grow or add locations.

What is the best free scheduling app for a small business?

Sling has the most generous free plan, covering scheduling and messaging for up to 30 users. ZoomShift is free for up to 20 users at one location, and Homebase, Connecteam and Camelo are free for up to 10. Free plans usually exclude the time clock, labor cost tools or a second location, so check those limits first.

How much does a small business scheduling app cost?

For a 15-person team at one location, expect $0 to $75 a month at list prices. Homebase Essentials is $30 per location, Connecteam Basic is $29 for the first 30 users on annual billing, When I Work is $2.50 per user, and Deputy Lite is $5 per user with a $30 monthly minimum. Add-ons such as payroll cost extra.

Can I use Excel or Google Sheets instead of an app?

Yes, for a very small, stable team. A spreadsheet works until people start swapping shifts, asking for time off by text or missing schedule changes. Apps add phone notifications, swap approvals, availability and overtime warnings. Many owners start with a free template and move to a free app plan once the team passes about 8 to 10 people.

Do small business scheduling apps include payroll?

Some offer it as an add-on. Homebase payroll is $49 a month plus $6 per employee, Deputy offers payroll by Paycor on annual Core and Pro plans, and Square Shifts feeds Square Payroll. Others, such as When I Work and Sling, export hours to payroll providers. Workzenly includes payroll in the same system as scheduling and the time clock.
