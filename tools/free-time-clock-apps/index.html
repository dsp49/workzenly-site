A free time clock app lets employees clock in and out from a phone, tablet or computer and gives you timesheets without paying a monthly fee. The catch is in the limits. Most free plans cap the number of users or locations, hold back GPS and geofencing, or keep kiosk mode and payroll exports for paid tiers. This list is for US owners and managers of small hourly teams (a café, a cleaning crew, a five-person construction outfit, a retail shop) who want to know which free plan fits before signing up.

Short answer: Connecteam is the strongest free plan for teams of up to 10 people, Jibble is free for unlimited users, Homebase suits one location with a shared tablet, and BusyBusy is the free pick for construction crews who need GPS. Below you will find a comparison table, a card for each app with its real limits, and a checklist for keeping free time records that stand up under federal and state wage law.

Connecteam is a mobile app for frontline and deskless teams that bundles a time clock with scheduling, checklists, chat and HR tools. Its Small Business plan is free for up to 10 users, and Connecteam says the free plan includes all hubs and features. That makes it the most complete free employee time clock app we found for a team that will stay small.

Employees clock in from their phones with a GPS stamp on each punch, or from a shared kiosk station. Managers get automatic timesheets that can be exported for payroll. Watch the user count: Connecteam counts archived users as well as active ones, so seasonal staff you have let go still use up seats. Once you pass 10, the Basic plan is $29 a month for the first 30 users on annual billing.

Strengths

  • Free plan includes scheduling, forms and chat as well as the time clock
  • GPS stamp on clock-in and clock-out
  • Shared kiosk mode for one tablet at the door
  • Clear step up: $29/mo covers up to 30 users

Drawbacks

  • Free plan stops at 10 users, and archived users count toward the cap
  • Geofence sites are limited on lower paid tiers (Advanced allows 10)

Jibble is a time and attendance app whose free plan has no user limit. The trade-off is transparency: Jibble’s pricing page shows only the free plan, so check with Jibble which extras (more geofences, live location, advanced permissions) need a paid upgrade.

How we chose these tools

We started with the products that appear for “free time clock app” searches in the US, then kept only those with a free plan you can use indefinitely, not just a trial. We checked each one on its own pricing and help pages on November 7, 2026.

Our criteria

  • A real free plan: no time limit, and enough users for a small team.
  • Clock-in that fits hourly work: phone punches, a shared kiosk or tablet, and break tracking.
  • Location checks: whether GPS or geofencing is included for free or held back.
  • Exportable records: timesheets you can send to payroll and keep for audits.
  • A fair upgrade path: what the first paid tier costs and what it adds.

We left out apps with no free time clock. Sling’s free plan, for example, covers scheduling but not the time clock, and OnTheClock offers a 30-day trial instead of a free tier. Both are worth a look if you are happy to pay; OnTheClock appears in our list of the best employee time clock apps.

How to choose a free employee time clock app

A free plan is only a good deal if the records it keeps are complete. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, you must pay non-exempt employees time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek, and you must keep accurate records of hours worked. Use this checklist before you invite your team.

  1. Count every user the vendor counts. Some caps include managers, admins or archived employees. If you hire seasonally, a 10-user cap can fill up in one summer.
  2. Check how people will clock in. Phones suit field crews; a shared tablet with a PIN or photo suits a single store. Make sure the free plan supports the method you need.
  3. Decide whether you need location proof. A GPS stamp shows where someone punched. A geofence blocks or flags punches outside a set radius. Our guide to what a geofence is explains the difference, and how a GPS time clock app works covers privacy rules.
  4. Track meal and rest breaks. Federal law does not require breaks, but many states do. California requires a 30-minute meal period for shifts over five hours, so your app should record breaks, not just in and out times.
  5. Look at overtime handling. At minimum, the app should total weekly hours so you can spot anything over 40. If you have staff in California, you also need daily overtime after 8 hours; see our California overtime law guide. You can check any week by hand with our time card calculator.
  6. Plan for record retention. The FLSA requires employers to keep payroll records for at least three years and time cards for at least two. Confirm that you can export timesheets and that history is not deleted when you change plans.
  7. Price the first paid step. Work out what you will pay at 11, 25 and 50 employees. A free plan that jumps to a high per-user fee can cost more within a year than a cheap paid plan.

If you are not ready for an app at all, a printable free timesheet template works for a handful of employees, as long as everyone records actual start, break and end times.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a completely free time clock app?

Yes. Jibble and BusyBusy offer free plans with unlimited users, and Connecteam is free for up to 10 users. Homebase is free for one location with up to 10 employees. Each one holds some features back, such as geofencing, kiosk mode, scheduling or payroll, so check the limits against what your team needs.

What is the best free time clock app for small business?

For a team of up to 10 people, Connecteam’s free plan is the most complete because it includes scheduling, GPS-stamped punches and a kiosk mode. For larger teams that need to stay at $0, Jibble has no user cap. For a single shop that clocks in at one tablet, Homebase’s free Basic plan is a good fit.

Which free clock in clock out app has GPS?

Connecteam’s free plan stamps GPS on each punch, Jibble’s help center lists GPS clock-ins on its free plan, and BusyBusy includes GPS time tracking for free. TimeCamp also lists GPS location tracking on its free tier. Geofencing that blocks off-site punches is often a paid feature, so confirm it before you rely on it.

Can a free time clock app keep records that meet FLSA rules?

It can, if it records actual hours worked and lets you export and keep them. The FLSA requires accurate records of hours, payroll records kept for three years and time cards for two. Make sure the free plan keeps full history, records breaks, and lets you download timesheets before you depend on it.

What happens when my team outgrows the free plan?

Usually the app asks you to upgrade before you can add more users or locations, and some features switch off. Connecteam moves you to Basic at $29 a month for up to 30 users, and Homebase charges per location. Export your timesheets regularly so your records are safe whatever you decide.
