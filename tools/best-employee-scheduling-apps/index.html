When I Work is a mobile-first scheduling app for hourly and shift-based teams, from coffee shops and retail stores to call centers and healthcare. It is the safest default for a team of 10 to 200 that wants the schedule built for them: even the cheapest Essentials plan includes auto-scheduling, multi-location schedules, forecast tools and OpenShifts, and you pay only for the users you have.

The auto-scheduler fills shifts in one click from each person’s eligibility, availability and preferences. OpenShifts post uncovered shifts to everyone qualified, swap requests go to a manager for approval, and team messaging keeps shift talk out of personal phone numbers. Attendance data syncs to payroll tools such as Gusto, QuickBooks Online, Paychex and Rippling.

Strengths

  • Auto-scheduling, OpenShifts and swaps on the lowest plan

Drawbacks

  • No free plan, only a 14-day trial
  • Time Tracking & Attendance is a separate add-on
  • Scheduling rules and role permissions need the $5 Pro plan

Homebase is scheduling, time tracking and team messaging for small local businesses such as cafes, salons, shops and clinics. Its Basic plan is free for one location and up to 10 employees and includes a weekly schedule builder and basic clock-in on a tablet, computer or POS. When you grow, you pay per location rather than per employee, which is unusual among scheduling apps.

Homebase prices by location, not by head, so paid plans cover unlimited employees. The schedule builder warns about overtime and conflicts before you publish, and it can show labor cost against a sales forecast pulled from Square, Clover, Shopify or Toast. Employees trade, cover and pick up open shifts from the app.

Strengths

  • Free plan for one location with up to 10 employees
  • Flat per-location price with unlimited employees on paid plans
  • POS sales sync from Square, Clover, Shopify and Toast
  • Payroll add-on in the same app ($49/month plus $6 per employee)

Drawbacks

  • The AI scheduling assistant needs the $70 Plus plan
  • Free plan stops at one location and 10 employees
  • Payroll costs extra on every plan

How we chose these scheduling apps

Our criteria

  • Built for shift work. Each app publishes schedules to employees’ phones and supports swaps or open shifts. General calendar and appointment-booking tools were left out.
  • Available to US businesses today, with a US website, US pricing and an app for iOS and Android or a web app.
  • Verified on the vendor’s own site on November 7, 2026: features, pricing page, free plan or trial and platforms. We did not copy review-site ratings.
  • Different buyers. We kept tools that win for a specific buyer (free, restaurants, compliance, field crews) instead of nine near-identical options.
  • Transparent cost. Tools that publish prices ranked ahead of similar tools that do not.

How to choose employee scheduling software

Start with your headcount, number of locations and the one problem that costs you the most today: no-shows, overtime, last-minute swaps or payroll errors. Then check each shortlisted app against this list.

  1. Price model. Per-user pricing (When I Work, Deputy, Sling) is cheapest for small teams. Per-location pricing (Homebase, 7shifts) wins when one site has many part-timers. Flat-fee bundles (Connecteam, Camelo) are predictable up to 30 users. Model your real headcount, including seasonal staff.
  2. Free plan limits. Free plans cap users (5 to 30), locations (usually one) or features (often no time clock). Check which limit you will hit first.
  3. Overtime warnings. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, and California adds daily overtime after 8 hours. An app that warns you before you publish a 44-hour week saves real money. Our guide to how to calculate overtime shows the math.
  4. Predictive scheduling laws. Some employers must post schedules in advance and pay a premium for late changes. Oregon requires 14 days’ notice for large retail, hospitality and food service employers, Chicago requires 14 days in covered industries, and New York City requires 14 days for fast food and 72 hours for retail. If that applies to you, pick an app with fair workweek tools.
  5. Breaks and minors. Federal law does not require meal breaks, but short rest breaks of 20 minutes or less must be paid, and states such as California require meal and rest periods. Federal rules also limit 14- and 15-year-olds to 3 hours on a school day and 18 hours in a school week. Apps that flag these save you from fixing them later.
  6. Time clock and payroll. A schedule is only a plan. Check whether clock-ins compare against it, whether a GPS time clock is included or an add-on, and how hours reach payroll.
  7. Employee experience. Ask two employees to install the app during the trial, trade a shift and request time off. If they can’t do it unaided, adoption will stall.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee scheduling app?

For most US hourly teams, When I Work is the best all-round employee scheduling app because auto-scheduling, OpenShifts and swaps are in its lowest plan at $2.50 per user per month. Homebase is better if you have one location and want a free plan, Deputy if you face fair workweek rules, and 7shifts if you run a restaurant.

Is there a free employee scheduling app?

Yes. Sling is free for up to 30 users, ZoomShift for up to 20 users at one location, Homebase for one location with up to 10 employees, Connecteam and Camelo for up to 10 users, and 7shifts for one restaurant with up to 15 employees. Free plans usually leave out the time clock, labor cost tools or multiple locations.

How much does employee scheduling software cost?

Per-user apps run from about $1.70 to $9 per user per month. Per-location apps such as Homebase start at $30 per location per month, and 7shifts at $39.99 per location for teams up to 30. Connecteam and Camelo charge $29 a month for the first 30 users. Enterprise tools such as HotSchedules quote on request. Check each vendor’s pricing page for current offers.

Can a scheduling app prevent overtime?

Many can warn you. Homebase, Connecteam and ZoomShift Premium flag shifts that push an employee past 40 hours before you publish, and several also block early clock-ins. The app cannot stop you from approving extra hours, and you still owe overtime for every hour actually worked, scheduled or not.

Do employee scheduling apps handle predictive scheduling laws?

Some do. Deputy has a fair workweek feature, and 7shifts and HotSchedules flag late changes and premiums for restaurants in cities with these rules. Most small-business apps do not track predictability pay. If you are covered in Oregon, Chicago, New York City or another city with a law, confirm the specific rules each app supports.
