The best employee time clock app is the one your staff will actually use at the start of every shift and that gives you clean, payroll-ready hours at the end of the week. For most US small businesses that means phone clock-in with a GPS check, a shared kiosk for the front door, break tracking for state rules, and an export to the payroll you already run. This guide is for owners, managers and HR leads of hourly teams in restaurants, retail, healthcare, cleaning, construction and field services.

Our quick verdict: Connecteam is the best overall time clock app for small business teams that work on their phones, Homebase is best for a restaurant or store with one location, Buddy Punch has the strongest break-compliance tools, and QuickBooks Time is the natural pick if you already run QuickBooks. Connecteam is an employee app for frontline teams that combines the time clock with scheduling, task checklists, forms, chat and HR. It is free for up to 10 users, and the paid Basic plan costs $29 a month for the first 30 users on annual billing, which works out to under $1 per person for a team of 30.

Employees clock in on their phones with a GPS stamp, or at a shared kiosk station. Higher tiers add the controls bigger teams want: Advanced ($49 a month for 30 users) brings full time clock customization, auto clock-out and up to 10 geofence sites, and Expert adds unlimited geofences and auto-assigned shifts. Timesheets export to payroll, and the same app handles shift swaps and company updates, so staff do not juggle three tools.

Strengths

  • Flat price for the first 30 users keeps costs predictable
  • Free plan for up to 10 users
  • GPS punches plus a shared kiosk mode
  • Scheduling, checklists and chat in the same app

Drawbacks

  • Geofence sites are capped below the Expert plan
  • Many features means more setup for a simple punch clock

Homebase is built for local hourly businesses. Paid plans are priced per location rather than per user: Essentials is $30 a location per month and adds advanced time tracking, location-verified mobile clock-in and team communication.

Homebase’s pricing page lists alerts when employees approach or reach overtime under its labor cost tools, which helps a manager stop a 41st hour before it happens. Payroll is an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid. New accounts get a 14-day trial of the top plan with no card required.

Buddy Punch is time clock software for businesses whose hourly employees work in more than one place: job sites, patients’ homes, storefronts and plant floors. How we chose these tools

We started with the time clock apps US small businesses compare most often, then read each vendor’s pricing page, feature pages and help docs on November 7, 2026. We did not use review-site ratings to rank anything.

Our criteria

  • Clock-in options: phone, shared kiosk or tablet, and whether photo, PIN or face checks are available.
  • Location controls: GPS stamps, geofencing, and which plan includes them.
  • Compliance help: break tracking, overtime alerts and edit history.
  • Payroll path: built-in payroll or a direct export to common US payroll tools.
  • Transparent pricing: a published price we could check, and its unit (per user, per location or base fee).

Each card says who the app is best for, because the best employee time clock app for a five-person café is rarely the right one for a 60-person field crew. How to choose an employee time clock app

Start with how your people work, then check the rules your records must meet.

  1. Map where people clock in. One door with a tablet points to a kiosk with PIN or photo. Several job sites point to phone clock-in with GPS and a geofence. Many teams need both.
  2. Price it at your real headcount. Per-user, per-location and base-fee-plus-user pricing give very different totals. Work it out for today and for next year.
  3. Check overtime and the workweek. Under the FLSA, non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a fixed workweek. The app should total hours by your workweek and flag overtime early. Our guide on how to calculate overtime covers bonuses and shift differentials that change the rate.
  4. Handle state break and overtime rules. California requires meal periods and daily overtime after 8 hours, and other states set their own break rules. Pick an app that records breaks as punches and lets you set daily overtime where it applies.
  5. Keep an edit trail. Manager edits to time cards should be logged with who changed what. That history protects you in a wage dispute.
  6. Confirm the payroll handoff. Either built-in payroll or a direct export to the provider you use. Retyping hours is where most errors start.
  7. Test it with two employees for a week. Most apps here have a 14- to 31-day trial. Run one full pay period before you roll it out.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee time clock app for small business?

It depends on how your team works. Connecteam is a strong all-round choice for mobile teams and is free for up to 10 users. Homebase fits single-location restaurants and shops, Buddy Punch has the deepest break-compliance tools, and QuickBooks Time is the easiest fit if you already run QuickBooks for accounting or payroll.

Which time clock app has a kiosk for a shared tablet?

Connecteam, Homebase, Buddy Punch (Pro), QuickBooks Time, Deputy, Jibble and OnTheClock all offer a shared kiosk mode. Deputy’s kiosk uses facial recognition on its Core and Pro plans, and Buddy Punch can take a webcam photo with each punch, which helps stop one employee clocking in for another.

Do employee time clock apps track breaks?

Most do. Employees punch out and back in for a meal or rest break, and the timesheet shows the gap. Buddy Punch’s Pro plan and Deputy add break-compliance tools that flag missed breaks. That matters in states such as California, where a missed meal period can mean an extra hour of pay.

How much does an employee time clock app cost?

Published prices on vendor sites range from free plans (Connecteam, Homebase, Jibble) to about $2.50 to $12 per user a month for paid tiers. Some add a base fee, such as Buddy Punch at $19 a month and QuickBooks Time at $20. Homebase charges per location instead of per user.

Can employees clock in from their own phones?

Yes. Every app in this list has iOS and Android apps for clocking in from a personal phone. Many add a GPS stamp or a geofence so you can see that the punch happened on site. Some states have rules about requiring personal devices or tracking location, so tell staff what is collected and when.
