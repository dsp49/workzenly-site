Workzenly makes time clock, scheduling, PTO and payroll software, and it appears in this list as one option. We researched every product on its own website on November 7, 2026. No company paid to be included. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s site before you buy.

The best Homebase alternatives depend on why you are leaving. When I Work and Sling cost less for small teams spread over several locations, 7shifts goes deeper for restaurants, Connecteam suits crews at job sites, and Deputy adds labor law compliance for multi-site operators. This guide compares nine apps like Homebase, with prices checked on each vendor’s site.

Homebase is scheduling, time clock and team communication software for hourly small businesses. Its free Basic plan covers one location with up to 10 employees. Paid plans are priced per location with unlimited employees: Essentials is $24 per location per month on annual billing ($30 monthly), Plus is $56 and the top plan, which adds HR and compliance tools, is $96. Payroll is an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid, and Tip Manager, Task Manager and hiring tools cost extra.

Most people searching for Homebase competitors have hit one of three limits: an 11th employee or a second location ends the free plan, per-location fees add up across many small sites, or they need a feature such as geofenced job-site clock-ins or advanced compliance rules. When I Work is the usual first comparison for Homebase users. The key difference is billing: When I Work charges per user, from $2.50 per user per month on Essentials, while Homebase charges per location. If you run five small sites with six people each, per-user pricing can cost less than five per-location subscriptions. Multi-location scheduling is included on the entry plan.

Essentials includes auto scheduling, schedule templates, forecast tools, OpenShifts, shift swaps and in-app messaging. Pro adds scheduling rules, role permissions and labor sharing between locations. Time tracking and attendance is a separate add-on, and payroll runs through integrations rather than inside the app. There is a 14-day free trial and no free plan.

Strengths

  • Entry plan from $2.50 per user/month with unlimited users
  • Auto scheduling, schedule templates and multi-week, multi-location scheduling on Essentials
  • OpenShifts, shift swapping and in-app team messaging on every plan
  • 14-day free trial of everything, no credit card required

Drawbacks

  • No free plan
  • Time tracking and attendance is a separate add-on you switch on per plan
  • No built-in payroll; it connects to payroll providers instead

How to choose between Homebase alternatives

First, price your real setup. Count locations, employees (including managers) and the add-ons you use today, such as payroll and tip pooling. Homebase’s per-location pricing is hard to beat for one busy location with many part-timers; per-user apps win for many small locations; flat team pricing sits in between.

Then check each finalist against US labor rules during its free trial:

  • Overtime. The FLSA requires 1.5 times the regular rate for non-exempt hours over 40 in a workweek. Check for overtime warnings while scheduling, not only on the timesheet. Our guide on how to calculate overtime shows the math, including bonuses.
  • Daily overtime and breaks. California adds daily overtime after 8 hours, and several states require meal breaks. The app should let you set break rules per location.
  • Predictive scheduling. Oregon and cities such as Seattle, San Francisco, New York City, Chicago and Philadelphia require advance schedule notice for certain employers. Ask how each app records publish dates and late changes.
  • Clock-in control. Decide between phone clock-in with GPS or geofencing, and a shared tablet kiosk. Compare options in our list of free time clock apps.
  • Records. Keep payroll records for three years and time cards for two, as the FLSA requires. Export your Homebase timesheets before you cancel.
  • Payroll. If you use Homebase payroll, line up a new provider or an app with payroll included before your last pay run, so year-end tax forms stay with one provider where possible.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to Homebase?

Sling has the largest free team: its free plan covers scheduling, time-off requests and messaging for up to 30 users, though the time clock needs a paid plan. 7shifts’ free Comp plan covers one restaurant location with up to 15 employees. Connecteam is free for up to 10 users, with one time clock and one schedule.

Homebase vs When I Work: which costs less?

Homebase charges per location, from $24 per location per month on annual billing with unlimited employees, and has a free plan for 10 employees at one location. When I Work charges $2.50 per user per month and up. One large location usually favors Homebase; several small locations often favor When I Work.

Which Homebase competitor includes payroll?

Gusto runs payroll on every plan and adds scheduling on Plus. Deputy sells US payroll enabled by Paycor as an add-on on annual Core and Pro plans. 7shifts sells a US payroll add-on and includes it in Premium. Workzenly includes payroll with scheduling, the time clock and PTO tracking.

What happens when I outgrow Homebase’s free plan?

Homebase’s Basic plan covers one location with up to 10 employees. Once you add an 11th employee or a second location, Homebase asks you to choose one of its three paid plans, each priced per location per month, starting with Essentials. That is a good moment to compare per-user and flat-priced alternatives.

Can I move my employees from Homebase to another app?

Yes. Export your employee list and timesheets from Homebase, then import the staff list into the new app, which most scheduling tools accept as a spreadsheet. Rebuild one typical week as a schedule template, run both apps for a pay period to compare hours, and download payroll records before you cancel.
