If you are looking for free payroll software, here is the short version. As of November 7, 2026, Payroll4Free is the only US service we found that stays free with no end date, and only if you pay nine or fewer people no more than once a month and file the payroll taxes yourself. That last number matters more than the trial, because payroll is something you will run every week or two for years.

Payroll4Free is a small company headquartered in Albany, New York, and it is the only service we found that is genuinely free payroll software for small business with no end date. Its site says all basic services are free “as long as you are paying 9 or fewer individuals no more than once monthly.” Advertisers pay for small ads that you see inside the software when you log in to run payroll.

The free tier covers payroll processing, federal, state and local tax calculations, filled-out tax forms, vacation, sick and PTO accruals, reports, an employee portal for pay stubs and W-2s, and direct deposit through your own bank or printed checks. You can pay both W-2 employees and 1099 contractors. There are two optional paid services: a Payroll Tax Service that deposits and files your taxes for you, and a Direct Deposit Service that moves funds through Payroll4Free’s bank. Its FAQ says the software needs a small browser extension, and it supports paper checks for any worker at any time.

Strengths

  • No monthly fee for up to nine people paid monthly
  • W-2 employees and 1099 contractors on the same payroll
  • Live customer service included in the free tier
  • Print paper checks or pay by direct deposit through your own bank

Drawbacks

  • You deposit and file payroll taxes yourself unless you pay for the tax service
  • Free terms assume a monthly pay schedule
  • Ads appear inside the software, and it needs a browser extension

How to choose free payroll software for your small business

Start with one question: who files the taxes? Payroll is more than calculating paychecks. An employer must withhold federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, deposit those taxes on schedule, and file returns. That work is where most of the cost and most of the penalties sit, so a free tool that leaves it to you is only free if your time is.

What you take on if the software does not file for you

  • Federal deposits. Withheld income tax plus both halves of Social Security and Medicare are deposited through the Treasury’s free Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS) on a monthly or semiweekly schedule.
  • Federal returns. Form 941 every quarter, Form 940 for federal unemployment tax every year, and Forms W-2 and W-3 by January 31. Contractors paid $2,000 or more for services in 2026 get a 1099-NEC (the threshold was $600 for payments before 2026).
  • State and local. State income tax withholding (in states that have it), state unemployment insurance, local taxes in some cities and counties, and new-hire reports to your state.
  • Records. The Department of Labor requires payroll records to be kept for at least three years and time cards for two. The IRS says to keep employment tax records for at least four years.

If that list looks like a lot, a full-service plan from about $29 to $59 a month plus a per-person fee takes it off your desk, and the IRS penalty for a late federal tax deposit runs from 2% to 15% of the unpaid amount. Our guide on how to process payroll walks through each step if you decide to do it yourself.

A buyer’s checklist

  1. Read the free terms. Free months are promotions with conditions. Note the end date, what happens after, and whether you need a contract.
  2. Price the year, not the trial. For 8 employees, a $49 base plus $6 each is $97 a month, or $1,164 a year. Compare that across options.
  3. Check your states. Some plans include one state and charge for each extra one.
  4. Match your pay schedule. Payroll4Free’s free tier assumes monthly pay. Many states set minimum pay frequencies for hourly workers, so check your state labor department before switching to monthly pay.
  5. Get the hours right first. Non-exempt employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek under the FLSA, and some states add daily overtime. Our overtime calculator shows the math, and a time clock that feeds payroll removes the retyping.
  6. Classify workers correctly. Contractor-only plans are cheap, but paying an employee as a contractor creates back taxes. See 1099 vs. W-2 before you choose.
  7. Give employees pay stubs. Many states require an itemized wage statement each payday. Every option here offers digital pay stubs; our guide to reading a pay stub explains each line.

Frequently asked questions

Is there any truly free payroll software in the US?

Yes, but only one we could confirm. Payroll4Free says its basic service is free if you pay nine or fewer people no more than once a month. It calculates taxes and fills out forms, but you deposit and file taxes yourself unless you buy its paid tax service. Every other option we checked is free only for a trial or promotional period.

Is there a free payroll app for small business?

There is no full-service payroll app that stays free. Roll by ADP offers three free months, then charges $59 a month plus $7 per worker. Homebase offers a free scheduling and time clock app for one location of up to 10 employees, and was offering six free months of payroll in November 2026 as a limited-time promotion.

Which payroll software free trial lasts longest?

In November 2026, Homebase and ADP RUN advertised up to six free months of payroll, and Roll by ADP offered three. Wave and OnPay offer about one month, and Zoho Payroll offers 14 days. Promotions change often, so check the vendor’s terms page for eligibility and the date the offer ends.

Can I run payroll myself for free without software?

Yes. You can calculate withholding with IRS Publication 15-T, deposit taxes for free through EFTPS, and file Forms 941, 940 and W-2 yourself. You also handle state withholding, unemployment insurance and new-hire reporting. It costs nothing but time, and mistakes carry penalties, so most owners switch to paid software once they have several employees.

What is the cheapest payroll software after the free period?

Of the full-service options we checked, Zoho Payroll lists the lowest price: $29 a month plus $5 per employee for one state when billed yearly, or $39 plus $6 when billed monthly. Square Payroll’s full-service plan is $35 a month plus $6 per person. If you pay only contractors, Square’s contractor plan is $6 per person paid with no base fee.
