If you are weighing Connecteam alternatives, here is what you are comparing against. Connecteam is an employee app for deskless teams that combines a GPS time clock, shift scheduling, checklists and forms, chat and HR tools. It is free for up to 10 users (archived users count), and paid plans start at $29 a month for the first 30 users on annual billing, with geofence sites limited on lower tiers. It is a strong fit for many small frontline teams.

Businesses look at other options when they want payroll inside the same product, scheduling built for restaurants or retail, pricing per location instead of per team, deeper construction job costing, or a lighter tool that only does the time clock. Homebase is the closest match for local businesses, When I Work and Sling are cheaper scheduling-first apps, Deputy suits multi-location shift work, and Jibble stays free for any team size.

Homebase is the alternative most like Connecteam for restaurants, cafés, shops and other local businesses. It covers scheduling, a time clock, team messaging and HR, and it offers built-in payroll as an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid ($29 a month for teams of up to 5).

The big difference is pricing. Homebase charges per location, not per user: the free Basic plan covers one location and up to 10 employees with a tablet, computer or POS time clock; Essentials is $30 a location per month and adds advanced scheduling, location-verified mobile clock-in and team communication. For a single store with 25 part-timers, per-location pricing can cost less than per-user plans.

Strengths

  • Per-location pricing suits teams with many part-timers
  • Built-in payroll add-on
  • Free plan for one location
  • POS integration

Drawbacks

  • Free plan caps at 10 employees and one location
  • GPS mobile clock-in starts on a paid plan
  • Costs rise with each location

When I Work is scheduling software for hourly workers with a time clock and team messaging. Essentials costs $2.50 per user a month, Pro $5 and Premium $8 for scheduling, and a Time Tracking & Attendance switch on each plan raises the price. Staff swap and pick up shifts on their phones, and managers see punches next to scheduled times.

Compared with Connecteam, When I Work is more focused: it does not try to cover forms, checklists or a company knowledge base. If scheduling is your main pain and you already have payroll, that focus is a plus. Essentials includes payroll integrations. There is no free plan, but the 14-day trial needs no card.

How we chose these tools

We looked at the Connecteam competitors that US frontline businesses compare, then checked each vendor’s pricing and feature pages on November 7, 2026. We did not list Connecteam itself, and we chose Connecteam alternatives that each beat it on at least one point a buyer cares about.

Our criteria

  • Time clock quality: phone and kiosk clock-in, GPS and geofencing, break tracking.
  • Scheduling: shift swaps, open shifts and labor cost tools.
  • Payroll path: built-in payroll or a direct sync with US payroll providers.
  • Pricing model: per user, per location, or base fee plus per user, and the free plan if any.
  • Fit by industry: restaurants and retail, healthcare, construction and field service.

How to choose apps like Connecteam

Write down the one or two things Connecteam does not do well for you. That shortlist is more useful than any feature grid when you compare Connecteam alternatives.

  1. Payroll inside or outside? Homebase, OnTheClock and Workzenly offer payroll in the product. When I Work, Deputy, Sling, Buddy Punch and ClockShark export hours to a payroll provider. QuickBooks Time syncs with QuickBooks payroll.
  2. Price it your way. Connecteam’s flat price for 30 users is hard to beat for teams of 20 to 30. Per-location pricing (Homebase) wins for one big store; per-user pricing (Sling, When I Work) wins for small teams.
  3. Check predictive scheduling rules. Cities including New York City, San Francisco, Seattle, Chicago and Philadelphia, and the state of Oregon, have fair workweek laws that require advance schedule notice for some industries. If you operate there, look for schedule-change logs and notice tools.
  4. Check break and overtime handling. Hours over 40 in a workweek need time and a half under the FLSA, and some states add daily overtime and required meal breaks. See our California overtime law guide for the strictest example.
  5. Keep your history. Before you leave Connecteam, export timesheets. The FLSA requires employers to keep time cards for two years and payroll records for three.
  6. Run a parallel pay period. Keep both apps for one pay period and compare totals before you switch everyone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Connecteam?

For local businesses, Homebase is the closest alternative, with scheduling, a time clock and a payroll add-on priced per location. When I Work and Sling are cheaper scheduling-first apps, Deputy suits multi-location shift work, and Jibble is free for unlimited users if you only need time tracking. The best fit depends on what Connecteam lacks for you.

Is there a free alternative to Connecteam?

Yes. Jibble is free for unlimited users for time tracking, and Homebase is free for one location with up to 10 employees. Sling’s free plan covers scheduling for up to 30 users but not the time clock. Connecteam itself is free for up to 10 users, including archived users.

Connecteam vs Homebase: which is better?

Connecteam charges a flat price for the first 30 users and includes forms, checklists and chat. Homebase charges per location and offers built-in payroll as an add-on. A single location with many part-timers often pays less with Homebase, while a team spread across sites may get more from Connecteam.

Which Connecteam competitors include payroll?

Homebase offers payroll as an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid. QuickBooks Time syncs with QuickBooks payroll and is also sold in Workforce bundles with payroll. OnTheClock offers built-in payroll, and Workzenly includes payroll with pay stubs. Others in this list export hours to a payroll provider.

Why do businesses switch from Connecteam?

Common reasons include wanting payroll in the same product, needing more geofence sites than their plan includes, passing 10 users on the free plan, or preferring a simpler time clock without the forms and chat. Others want scheduling built for restaurants or construction job costing.
