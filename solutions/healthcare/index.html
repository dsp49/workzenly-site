Overtime

The 40-hour week, or 8/80 if you are a hospital

Weekly hours per person add up while you build, and during the week the punches add up against them. A clinic pays overtime after 40 hours in the workweek. Hospitals and residential care facilities may instead use the federal 8/80 system, by prior agreement with employees: overtime after 8 hours in a day and 80 in a fixed 14-day period. Which costs less depends on shift length.

The practice manager’s guide to clinic staff scheduling software

A clinic runs on coverage: a provider in every room with patients booked, an MA for each provider, someone on triage, someone at the desk. Those people work 8-, 10- or 12-hour shifts, hold licenses and cards that expire, and are paid by the hour. This guide covers what clinic staff scheduling software has to handle, the rules a healthcare schedule sets off, credential tracking, and how to choose.

One distinction first. Patient appointments live in your practice management or EHR software and carry patient health information. The staff schedule carries names, roles, shifts, punches, hours, PTO and credentials, nothing about a patient, so Workzenly never needs a chart. Our healthcare scheduling software guide draws the same line for hospitals and home care.

What clinic staff scheduling software needs to do

Five jobs. Schedule by room and role, so you see that rooms 4–6 have a provider and an MA from 8:30 to 7, not just that six people work Thursday. Keep each person’s credentials beside their name. Handle PTO and swaps with the coverage in view, so a day off never leaves triage empty. Record hours from a kiosk at the staff entrance or a phone, with meal breaks and overtime worked out. Then pay the hours that were approved. Scheduling, HR documents, PTO, the time clock kiosk and payroll in Workzenly are those five jobs on one record.

Tools built only for scheduling, including several of the best employee scheduling apps, stop at the published grid; hours are retyped into payroll and credentials stay in a binder.

8-, 10- and 12-hour shifts, and rotations

A clinic open 7 to 7 covers the day with two overlapping 10-hour shifts, 7–5:30 and 8:30–7, each with a 30-minute unpaid meal break. Four 10s make a 40-hour week with a weekday off, which is why MAs and nurses like them; front desk staff usually stay on 8s. Urgent care runs 12-hour shifts, often three a week per nurse, or on a fixed rotation such as the Pitman schedule (two on, two off, three on, over two weeks) or the DuPont schedule (day and night 12s over four weeks). Both average 42 hours a week, so some weeks run to 48 and carry 8 overtime hours. Whatever the pattern, set the shift once with its meal break, roll it forward, and watch weekly hours per person while you build. Our shift schedule templates include 10-hour and 12-hour layouts.

Overtime: the 40-hour week and the 8/80 option

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt staff earn time and a half for hours over 40 in a fixed seven-day workweek, and evening or Saturday differentials go into the regular rate first; see our shift differential guide and how to calculate overtime. The Department of Labor’s Fact Sheet #21 says payroll records must be kept for at least three years, and time cards and work schedules for two.

Hospitals and residential care establishments have one extra option. Under 29 CFR 778.601, described in the Department of Labor’s Fact Sheet #54, they may use a fixed, regularly recurring 14-day work period instead of the 7-day workweek, by prior agreement or understanding with the affected employees before the work is performed. Overtime is then owed for hours over 8 in any workday and over 80 in the period, and one employee cannot be under both systems. A clinic is neither, so it stays on the 40-hour week. State limits on mandatory overtime for nurses

Federal law does not cap an adult’s hours. Several states restrict mandatory overtime for nurses or other direct-care staff, mostly at hospitals and 24-hour facilities. Three examples, as each state describes its own law:

  • New York. Labor Law Section 167 bars health care employers under Article 28 of the Public Health Law from requiring RNs and LPNs to work beyond their regularly scheduled hours, except in a health care disaster, a declared emergency, an unforeseen emergency where overtime is needed for safe patient care, or an ongoing procedure. The state Department of Labor says the employer must first make a good-faith effort to fill the hours voluntarily and keep a nurse coverage plan under 12 NYCRR Part 177; on-call time cannot substitute for mandatory overtime.
  • Washington. RCW 49.28.140 says no employee of a health care facility may be required to work overtime, except for an unforeseeable emergent circumstance, prescheduled on-call time (not in place of regular shifts), documented reasonable efforts to obtain staffing, or finishing a patient care procedure in progress. Washington L&I says facilities that do not operate 24 hours a day are not covered.
  • Pennsylvania. Act 102 bars a health care facility from requiring hourly or nonsupervisory employees in direct patient care or clinical care services to work beyond their agreed, predetermined and regularly scheduled shifts. Employees may still agree to a regularly scheduled shift longer than 8 hours or volunteer for overtime; physicians, physician assistants and dentists are not covered.

For software, the point is the record: an extra shift was offered, this person accepted it, at this time, so voluntary overtime can be shown to be voluntary. Workzenly keeps that record for every open shift and swap. Whether your clinic is covered is a question for your state labor department.

Breaks on 10-hour and 12-hour shifts

Federal law requires no meal or rest breaks for adults. The Department of Labor’s Fact Sheet #22 says short rest periods, usually 20 minutes or less, count as hours worked and must be paid, while a bona fide meal period, typically 30 minutes or more, need not be paid if the employee is completely relieved from duty. An MA who eats at the nurses’ station while answering the phone has not been relieved.

States add their own rules, and long shifts make them bite. California’s Division of Labor Standards Enforcement says a 30-minute meal period is required for a work period of more than five hours and a second one past ten hours; the second may be waived by mutual consent only if the day is no more than 12 hours and the first was not waived. Each missed meal period costs one extra hour of pay at the regular rate, and a paid 10-minute rest break is due for every four hours or major fraction. Washington L&I requires a 30-minute meal period on shifts over five hours and a paid 10-minute rest break per four hours worked. So a 12-hour shift in either state carries two meal periods and three rest breaks. Set your break rule during setup and check your state.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Wage, break and overtime rules change every year, so check your state labor department, your licensing board or a healthcare employment lawyer for your situation.

Credential tracking as stored data

A clinic’s credential list is short but unforgiving: provider, RN and APRN licenses, MA certificates where your state or payers require them, BLS or CPR cards for clinical roles, and whatever training your policies add. Track them as data stored with each person: the document photographed, the dates typed from the card, and one list across the clinic sorted by expiry. That is what Workzenly’s HR documents feature does, and the expiry date then shows beside the name on the schedule and in the employee’s own app.

Two honest limits. Workzenly stores and shows the dates; it does not send automatic expiry alerts, so give one person the job of opening the list each month and chasing renewals due in the next ninety days. And storing a card is not primary-source verification with a state board, which is a separate process. A small practice needs the simpler version done well: the right documents on file, visible to the people who schedule.

How to choose

  1. Run last week through it. Take your real schedule, one swap, one PTO request and one missed clock-out, and see which tool turns them into a correct pay run with the fewest manual steps. Our payroll process guide lists every step.
  2. Look for rooms and roles, not just people. Can you see that triage is covered from 7 to 5:30, or only that Ruth works Thursday?
  3. Ask where credentials live. With the person, with dates, visible to whoever schedules? Or in an HR folder the scheduler never opens?
  4. Check the staff entrance. Phones stay in lockers in many clinical areas, so a tablet kiosk with PINs is not optional. Ask how breaks and missed punches work on it; our time and attendance page covers both.
  5. Count the hand-offs. Every time hours are retyped between tools, someone fixes a pay stub later.
  6. Keep patient data out of it. If a tool asks for an EHR connection to schedule staff, ask why.

FAQ

Questions from practice managers about clinic staff scheduling software

Is this patient appointment scheduling?

No. Workzenly is clinic staff scheduling software: which providers, nurses, MAs and front desk staff work which shifts, the hours they clock, and the payroll those hours feed. Patient appointments stay in your practice management or EHR system, and the staff schedule holds no patient information.

Can it check certifications before someone is scheduled?

Each license and card is stored with the person, with the dates from the document, and all of them appear in one searchable list. The expiry date sits beside the name on the schedule, so whoever builds the week sees it before assigning a shift. Workzenly does not send automatic expiry alerts; checking the list is a task someone owns.

Does it handle 10-hour and 12-hour shifts and rotations?

Yes. Shifts can be any length, with the unpaid meal break set once per shift type. Weekly hours per person show while you build, so a Pitman or DuPont rotation, which averages 42 hours and has 48-hour weeks in it, shows its overtime weeks before you publish.

Does Workzenly support the 8/80 overtime rule?

The 8/80 system is a federal option only for hospitals and residential care establishments, by prior agreement with employees, so a clinic or medical practice stays on the 40-hour workweek. If you run a hospital or residential care facility on 8/80, tell us on the demo call and we will set the rule up with you.

Does the schedule contain patient information?

No. The schedule and timesheets hold staff names, roles, shifts, punches, hours, PTO and credentials, and no patient health information. Keep patient data in your EHR and practice management system; the staff schedule needs none of it.

How do clinic staff clock in?

A shared tablet at the staff entrance works as a time clock kiosk, with a PIN for each person. Staff who move between sites, such as a float MA, clock in from the Workzenly app on their phone, with GPS and geofencing confirming they are at the clinic. Both land in the same timesheet.
