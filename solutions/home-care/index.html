The agency owner’s guide to home care workforce management software

A home care agency runs on visits, not shifts. The hours between clients are work, overtime arrives in two-hour pieces, and if Medicaid pays, the state has rules about proving a visit happened. Here is what home care workforce management software has to handle, and how to choose it.

What home care workforce management software needs to do

Five jobs. Schedule visits by client and publish them where caregivers see them. Record the start and end of each visit at the client’s address from the caregiver’s phone, with the location captured at the punch. Put travel between clients on the timesheet. Handle time off and call-outs without a coordinator on the phone all evening. Pay for exactly the hours approved. Scheduling, GPS time tracking, timesheets, PTO and payroll in Workzenly are those five jobs on one record. Tools built only for mobile clock-ins, such as the ones in our list of the best GPS time clock apps, stop at the punch.

The geofence makes a phone punch trustworthy. GPS.gov says GPS-enabled smartphones are typically accurate to within about 16 feet under open sky, worse near buildings and trees, and Android’s developer documentation recommends a minimum geofence radius of 100 to 150 meters (330 to 490 feet), so give an apartment building more room than a house. Our explainer on what a geofence is and the GPS time clock app guide cover the radius and the written policy.

Hours worked rules: travel time, waiting time and sleep time

Travel between clients is hours worked. The Department of Labor’s Fact Sheet #79D says travel from one client to another during the workday is compensable, and the agency must make sure it is paid; ordinary travel from home to the first client and back from the last is not. The drive time counts toward the 40 hours for overtime. In Workzenly the gap between a clock-out at one door and the clock-in at the next shows on the timesheet as travel, and a coordinator corrects a long gap, such as an unpaid lunch, with a note.

A caregiver who must stay in the home ready to help is on duty and paid. On a shift under 24 hours, all the time is hours worked even if the caregiver may sleep. On a shift of 24 hours or more, employer and employee can agree to exclude a regularly scheduled sleep period of up to 8 hours if adequate sleeping facilities are provided and sleep is usually uninterrupted; interruptions are paid, and under 5 hours of sleep the whole night is paid. Our guide to time and attendance tracking covers the records behind this.

Live-in and overnight shifts

Under Fact Sheet #79B, a live-in caregiver lives on the client’s premises permanently or for extended periods: seven days a week, five days a week (120 hours or more), or five consecutive days or nights. Meal periods and sleep time can be excluded by a written agreement the employer keeps, and all hours worked must still be recorded. Since January 1, 2015, agencies may not claim the live-in overtime exemption, so an agency’s live-in caregiver earns overtime after 40 hours under federal law; New York draws its line for residential employees at 44 hours, according to the state labor department. In Workzenly a 24-hour shift is one long visit, interruptions are punch fixes with a note, and the signed sleep agreement sits in the caregiver’s record with the HR documents.

Minimum wage and overtime for home care agencies since 2015

Since a 2013 Department of Labor rule took effect on January 1, 2015, third-party employers such as home care agencies could not claim the FLSA companionship services or live-in exemptions, so their aides were owed the $7.25 federal minimum wage and overtime after 40 hours. In July 2025 the Department proposed returning to the earlier 1975 rules (90 Fed. Reg. 28976), and its Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-4 told investigators to stop enforcing the 2013 rule against agencies while that proposal is pending. As of this update the 2013 text is still in the regulation at 29 CFR 552.109, and the bulletin says it does not change any legal requirement. Many states require overtime for home care workers on their own. Talk to an employment attorney before changing how you pay overtime.

The arithmetic of short visits does not change. Yvette Mwangi earns $17.00 an hour and works 19 visits: 38.0 hours of visit time plus 4.5 hours of driving is 42.5 hours. Her pay is 40 × $17.00 = $680.00 plus 2.5 × $25.50 = $63.75, so $743.75; leave the travel off and she is $97.75 short. See how to calculate overtime for the regular-rate math; PTO is paid but is not hours worked. Keep payroll records at least 3 years and time cards at least 2, and for a paper trail during a trial week start from our timesheet templates.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Home care wage rules are changing federally and differ by state; check your state labor department, your state Medicaid agency and an employment lawyer.

EVV and your time record: two different jobs

Under Section 12006 of the 21st Century Cures Act, states must require electronic visit verification for Medicaid-funded personal care services (by January 1, 2020) and home health services (by January 1, 2023) that involve an in-home visit, according to medicaid.gov. EVV records the service, the client, the caregiver, the date, the location and the start and end times, and most states run or approve specific EVV systems. Workzenly is not an EVV system and does not connect to one. Its punch records are the agency’s time record for paying caregivers under the FLSA; EVV is handled by your state’s system, and agencies that bill Medicaid run both. Ask us on the demo call how agencies in your state do it.

How to choose

  1. Run last week through it. A real caregiver’s week with the drives, one call-out and one forgotten clock-out. See which tool makes a correct pay run with the fewest manual steps.
  2. Check the geofence per address. Its own radius per client, outside punches flagged rather than blocked, nothing recorded between punches.
  3. Make travel visible. If between-client time is not its own line, somebody is estimating it or nobody is paying it.
  4. Settle EVV first. If you bill Medicaid, ask every vendor whether they are an EVV system, work beside one, or neither.
  5. Count the hand-offs. Every retype between tools is a pay stub fixed later; our payroll process guide lists the steps.

FAQ

Questions from home care agency owners

Something else on your mind? Write to info@workzenly.com.

Does Workzenly meet my state's EVV requirement?

No, and we do not claim it does. Under the 21st Century Cures Act, agencies paid by Medicaid for personal care or home health visits have electronic visit verification duties that run through their state's EVV system. Workzenly's punch records are your time record for paying caregivers under the FLSA. Ask us on the demo call how agencies run both.

How does a caregiver clock in at a client's home?

Each client address gets its own geofence, a circle around the home with a radius you set. On arrival the app shows the visit and whether the caregiver is inside the fence, and they tap clock in. The time and location are saved with the punch. A punch outside the fence is recorded and flagged for the coordinator, never thrown away.

Is travel time between clients paid?

Yes. The Department of Labor's Fact Sheet #79D says travel from one client to another during the workday is hours worked, and the agency must make sure it is paid. The commute from the caregiver's own home to the first client and back from the last is ordinary commuting and generally not paid. Workzenly puts the time between one clock-out and the next clock-in on the timesheet as travel, counted toward the 40-hour week.

Does the app track caregivers between visits?

No. Location is captured only at the moment of a clock-in or clock-out, with whether that spot was inside the client's geofence. Nothing is recorded while a caregiver drives between visits or after they clock out. Your written policy should tell caregivers what is recorded and when.

Can caregivers see each other's schedules or pay?

Each caregiver sees their own visits, hours, PTO balance and pay stubs, plus the open visits the coordinator has posted, and never a coworker's pay or record. Coordinators see the schedule, attendance and time off for the caregivers they manage. Pay rates and pay stubs are visible only to people with payroll access.

How long does it take to set up a home care agency?

Most agencies are scheduling visits and clocking in within a week.
